Can AI Agents Reliably Optimize Computational Chemistry Codes?

Mathew Chow, Tao E. Li

arXiv:2611.10378·physics.chem-ph·Published 2026-11-07

Artificial intelligence (AI) approaches have opened new possibilities in computational and theoretical chemistry. AI agents can now execute computational workflows autonomously, and their use in scientific software development represents another promising direction. Herein, we present a general strategy for the controlled optimization of scientific software using a dual-agent harness. By using separate worker and controller agents for optimization and evaluation, respectively, we find that this approach can reduce computational costs while preserving numerical accuracy for the computational chemistry methods examined. We apply this strategy to TIP4P water simulations in LAMMPS, as well as to Coulomb and exchange matrix construction and linear-response time-dependent density functional theory (TDDFT) calculations in PySCF. Among these applications, the largest speedups are obtained for TDDFT. We then extend this strategy from optimizing established packages to developing efficient parallel CPU- and GPU-accelerated implementations of an initially serial molecular dynamics code, resulting in speedups of two to three orders of magnitude. Finally, we apply the protocol to open-ended algorithm development by constructing an adaptive self-consistent field approach that coordinates the use of multiple solvers. We find that the resulting approach can converge some challenging systems and occasionally reach lower-energy solutions than those obtained with the standalone solvers tested. These applications demonstrate that a strategy developed for improving code efficiency may also support the systematic development of computational methods. Overall, our benchmark study provides an example of AI-assisted advances in computational chemistry.
