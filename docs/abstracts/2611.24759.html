Characterizing adsorption thermodynamics in carbon capture materials: an efficient first-principles approach

Anna T. Bui, Grant M. Rotskoff, Thomas E. Markland

arXiv:2611.24759·cond-mat.mtrl-sci·Published 2026-11-18

Porous materials offer a vast design space for CO$_2$ capture, whose exploration hinges on efficient and accurate first-principles predictions of the thermodynamics governing adsorption. However, simulating isotherms and heats of adsorption using grand canonical molecular simulations is extremely computationally expensive when combined with accurate ab initio treatments of the molecular interactions, limiting screening to low-pressure uptake encoded in the Henry constant. Identifying and designing the best CO$_2$ capture materials requires a strategy that can efficiently calculate their adsorption properties using ab initio potential energy surfaces such as those now available from foundational machine-learned interatomic potentials (MLIPs). Here we develop a neural classical density functional (cDFT) framework for carbon capture processes that allows direct calculation of adsorption isotherms from low to high loading limits of metal-organic frameworks (MOFs) without grand canonical simulations. We demonstrate the success of our approach by calculating the ab initio adsorption isotherms for over 5,000 experimentally reported MOFs. From our screening, we show how these materials can be characterized using four isotherm shapes, arising from their pore size and binding sites, which the Henry constant cannot distinguish. We show that these shapes, rather than the binding strength alone, decide which MOFs suit which process: small pores with strong binding sites for vacuum and temperature swings at low pressure, large pores with weakly binding walls for pressure swings. Finally, we identify the most promising candidates by weighing their working capacity against the energy needed for regeneration.
