Optimizing Drilling Trajectories in Multi-Material Structures Using Quantum Annealers

Eneko Osaba, Antonio Rubio-Mateos

arXiv:2611.18121·cs.AI·Published 2026-11-13

In this work we present a quantum-hybrid optimization approach for planning drilling trajectories on multi-material structures, with the objective of minimizing total energy consumption. The proposed formulation models the drilling process as a constrained routing problem in which each hole must be visited while accounting for heterogeneous material properties, material-dependent drilling costs and displacement energy, as well as tool-wear penalties. Our method incorporates realistic industrial constraints, including open-path routing and mandatory grouping of holes belonging to the same material to avoid frequent tool-bit changes. The modeled problem is solved using D-Wave's Leap Constrained Quadratic Model Hybrid Solver. To evaluate performance, we benchmark instances ranging from 10 to 55 holes with varying material distributions and cost matrices. The results highlight the capability of quantum-hybrid optimization to handle complex multi-physics constraints and to generate energy-efficient drilling sequences under realistic manufacturing conditions.
