# Strain-induced non-trivial band topology and large magnetoresistance in epitaxial SrTaO$_3$ thin films

**Authors:** Aditya Mehta, Soumyakanta Panda, Pratik Sahu, Amit Chauhan, Dibyajyoti Sahoo, Shwetha G Bhat, Rohit Soni, Goutam Tripathy, Niharika Mohapatra, B. R. K. Nanda, D. Samal

Epitaxial thin films of complex transition metal oxides constitute an ideal platform for realizing correlated topological phases through strain engineering of crystal symmetry and band topology. Here, we investigate the electronic transport properties and the band structure of the 5$d^1$ paramagnetic metal SrTaO$_3$ thin film under epitaxial compressive strain. The film exhibits a large non-saturating quasi-quadratic magnetoresistance (MR) and a nonlinear Hall effect characteristic of two-band transport, together with the field induced re-entrant metallic behavior. The violation of Kohler's scaling further corroborates the scattering from multiple bands. Semiclassical two-band analysis yields carrier densities and mobilities of the order $\sim 10^{21}~\mathrm{cm^{-3}}$ and $\sim 10^{4}~\mathrm{cm^2 V^{-1} s^{-1}}$, respectively. The observed large mobility and unsaturated MR, indicates the presence of a non-trivial band structure. Consistent with this, results from density functional theory calculations and tight binding model studies reveal that the stable structure has an octahedral rotation $a^0a^0c^+$, where the non-symmorphic symmetry ensures the presence of topological nodal lines yielding finite Berry curvature in the vicinity of the Fermi level. Besides the nodal line, trivial electron and hole pockets exist near the Fermi level, contributing to electron transport. These findings establish that epitaxial strain drives SrTaO$_3$ into a nontrivial topological semimetal, a phase inaccessible in the bulk counterpart.
