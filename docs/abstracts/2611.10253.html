On the nature of low-energy electronic states in twisted bilayer graphene: a first-principles study

Albert Zhu, Daniel Bennett, Daniel T. Larson, Mohammed M. Al Ezzi, Efstratios Manousakis, Efthimios Kaxiras

arXiv:2611.10253·cond-mat.str-el·Published 2026-11-06

The nature of low-energy electronic states in twisted bilayer graphene (TBG) near the magic angle has been extensively discussed in the literature on qualitative grounds, since these states play a key role in the construction of effective few-band models toward a many-body theory. Here, we report extensive and systematic first-principles calculations of TBG electronic states in the magic-angle range, using density functional theory with no further approximations. We construct moiré-scale localized Wannier states for the low-energy bands and develop an effective ten-band model for each $K$ valley. The Wannier orbitals contain both atomic and moiré-scale details and are centered near the high-symmetry stackings in the moiré cell. We calculate Coulomb and exchange integrals of these localized states, quantities that are the basic ingredients in many-body models. We also compare the values of these integrals to those obtained or assumed in previous works. By symmetrizing the ten-band Hamiltonians and basis orbitals, we provide insight into how the dominant hoppings between the flat-band orbitals evolve as a function of twist angle.
