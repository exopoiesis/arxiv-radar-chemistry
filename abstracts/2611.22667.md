# Generating Circularly Polarized and Chiral Cathodoluminescence from a Single Plasmonic Nanoantenna

**Authors:** Stan de Peinder, Hollie Marks, Quynh Nguyen, Álvaro Rodríguez Echarri, Albert Polman

The generation and control of circularly polarized and chiral light is important for a wide range of applications, from sensing to optical communication and catalysis. Generally, the generation of chiral light emission from plasmonic nanostructures relies on the use of nanoantennas with predefined chiral shape, which requires complex nanofabrication. In this work, we show how localized electron excitation controls the generation of circularly polarized- and chiral cathodoluminescence from a single achiral plasmonic nanoantenna. We exploit the $π$/2 phase difference between the transverse and longitudinal components of the electric field that is carried by 30 keV electrons, to coherently excite degenerate dipolar modes of which the far-field radiation interferes in the far field, thereby creating a strong optical spin-Hall effect. Using angle-resolved cathodoluminescence polarimetry we measure the angle-resolved Stokes parameters for selected positions of the electron beam near the nanoparticle and show how the precise location of the electron beam controls the generation of the circularly polarized and chiral electric field components. Our findings represent an elemental form of generation of circularly polarized light, by synthesizing the circularly rotating electric fields characteristic for CPL from interference of orthogonal dipolar modes with controlled phase lag.
