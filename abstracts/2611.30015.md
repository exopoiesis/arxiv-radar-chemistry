# Nonmonotonic In-Plane Thermal Transport in Metal-Embedded Graphitic Carbon Nitride

**Authors:** Wenwu Jiang, Hekai Bu, Wei Qiu, Yong Ni

Embedding metal atoms within the intrinsic cavities of graphitic carbon nitride (g-C3N4) modifies its local bonding and vibrational properties, yet its consequences for lattice thermal transport remain poorly understood. Here, we combine a machine-learned potential (MLP) with homogeneous nonequilibrium molecular dynamics (HNEMD) to investigate in-plane thermal transport in Cu-embedded g-C3N4 across the full range of cavity occupation. The thermal conductivity exhibits a pronounced nonmonotonic dependence on Cu occupation, decreasing at intermediate occupation and partially recovering as the cavities approach full occupation. Frequency-resolved analysis reveals that this behavior originates from the competition between two phonon contributions. The low-frequency vibrations introduced by Cu couple to the intrinsic modes of g-C3N4, reconstructing the phonon dispersion and suppressing thermal transport below 10 THz. With increasing Cu occupation, the collective mechanical response of the N atoms surrounding the cavities is enhanced, accompanied by increased phonon group velocities in the 20-30 THz range and a corresponding recovery of the high-frequency thermal contribution. The competition between the saturating low-frequency suppression and the increasing high-frequency contribution gives rise to the nonmonotonic thermal conductivity. A similar occupation dependence and spectral redistribution are observed for Ag-embedded g-C3N4. These results identify competing phonon contributions as the microscopic origin of nonmonotonic thermal transport in metal-embedded g-C3N4 and suggest that occupation of intrinsic cavities provides a route to redistributing phonon transport in porous crystalline materials.
