# Manipulation of Ferroelectric Polarization Under Near-Gap Light

**Authors:** Hanli Cui, Jian Zhou

Noncontacting light control of ferroelectric (FE) order has been receiving extensive interest in condensed matter physics. In this work, we investigate near-resonant light irradiation that induces structure-dependent photoexcited carrier density along the transition pathway. We combine a minimal Rice-Mele model containing intersite density-density correlations with ab initio calculations for realistic materials such as GeTe and SnTe. If the photon energy is below-gap without carrier excitation, only off-resonant scattering occurs and the FE order tends to be diminished. Slightly increasing photon energy at the near-gap regime would lead to a centrosymmetric non-polar (NP) to FE phase transition, which is not thermally accessible in conventional pressure-temperature phase diagram. This is in contrast to the well-above-gap light situation that tends to suppress the FE order under a fixed amount of photoexcitation. We apply phenomenological Landau theory and show that they belong to the continuous and first-order phase transitions, respectively. The occupation constrained calculations suggest that these reversible NP-FE phase transitions could be triggered under moderate light fluence. Our mechanism provides a potential route to reversible light-stabilized polar phases with reduced lattice heating and marginal lattice damages.
