Generating grain boundary structures at a specified energy with a symmetry-constrained diffusion model

Kazuya Miyamoto, Ibuki Okuda, Izumi Takahara, Teruyasu Mizoguchi

arXiv:2611.27188·cond-mat.mtrl-sci·Published 2026-11-20

Grain boundaries (GBs) govern many properties of polycrystalline materials, and inverse design would make the GB structure itself a design variable. Crystal generative models such as MatterGen operate on periodic cells, which contain two GBs. The cell energy defines a single GB only when the two are equivalent, but existing models do not enforce this equivalence. Building on MatterGen, we developed a symmetry-constrained diffusion model in which the two GBs are related by a symmetry operation: atoms near one GB are generated, and those near the other are reconstructed through a symmetry projection applied at every denoising step. We applied it to the $Σ5(210)[001]$ symmetric tilt GB in Al, conditioning generation on the GB energy from a machine-learning interatomic potential (MLIP). All 350 generated structures kept a geometrically equivalent GB pair after relaxation. Given a target GB energy, the model generated structures at that energy: for the three in-range targets with many nearby training structures, 44% to 70% fell within $\pm 0.1$ J/m$^2$ of the target. Tracking weakened at higher energies and failed outside the training range. Of the 91 near-target structures, 90 matched no training structure under the adopted geometric criterion, indicating that the model can propose configurations near the target energy that are distinct from the training structures, a capability that could help identify unknown metastable GB structures observed in experiments. Building GB-pair equivalence into generation thus makes GB properties well-defined design targets, enabling inverse design of GB structures.
