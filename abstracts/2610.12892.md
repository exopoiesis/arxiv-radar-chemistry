# Room-temperature ferroelectric p-wave magnet

**Authors:** Kaiwen Shen, Nicoly Hembeck, Warlley H. Campos, Jianting Dong, Bin Li, Yuhang Li, Gui Wang, Jan Priessnitz, Yifan Gao, Yingfen Wei, Hao Jiang, Xumeng Zhang, Ming Wang, Jia Zhang, Wu Shi, Qi Liu, Libor Šmejkal, Xianzhe Chen, Ming Liu

P -wave magnets exhibit odd-parity spin polarization in momentum space while retaining zero net magnetization, offering a promising route toward nonrelativistic spintronics. Previously reported p-wave magnets based on combined time-reversal and lattice-translation symmetry, Tt, have thus far exhibited ordering temperatures below the liquid-nitrogen range. Here, we demonstrate a distinct route to a pristine p-wave magnetism that does not require T t symmetry, and instead is based on incommensurate spin-spiral order. Specifically, the cycloidal magnetic structure of the multiferroic BiFeO3 (BFO) intrinsically realizes p-wave magnetism coexisting with ferroelectric polarization at room temperature. Combining symmetry analysis, density-functional-theory calculations, and circular photogalvanic-effect (CPGE) measurements, we find a large nonrelativistic spin splitting of approximately 100 meV associated with the cycloidal order. The resulting p-wave spin polarization gives rise to a characteristic CPGE response whose sign can be reversibly switched by electrically controlling the cycloid chirality. Our results establish ferroelectric BiFeO3 as a room-temperature platform for electrically switchable p-wave magnetism and provide a route toward voltage-controlled spintronic devices based on parity-breaking magnetic order.
