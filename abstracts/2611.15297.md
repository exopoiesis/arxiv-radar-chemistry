# Periodic Trends in Oxygen Vacancy Formation Energies Across Binary Main-Group Metal Oxides

**Authors:** Manish Kumar, Bridget S. Ferris, Avital Isakov, Robert B. Wexler

The function of oxide materials, from solid-oxide fuel cells to solar thermochemical fuel production, depends on their oxygen vacancies. Their formation energetics have been mapped systematically for ternary perovskites, but reported values for the binary oxides, their conceptual and thermochemical building blocks, remain scattered across studies with differing computational methods. Here we report a systematic r$^2$SCAN density functional theory survey of neutral oxygen vacancy formation energies across 39 binary main-group metal oxide polymorphs spanning groups 1, 2, 13, 14, and 15, using the ShakeNBreak method to locate reconstructed defect ground states. The formation energies span 8 eV and decrease down each group and, for most multivalent cations, with increasing oxidation state. Both trends follow the reducibility of the cation. An interpretable crystal feature model is fit to these energies; its inputs are properties of the pristine bulk, with the reduction potential alternatively taken from tabulated thermochemistry. Retaining only its two dominant descriptors, the crystal reduction potential and the band gap, the model reproduces the formation energies with a held-out mean absolute error of 0.31 eV, lower than that of its four-descriptor parent model for transition-metal perovskites. The bond-breaking and stability terms of the full Born-Haber fit prove statistically null for the binary oxides, and the largest residuals identify the hosts where electron accommodation departs from the cation-reduction picture that the descriptors encode. The dataset provides a benchmark for machine-learning interatomic potentials, and the model estimates vacancy formation energies of main-group oxides from bulk properties alone.
