A Rule That's Made to be Broken? Reframing the Arrhenius Law for Ionic Transport

Vanessa K. Ward, Karen E. Johnston

arXiv:2611.11302·cond-mat.soft·Published 2026-11-08

The Arrhenius law is ubiquitous in the physical sciences. It describes how an observable property scales with temperature, $T$, as $\exp(\pm E_A/k_BT)$ for a constant $E_A$, the 'activation energy'. Fitting to the Arrhenius law is a common procedure for both experimental and computational data. However, the physical significance of any 'activation energy' calculated is unclear. Here we focus on self-diffusion coefficients for potential solid electrolytes, and by identifying the individual 'diffusive steps' within the system, show how a physically significant activation energy can be extracted. There are also many examples of non-Arrhenius behaviour including `super-Arrhenius' curvature, where the effective activation energy appears to increase with decreasing temperature. We demonstrate how it is still possible to extract the underlying Arrhenius law, by quantifying self-correlations such as back-and-forth motion. The methodology described here is material-agnostic and can be utilised to analyse the molecular dynamics trajectories of both ordered and disordered materials.
