# Modulations of the photoswitching performances of azobenzene-based molecular devices by the π-conjugated spacers

**Authors:** Laurette-Apolline Jerro, Imen Hnid, Imane Arbouch, Ali Yassin, David Guérin, Alexis Elias, Colin van Dyck, Lionel Sanguinet, Jérôme Cornil, Stéphane Lenfant, Philippe Blanchard, Dominique Vuillaume

The nature of the π-conjugated spacer is a key element to tune the electrical conductance and optical switching properties of metal-spacer-azobenzene-metal molecular devices. We report that the azobenzene-based molecular junctions with a thiophene or a phenylthiophene spacer exhibit a high cis/trans conductance ratio (ca. 1,000), while those with a bithiophene spacer have a lower conductance switching ratio (ca. 500) at the stated conditions (light wavelength and intensity) and for one batch of samples (out of two). Similarly, the two former molecular junctions have quite similar trans-to-cis isomerization kinetics, while the latter has a lower switching rate. The energy level of the highest occupied molecular orbital, which controls the electron transfer in the molecular junction, is weakly influenced by the nature of the spacer, nor by the conformation of the azobenzene moieties. The large increase in the conductance of the molecular junction with azobenzene in the cis-state is explained by an increase in the electronic coupling energy between the molecule and the top electrode. Theoretical studies by Density Functional Theory and Non Equilibrium Green's Functions are qualitatively consistent with the experiments. We find a consistent structure-property trend, showing that the higher molecule/electrode coupling energy for the cis-state is explained by the top benzene ring of the azobenzene lying flat with respect to the top electrode for the thiophene, phenylthiophene and bithiophene spacers. On the contrary, we observe that a spacer with a carbon-carbon triple bond (acetylene thiophene) induces a large irreproducibility and unpredictability of the results.
