# Thermodynamics of complex crystals from a generalized mean-field bond model

**Authors:** Raynol Dsouza, Thomas D. Swinburne, Jörg Neugebauer

We extend our previously introduced mean-field bond model from unary fcc lattices to crystals with multiple chemical species and sublattices, accurately predicting finite-temperature thermodynamics without statistical sampling. Our generalization reformulates the model in terms of the Wyckoff positions of the crystal, representing each by one atom, and solving for the bond density of the reference bond of each of its neighbor shells. Bond-bond correlation functions are built from the zero-temperature force constants, allowing the deviation of every bond in the crystal to be expressed through linear functions of the reference bond of a shell. Thermodynamic self-consistency is enforced by requiring a single virial temperature for the whole crystal, and compatibility of average bond lengths with crystal volume. Model predictions are validated against molecular dynamics simulations of unary, binary, and ternary crystals using Morse-type bonding potentials, parametrized for each shell from the force constants of empirical interatomic potentials at several isotropic strains. In each case, the predicted bond densities and thermodynamics agree with the simulations to within their statistical uncertainty. We discuss strategies for combining the model with bonding potentials parametrized from first principles and foundation models to predict the finite-temperature thermodynamics of complex crystals at ab initio accuracy without statistical sampling.
