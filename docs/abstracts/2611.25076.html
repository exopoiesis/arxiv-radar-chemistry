ShEPhERD-score: Fast, adaptable three-dimensional alignment of molecular features

Nate Somers, Kento A. Abeywardane, Connor W. Coley, Masha Elkin

arXiv:2611.25076·q-bio.BM·Published 2026-11-18·Updated 2026-11-20

Three-dimensional (3D) molecular similarity scoring and alignment are widely employed in ligand-based virtual screening and molecular design, and are increasingly used for training and evaluating machine learning models. Existing open-source methods utilize various molecular representations spanning shape, electrostatics, pharmacophores, and hybrid representations, but their implementations as independent software packages with distinct application programming interfaces (APIs), optimizers, and limited GPU support make them difficult to compare, combine, or extend. Here, we develop ShEPhERD-score into a unified, modular platform for differentiable 3D molecular similarity scoring and alignment by combining diverse molecular representations with high-performance Numba CPU and Triton GPU kernels to support both large-scale library screening and pairwise similarity workflows. In matched comparisons with open-source 3D similarity tools on the DUDE-Z virtual screening benchmark, ShEPhERD-score performed comparably or better in eight of nine cases. Across the scoring modes in ShEPhERD-score, throughput ranges from 17,800 to 1.03 million alignments/second for ten-million-conformer libraries using a single NVIDIA L40S GPU. Moreover, we found that the most effective scoring mode varied by protein target, indicating that target-specific calibration against validation data may be valuable before large-scale screening and underscoring the need for a unified platform that provides diverse molecular representations. Together, these capabilities establish ShEPhERD-score as a single, extensible toolkit for rapid virtual screening and machine learning workflows.
