Dynamics and transport in LiF using classical and machine learned interatomic potentials

Harvey L. Devereux, Margaret-Ann Withington, Alin M. Elena, Cillian Cockrell, Kostya Trachenko

arXiv:2611.21900·cond-mat.mtrl-sci·Published 2026-11-16

We perform molecular dynamics simulations of molten Lithium Fluoride, comparing the classical Buckingham and Born-Huggins-Mayer potentials with machine-learnt interatomic potentials - the MACE-MP-0b3 (small) foundation model potential, and a fine-tuned version thereof. We find that the MACE-MP-0b3, out-of-the-box, is able to accurately reproduce the experimental shear viscosity across the liquid state comparable with polarisable potentials despite not itself being polarisable. Previous viscosities computed using classical potentials are under-predicted, which has previously been attributed to a suppressed melting temperature. Prompted by this we compare the melting points of these potentials. We find that the machine-learnt potentials address the significant underestimation of the melting point of the classical potentials, though the refined potential and the foundation model differ significantly between themselves. We probe the microscopic structure and dynamics of each model to further elucidate the considerable change in viscosity between classical and machine-learnt potentials. We find that, despite their lower melting temperature, classical potentials produce considerably stiffer, higher frequency, single-particle and collective charge-transport dynamics on account of their fixed charges. However, in the long-wavelength limit, the many-body machine-learnt potentials feature more elastic collective motion that governs the diffusion of momentum and is therefore responsible for the enhanced shear viscosity.
