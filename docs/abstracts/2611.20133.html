Robust Antiferromagnetic Ordering above 500 K in a Family of Bilayer van der Waals Materials

Tengda Rong, Linxin Wu, Xingke Fu, Anqi Huang, Feiran Shen, Zixian Dong, Jianwei Huang, Hao Li, Benyuan Cheng, Lunhua He, Long Jiang, Kun Cao, Zhen Chen, Meng Wang, Xiang Chen

arXiv:2611.20133·cond-mat.mtrl-sci·Published 2026-11-15

Stabilizing magnetic order above room temperature in layered materials is highly desirable for low-dimensional spintronics applications. However, realizing room-temperature antiferromagnetism in layered magnets remains challenging. Here, we present a layered semiconducting oxyhalide material family, Sr$_3$Fe$_2$O$_5$X$_2$ ($X$ = F, Cl, Br), which exhibits robust high-temperature antiferromagnetic ordering. The two-dimensional nature and scalability of this system are demonstrated by atomically resolving the van der Waals gap of Sr$_3$Fe$_2$O$_5$Br$_2$ and synthesizing centimeter-scale single crystals of Sr$_3$Fe$_2$O$_5$Cl$_2$. Temperature-dependent neutron scattering and magnetization measurements determine their G-type magnetic structure and transition temperature. Remarkably, the Néel temperature of Sr$_3$Fe$_2$O$_5$F$_2$ ($\sim$580 K) is the highest among all reported layered antiferromagnets. Density functional theory calculations demonstrate that strong nearest-neighbor superexchange interactions along both out-of-plane and in-plane directions, particularly the magnetic couplings along lattice $c$ direction within the bilayer motif, are primarily responsible for the high Néel temperatures in Sr$_3$Fe$_2$O$_5$X$_2$. In contrast, inter-bilayer spin couplings are negligibly weak, without much influence on the stabilization of the antiferromagnetic order. Therefore, theoretical calculations predict that this robust antiferromagnetic order could persist or even strengthens at room-temperature even at the monolayer limit. Our results highlight iron-based layered oxyhalides as a promising platform for exploring beyond-room-temperature antiferromagnetism in the two-dimensional limit, holding immense potential in areas like information storage and spin-based devices.
