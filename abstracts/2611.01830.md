# Temperature-Dependent Raman Signatures of Local Symmetry Breaking in Vacancy Double Perovskite Cs$_2$SnI$_6$

**Authors:** Tomke E. Glier, Nicholas J. Weadock, Julian A. Vigil, Cole Shank, Xixi Qin, Gabriel J. Graf, Hemamala I. Karunadasa, Volker Blum, Michael F. Toney, Dmitry Reznik

Understanding and controlling the optical, thermal and mechanical properties of K$_2$PtCl$_6$-type double perovskites is essential for the design of lead-free, high-performance thermoelectric and optoelectronic materials. In this study, we investigate Cs$_2$SnI$_6$ using temperature-dependent near-resonance Raman scattering, complemented by density functional theory-based phonon calculations. The appearance of Raman-forbidden phonon modes in the Raman response at cryogenic temperatures provides strong evidence for local symmetry breaking that does not develop into a global structural phase transition. Through detailed analysis of phonon intensities, energies, and spectral lineshapes, we characterize the temperature evolution of the symmetry-broken structural response of Cs$_2$SnI$_6$. Below 200 K, multi-phonon scattering is strongly enhanced, and symmetry-forbidden Raman modes emerge. The temperature dependence of phonon intensities follows a phenomenological Arrhenius behavior, revealing an activation energy of 15-20 meV. These results establish temperature-dependent Raman spectroscopy as a sensitive probe of local structural distortions and anharmonic structural dynamics in Cs$_2$SnI$_6$, providing a robust experimental basis for discussing the microscopic origins of symmetry breaking and electron-phonon coupling in halide double perovskites.
