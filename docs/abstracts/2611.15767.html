A Hierarchical Harmonic-to-Anharmonic Framework for ab initio Screening and Mechanistic Analysis of Negative Thermal Expansion in ZrV2O7

J. Bustamante, A. Miliute, N. Jaykhedkar, A. A. Naik, J. Grandel, F. Emmerling, T. M. Stawski, J. George

arXiv:2611.15767·cond-mat.mtrl-sci·Published 2026-11-11

Negative thermal expansion (NTE) in framework materials arises from complex lattice dynamics that are challenging to capture using conventional theoretical approaches. Here, we establish a hierarchical computational strategy for investigating NTE in ZrV2O7, integrating the quasiharmonic approximation (QHA), temperature-dependent effective potentials (TDEP), and machine-learning-accelerated ab initio molecular dynamics (ML-MD). Quasi-harmonic lattice-dynamical calculations identify low-frequency phonon modes with negative Grüneisen parameters as inexpensive screening descriptors, while TDEP incorporates finite-temperature anharmonicity and ML-MD further reveals temperature-dependent structural distortions that drive the lattice contraction. The observations are further corroborated with X-ray diffraction (XRD) measurements. The concurrent increase in transverse displacement (uT) provides microscopic evidence for a quasi-rigid-unit-mode (QRUM) mechanism underlying the NTE behaviour at elevated temperatures. This is consistent with previous reports on other framework materials, including ZrW2O7, where collective polyhedral rotations and transverse atomic motions have been linked to NTE. Overall, our results demonstrate how relatively inexpensive lattice-dynamical descriptors can identify potential NTE materials, while higher-level anharmonic simulations can then resolve the underlying structural mechanisms at the atomic scale.
