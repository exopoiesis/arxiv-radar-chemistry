# Tuning spreading transport in GaN HEMTs with an AlN space layer under non-equilibrium heating

**Authors:** Daosheng Tang, Rui Hu, Chen Ning, Bingyang Cao

Lateral spreading transport dominates near-junction heat dissipation in GaN high-electron-mobility transistors, yet thermal control has largely overlooked this geometry. It neither reaches the core AlGaN/GaN region nor, beyond the spatial spreading itself, accounts for the spectral non-equilibrium of the heat source. Here we tune the spreading resistance directly by inserting a thin, highly conducting AlN space layer above the source to intercept heat and divert it laterally. We test this approach across GaN, AlGaN/GaN, and AlGaN/AlN/GaN structures under both equilibrium and longitudinal-optical (LO)-pumped non-equilibrium heating, pairing non-equilibrium molecular dynamics (NEMD) with full-band phonon Monte Carlo (MC). In equilibrium, the AlN layer lowers the spreading resistance by 38% relative to the AlGaN-capped structure and by 17% relative to bare GaN, with little change in the one-dimensional resistance. The equilibrium color map over AlN thickness and interfacial bonding shows a strong interface effect but a weak thickness trend that reverses, so the gain is set by interface admission rather than solely by thickness or bulk conductivity. This ordering is independently confirmed by gray MC simulations. Under LO pumping, the spreading resistance rises by 58-83% in NEMD and by 9-20% in MC, and the equilibrium spatial optimum weakens or even reverses. This is because an optical-to-acoustic conversion gate now precedes every spatial gate and locks energy in slow optical modes. The three gates in series (optical-to-acoustic conversion, interface admission, and lateral diversion) close into a single non-equilibrium resistance network that predicts the full-band MC cases and frames the AlN space layer as a design variable within the non-equilibrium source state.
