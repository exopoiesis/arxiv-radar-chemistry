# OSIRIS-REx Lessons Learned Report: Insights from the Journey to Bennu and Back

**Authors:** Dante S. Lauretta, Heather L. Enos, C. W. V. Wolner

OSIRIS-REx, NASA's third New Frontiers mission, was the first U.S. mission to return a sample from an asteroid. Proposed in 2010 for an \$811 million lifecycle cost, the mission was completed for \$828 million, returned more than \$52 million of Headquarters reserves unused, and delivered at least 121.6 g of pristine material from near-Earth asteroid (101955) Bennu in September 2023, more than twice the Level-1 requirement. It did so despite a surface far more rugged than predicted, the discovery of particle ejection, sampling in a regolith much weaker than expected, operations during the COVID-19 pandemic, and an off-nominal parachute sequence on Earth return. This report sets out the lessons learned by the mission's leadership across six areas: leadership and governance; cost, schedule and risk management; systems engineering; mission operations; sample return, curation and analysis; and data archiving. Recurring themes are that a PI-led structure integrating science, engineering and project management gives the discipline to make fast, informed decisions; that reserves and schedule margin spent early to retire risk, such as funding Natural Feature Tracking as a backup navigation system, buy the flexibility to absorb discovery; that heritage hardware reduces risk only when its verification and engineering knowledge are preserved; and that contamination knowledge, sample science and data stewardship are mission-long responsibilities, not post-flight tasks. Each section pairs background with specific recommendations for future sample-return and small-body missions.
