Mapping phase competition in GaAs under pressure and temperature

Sakshi Patel, Paras Patel, Prafulla K. Jha

arXiv:2611.22332·cond-mat.mtrl-sci·Published 2026-11-17

Pressure-induced transformations in GaAs offer a means to investigate the interplay between crystal structure, electronic behaviour, and phase stability in a technologically important III-V semiconductor. Here, we have used structure search algorithm combined with first-principles calculations and identified high-pressure competitive phases. The previously proposed $Imm2$ structure lies above the calculated minimum-enthalpy envelope. The static enthalpy calculations with the PBEsol functional predict the transition sequence $F\bar{4}3m \rightarrow Cmcm \rightarrow I\bar{4}m2 \rightarrow C2/m \rightarrow Amm2$, at approximately 12, 14, 34, and 69 GPa, respectively. The initial zinc blende to $Cmcm$ transition is accompanied by a volume reduction of approximately 17.5 % and metallization within the PBEsol description. Phonon calculations support the dynamical stability of all five phases at representative pressures, while elastic calculations characterize their mechanical response. Vibrational free energies are incorporated within the quasiharmonic approximation to examine phase competition over 0-80 GPa and 0-1000 K. These results identify competing high-pressure structures and connect their relative energetics with the electronic, vibrational, and elastic responses of compressed GaAs.
