# Materials representations organize transferable supervision for chemical extrapolation

**Authors:** Enda Xiao, Terumasa Tadano

Materials discovery often requires chemical extrapolation beyond labeled data. When target compounds contain elements absent from training, which compounds of other elements provide transferable supervision remains unclear. Here we show that, without target-property labels, materials representations rank other elements by the supervision their compounds provide. Engineered descriptors rank them largely by elemental-property similarity, whereas a pretrained crystal-graph encoder ranks them by the geometry of its learned representation. At fixed training-set size, excluding the compounds of the encoder's six top-ranked elements raises formation-energy and bandgap errors by 100-110 meV per atom and 42-85 meV, versus little change for six lower-ranked elements. The rankings hold across properties, regressors and datasets. The encoder's ranking outperforms elemental-property similarity and periodic groups, suggesting that it captures how elements behave within compounds. Materials representations thus organize transferable supervision, which can be identified before model training, informing dataset assessment and label acquisition.
