# Antiferroelectric Fluids with Hierarchical Polar Organization

**Authors:** Hiroya Nishikawa, Hina Saito, Tetsu Katayama, Atsuko Nihonyanagi, Dennis Kwaria, Hiroyuki Inuzuka, Junxi Liu, Go Watanabe, Fumito Araoka

The discovery of the ferroelectric nematic (NF) phase has accelerated research on fluids exhibiting polar order along the molecular long axis. However, their antiferroelectric (AFE) counterparts remain less explored. Here, we investigate how terminal alkyl-chain elongation competes with polar core-core interactions in a homologous series. This series exhibits modulated AFE order accompanied by SmC-type cybotactic correlations, with the AFE phase persisting in homologs bearing terminal alkyl chains of up to 17 carbon atoms. Three distinct phase sequences on cooling lead to two types of two-dimensional (2D) AFE lattices. Multiple sharp X-ray diffraction (XRD) peaks reveal rectangular and oblique lattice structures, supporting their classification as AFE columnar phases. XRD analysis and molecular dynamics (MD) simulations support a hierarchical structural model in which locally aligned polar molecules occupy thin segments of finite width within polar columns. The columns occupy the sites of a 2D lattice, with neighboring columns polarized in opposite directions. This model provides a structural framework for understanding the distinct precursor phase sequences and links packing between columns to the resulting AFE superstructures.
