Discovery of Kondo-Kramers Nodal Line Topological State in strongly correlated material CeAuAl$_3$

Hari Bhandari, Kevin Allen, Kuan-Sen Lin, Yuan Fang, Arushi, Karthik Rao, Mounica Mahankali, Sanu Mishra, Jian-Xin Zhu, Christopher Lane, Qimiao Si, Emilia Morosan

arXiv:2611.10368·cond-mat.str-el·Published 2026-11-07·Updated 2026-11-10

Kramers nodal lines (KNLs) are predicted in all achiral, non-centrosymmetric crystal structures, and yet there are very few experimental realizations of KNLs, only in weakly correlated systems. Especially, their realization in strongly correlated systems remains unexplored. Here we investigate KNL topology in the non-centrosymmetric, achiral Kondo lattice CeAuAl$_3$, using magnetotransport, thermodynamic measurements, density functional theory (DFT), and tight-binding toy model calculations. We find that CeAuAl$_3$ hosts KNLs in the $\mathitΓ\text{-}Z$ and $X\text{-}P$ directions, protected by time-reversal and mirror symmetries. Breaking of these crystalline symmetries, along with time-reversal symmetry via spin polarization, lifts the KNL degeneracy, which, in turn, generates Berry curvature resulting in an intrinsic anomalous Hall effect (AHE). More importantly, CeAuAl$_3$ shows large AHE not only below the magnetic ordering temperature $T_N$ but rather below the Kondo temperature. This underscores the role of the Kondo correlations in bringing the KNLs at the Fermi surface rather than at accidental energies, as would be expected without correlations. Our work identifies a new topological state, Kondo-Kramers Nodal Line, with CeAuAl$_3$ being the first compound explicitly identified to host this new correlated topological state, and demonstrating the expected AHE associated with it. More generally, our work outlines the pathway for finding Kondo-KNL systems in all compounds that combine achiral, non-centrosymmetric structure with Kondo correlations.
