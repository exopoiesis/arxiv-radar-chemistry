# Cold Magnetized Quark Matter under Compact-Star Conditions

**Authors:** S. A. Ferraris, J. P. Carlomagno, G. A. Contrera, A. G. Grunfeld

We investigate the phase structure of cold magnetized two-flavor quark matter under compact-star conditions within a nonlocal chiral quark model with separable interactions. Electric charge neutrality and beta equilibrium are imposed in order to describe matter under realistic neutron-star conditions, while nonlocality is implemented through a Gaussian form factor. We find that low and moderate magnetic fields reduce the critical chemical potential for chiral symmetry restoration, signaling inverse magnetic catalysis. For stronger magnetic fields, the critical chemical potential becomes nearly independent of the magnetic field strength, Compared with the isospin-symmetric case, the combined effects of charge neutrality and beta equilibrium shift the first-order chiral transition to slightly higher chemical potentials, indicating that compact-star constraints delay the onset of chiral symmetry restoration. We further show that charge neutrality and beta equilibrium modify the de Haas--van Alphen oscillations in the quark density, shifting the Landau-level thresholds for the $u$ and $d$ quarks in opposite directions relative to the isospin-symmetric case. The effect on the chiral transition is small ($\sim 5$MeV) for fields $B\lesssim 10^{19}$G, relevant for magnetar interiors, and becomes sizable only for $B\gtrsim 3\times10^{19}$G. These results highlight the importance of incorporating realistic stellar conditions in studies of the QCD phase diagram at high baryon density.
