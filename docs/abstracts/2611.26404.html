Interpretation follows computation for deformed atomic nuclei

G. Hagen, T. Papenbrock

arXiv:2611.26404·nucl-th·Published 2026-11-19

The interpretation of nuclear levels in experiments and ab initio computations is challenging because it requires the production and analysis of many expectation values and transition strengths. Here we introduce an approach that yields directly interpretable states. Our ab initio coupled-cluster computations start from a reference state with axial symmetry and positive parity. Equation-of-motion techniques then yield intrinsic states with definite parity and angular-momentum projection onto the symmetry axis. Finally, the projection onto good angular momenta yields rotational bands based on these intrinsic states. In the neutron-rich nucleus $^{34}$Mg, where only the ground-state band is known, we find several low-lying positive- and negative-parity bands. The lowest, a $K^π=3^-$ band, is dominated by a single neutron particle-hole excitation rather than by octupole collectivity. These results challenge the common wisdom about how deformation and pairing work in nuclei.
