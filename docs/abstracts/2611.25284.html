Altermagnetic magnon splitting in hematite: from bulk g-wave to planar i-wave

Tobias Dannegger, Julian A. Beisch, Levente Rózsa, László Szunyogh, Ulrich Nowak

arXiv:2611.25284·cond-mat.mtrl-sci·Published 2026-11-18

Hematite is an insulating iron oxide with both a collinear antiferromagnetic and a weakly ferromagnetic phase with intriguing properties, such as long-range spin transport distances, that make it promising for spintronics applications. It has also been identified as a candidate for g-wave altermagnetism. Here, we present the results of fully relativistic ab initio calculations of the magnon properties of hematite, with a focus on altermagnetic effects. We quantitatively evaluate both the anisotropic and altermagnetic magnon band splitting as well as the magnon lifetimes. We show that dipolar interactions lead to a sizeable non-altermagnetic splitting and a hybridisation between opposite-chirality magnons resulting in a strong reduction of the spin expectation value of magnons close to the Γ point and around nodal planes, even in the collinear out-of-plane phase. Both the frequencies and the lifetimes have a large altermagnetic splitting in the $k_z = 0$ plane, which is characteristic of hematite and not generally expected for g-wave altermagnets. We compare our results to the prototypical, hexagonal g-wave altermagnet manganese telluride, which has four symmetry-protected nodal planes, whereas the fourth nodal surface in hematite is not completely fixed by symmetry. We derive implicit analytical expressions for this surface and discuss how it arises from a combination of rotational and translational symmetry, which leads to a transition to i-wave symmetry at the Brillouin zone boundary. The example of hematite reveals the trigonal altermagnets as a special case where the splitting function exhibits both bulk (g-wave) and planar (i-wave) components, and shows that the point symmetries alone are not sufficient to capture the full symmetry-determined behaviour of the splitting function.
