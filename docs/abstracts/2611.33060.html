Native vacancy energetics and association in TiN and CrN: chemical potential dependence, clustering, and surface effects

Barsha Bhattacharjee, Emilia Olsson

arXiv:2611.33060·cond-mat.mtrl-sci·Published 2026-11-24

Native vacancy populations in transition-metal nitride coatings reflect vacancy formation, association, and depth-dependent stability. Using density functional theory (DFT), we compare these effects in rocksalt TiN and CrN in bulk and at (001) surfaces, within phase-stability-constrained chemical potential ranges. In TiN, $V_\mathrm{N}$ is favoured under N-poor and $V_\mathrm{Ti}$ under N-rich conditions; in CrN, $V_\mathrm{N}$ remains preferred throughout its stability range. Association energies are referenced to corresponding isolated vacancies where positive values mean association lowers the energy and negative values favour separation. Mean bulk association energies for mixed metal-nitrogen pairs are $+0.132$ eV in TiN and $+1.053$ eV in CrN. At (001) surfaces, $V_\mathrm{N}$ in TiN prefers the surface and $V_\mathrm{Ti}$ the first subsurface layer. In CrN, $V_\mathrm{N}$ has similar formation energies at the surface and first subsurface layer, with a slight preference for the latter; $V_\mathrm{Cr}$ slightly prefers the second subsurface layer. Surface cluster energies are referenced to separated vacancies at their preferred depths. The compact $2V_\mathrm{Ti}+2V_\mathrm{N}$ cluster in TiN has favourable association in bulk ($+0.217$ eV per vacancy) but unfavourable association at the surface ($-0.346$ eV per vacancy). By contrast, $2V_\mathrm{Cr}+2V_\mathrm{N}$ in CrN remains stabilised, with its association energy decreasing from $+1.188$ to $+0.431$ eV per vacancy. CrN's stronger association occurs without larger structural distortions and is accompanied by distinct Cr-centred magnetic responses.
