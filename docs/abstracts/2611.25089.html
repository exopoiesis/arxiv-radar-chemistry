Chiral Phonons Protected by Symmetry in a Strongly Anharmonic Helical Material

Supriyo Ghosh, Dipti Jasrasaria

arXiv:2611.25089·cond-mat.mtrl-sci·Published 2026-11-18

How chiral phonons survive anharmonic phonon-phonon interactions, which underpin lattice dynamics at finite temperature, remains poorly understood. Using an ab initio many-body approach, we study crystalline tellurium (Te), a strongly anharmonic helical crystal. We show that rotational symmetries in Te impose pseudoangular momentum selection rules on phonon-phonon interactions, restricting mode mixing and scattering at rotationally invariant symmetry points in the Brillouin zone. These symmetries protect the circular polarization of vibrational modes at finite temperature but not their lifetimes such that the dynamic chirality of handed vibrational modes persists to 600 K, even as lifetimes drop sharply. We find that the temperature-dependence of angular momentum generated by a thermal gradient is governed by lifetimes rather than chirality, decreasing by two orders of magnitude between 10 and 600 K.
