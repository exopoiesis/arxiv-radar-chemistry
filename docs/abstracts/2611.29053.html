Fractal dimension of velocity-driven mixing: Reanalysis of Cao et al. (Phys. Rev. A 45, 3841 (1992)) and the universality of the Marstrand co-dimension rule

Rod W. Douglass, Steven R. Dunbar

arXiv:2611.29053·physics.flu-dyn·Published 2026-11-21

Cao et al. [Phys. Rev. A 45, 3841 (1992)] compared box-counting fractal dimensions of interfaces from continuum (SOLA-VOF) and molecular dynamics (MD) simulations of zero-Atwood-number mixing driven by random velocity perturbations. They reported D ~ 1.1-1.4 across physical models. We revisit their results with grid-converged geometric VOF simulations and modern box-counting algorithms. The grid-converged continuum dimension depends on the initial-condition realization (D = 1.13-1.38). Independent LAMMPS simulations show that Cao's MD dimension measures thermal scatter of atomic positions, not interface geometry, because their box-counting window lies below the scale of the interface structure: a flat interface at t=0 gives the same D ~ 1.36. When the thermal scatter is filtered by reducing the midpoint cloud to a curve (median binning at 2 sigma), the MD trajectory converges to the same late-time D ~ 1.43 as the continuum, recovering cross-model universality. We prove that the linear-regime box-counting dimension is exactly D = 2 - beta (A > 0) and D = 5/2 - beta (A = 0), where beta is the spectral exponent of the initial perturbation. For white noise (beta = 0) neither formula applies; Cao's early-time D ~ 2 is a boundary artifact. A three-dimensional grid convergence study gives D_3D = 2.361 +/- 0.002 at 400x400x200. Marstrand's co-dimension rule D_3D = D_2D + 1 shows a 7% discrepancy reflecting IC-realization dependence at A = 0, unlike the buoyancy-driven case where the discrepancy falls with resolution.
