PRIMEAPE: A Multilingual Benchmark for Fine-Grained Meta-Evaluation of Judge Models

Vladislav Mikhailov, Sarfraz Ahmad, Momina Ahsan, Zoia Butenko, Vladimir Dobrovolskii, Preslav Nakov, Erik Velldal, Lilja Øvrelid, Ekaterina Artemova

arXiv:2611.30925·cs.CL·Published 2026-11-23

Large language models increasingly serve as judges of other models, shaping benchmark rankings and model development. Yet, a judge whose rationale consistently supports its verdict may still disagree with human preferences. To study this, we introduce PRIMEAPE, a multilingual benchmark for evaluating judge verdicts and rationales. PRIMEAPE provides 2,205 response pairs covering 243 instructions in English, French, Spanish, Urdu, Hindi, Norwegian Bokmål, and Yoruba, with 11k human annotations of preferences and rationales across relevance, naturalness, truthfulness, safety, and overall quality. Our evaluation of twelve open- and closed-weight judges reveals that aggregate performance conceals differences across languages and criteria, while requesting rationales does not consistently improve agreement with human preferences. Judges also favor the first position, longer responses, and responses from related models, with effects that vary across criteria and languages. Crucially, judges can justify verdicts that disagree with human preferences while maintaining consistency between their verdicts and rationales. These findings show that consistency with a judge's own verdict does not establish alignment with human judgment. PRIMEAPE enables assessment of both, providing a foundation for more reliable multilingual judges. We release our benchmark and all annotation materials under the CC BY-NC 4.0 license at github.com/Toloka/primeape.
