Computational Modeling of Sublattice Magnetization in CoTb/Co/CoTb Magnetic Multilayer Nanojunctions Between Cobalt Leads

Sushita Sharma, Vinod Ashokan

arXiv:2611.22644·cond-mat.mtrl-sci·Published 2026-11-17

In this work, we study the sublattice nano-magnetic behavior of CoTb/Co/CoTb sandwiched between semi-infinite cobalt leads. To perform the analysis, we use the Ising spin-based effective field theory (EFT), which enables us to determine the relevant magnetic exchange parameters for elemental cobalt JCo-Co and terbium JTb-Tb. The exchange interaction parameters are determined by fitting the magnetization calculated using the Ising effective-field theory to the experimentally measured temperature-dependent magnetization curves. This fitting procedure yields the exchange constants for Co and Tb, along with their respective Curie temperatures. We use the magnetic exchange parameter obtained using Ising EFT-based method is then seeded on to mean field theory (MFT) to calculate the sublattice magnetizations of CoTb/Co/CoTb arranged between semi-infinite cobalt leads. The sublattice magnetizations for every atomic layer and corresponding lattice sites in the layered ferrimagnetic nanostructures are computed separately. This allows us to analyze spin-site-specific parameters, magnetic moments, magnetization, and ferrimagnetic compensation behavior for varying eutectic compositions, particularly for \(c \leq 0.5\). The results give enhanced insight into magnetic phenomena at the local level and their general relevance in the ultra-thin layered ferrimagnetic structure dynamics confined between semi-infinite cobalt electrodes. These results are needed for the understanding of magnon transport phenomena and consequent magnetic amplification effects and are crucial to the development of spintronic technologies. Most importantly, to date, no first-principles or density functional theory research exists for sublattice magnetization in such multilayered systems. The versatile approach established in this work can also be applied to other nanostructured magnetic systems
