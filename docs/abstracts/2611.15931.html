Anatomy of LyC Escape in MEGATRON: Connecting Feedback Physics to Demographics, Leakage Timescales, and Explorations of ISM Density as a Tracer of Escape Fraction

Nicholas Choustikov, Harley Katz, Taysun Kimm, Matthew J. Hayes, Aaron Smith, Crystal L. Martin, Shyam H. Menon, Larrance Xing, Joki Rosdahl, John Chisholm, Aayush Saxena, Martin P. Rey, Adrianne Slyz, Julien Devriendt, Alex J. Cameron, Anatole Storck, Corentin Cadiou

arXiv:2611.15931·astro-ph.GA·Published 2026-11-11

Constraining the Lyman Continuum (LyC) escape fraction ($f_{\rm esc}$) of high-redshift galaxies is essential to connect the abundant populations revealed by \textit{JWST} to cosmic reionization. We investigate this interplay using {\small MEGATRON}, a suite of zoom simulations with detailed radiation/chemistry and varied feedback prescriptions. We find that feedback physics simultaneously regulates UV source counts, LyC escape fractions, and escaping ionizing luminosities ($\dot{N}_{\rm ion,esc}$), producing different reionization histories. These differences are governed by the delay between a starburst and LyC escape: efficient early feedback and lower-density environments enable photons to escape while pre-supernovae stellar populations remain strongly ionizing. Consequently, models that produce fewer UV-bright galaxies yield earlier LyC escape and reionize sooner than models with more abundant UV sources and delayed LyC emission. Furthermore, UV-faint galaxies follow a common physical sequence in ISM structure with increasing $f_{\rm esc}$ irrespective of feedback physics: stronger leakers ($1\%\leq f_{\rm esc}\leq 50\%$) exhibit evidence of strong photoionization and SNe feedback alongside post-starburst star formation histories, while extreme leakers ($f_{\rm esc}\geq 50\%$) have also cleared their cold/dense ISM. We find that electron densities inferred from the $\rm [O\,{II}]\,λ\lambda3727$ doublet are a candidate diagnostic of $f_{\rm esc}$, with lower densities around $\rm H~{\small II}$ regions selecting stronger leakers. Moreover, this density can trace the early evolution of $f_{\rm esc}$, whereas UV slope-based calibrations over-predict $\dot{N}_{\rm ion,esc}$ for individual young galaxies by up to two orders of magnitude. Overall, the delayed duty cycle of LyC leakage must be accounted for when UV source counts are interpreted in the context of reionization.
