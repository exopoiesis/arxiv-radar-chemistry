Symmetry induced spin splitting of topological surface states at equal carrier density

Wouter Beugeling, Shyam Sundar Yadav, Maximilian Hofer, Valentin L. Müller, Moritz Siebert, Tobias Kießling, Ewelina M. Hankiewicz, Hartmut Buhmann, Laurens W. Molenkamp

arXiv:2611.18342·cond-mat.mes-hall·Published 2026-11-13

Surface states are among the most striking consequences of band inversion in topological materials. In magnetotransport of three-dimensional topological insulators, the typical signature of surface state transport is a quantized Hall conductance with nontrivial sequences of integer filling factors, varying with the total carrier density. For two independent Dirac carriers, one expects Hall plateaus only at odd-integer filling factors. In this work, we report the observation of symmetry induced spin splitting in magnetotransport experiments on a dual-gate three-dimensional topological insulator device based on HgTe. In the topological surface state regime, we observe Hall plateaus at both odd and even integers when we gate the device symmetrically, i.e., with equal density at the top and bottom. This phenomenon originates in structural inversion symmetry, which remarkably induces a spin splitting and forces the eigenstates to be symmetric and antisymmetric linear combinations of the top and bottom surface states. This is in contrast with two-dimensional systems, where spin splitting is caused by absence of inversion symmetry. We interpret the experimental observations with a perturbative model using isoparity eigenstates, the conserved quantum number associated with structural inversion. The result demonstrates that the picture where the top and bottom states are treated as independent Dirac carriers is not valid when the carrier density is symmetric.
