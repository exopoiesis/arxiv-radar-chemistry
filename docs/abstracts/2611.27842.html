Prediction and characterisation of cation topology of pentanary high-entropy alloy (MoWNbTaV)S$_2$

Shubham Agarwal, Tahir Wahab, Jesper Byggmästar, Antonio Cammarata, Tomas Polcar

arXiv:2611.27842·cond-mat.mtrl-sci·Published 2026-11-20

High-entropy transition-metal dichalcogenides offer properties tuned by composition, yet two basic questions remain open: which arrangement of the metals forms under synthesis conditions, and how it can be recognised in experiment. Density functional theory (DFT) cannot sample the vast space of possible arrangements, and the ground-state energies usually employed to screen them do not take into account the temperatures at which these materials are made. This work solves both issues for the pentanary (MoWNbTaV)S\textsubscript{2} monolayer with a Gaussian approximation potential trained on DFT data by iterative learning. Gibbs free energy calculations reveal that vibrational entropy reshapes the stability ranking, while five selected configurations retain their trigonal-prismatic framework during nanosecond molecular dynamics up to \qty{1300}{\kelvin}. Raman and infrared spectra combine parent-monolayer signatures with disorder-activated modes, providing fingerprints for experimental identification. % The predicted lattice parameter follows Vegard's law and agrees within \qty{0.8}{\percent} with X{-}ray diffraction on a sputtered alloy film, which identifies the solid solution but not the arrangement of its metals: distinguishing between arrangements requires the vibrational fingerprints. The potential and its development workflow enable further atomistic simulations and offer a transferable approach to screening related two-dimensional high-entropy materials.
