# Entanglement, correlations, and resource costs for symmetry-adapted quantum simulations of light through medium-mass nuclei

**Authors:** Darin C. Mumma, Eunjin In, Noah Thompson, Kristina D. Launey

Entanglement governs both the classical cost of simulating correlated many-body systems and the quantum resources needed to treat them. In this work, we use nuclear symmetries to investigate the entanglement and correlations in nuclei using \textit{ab initio} symmetry-adapted no-core shell-model nuclear wavefunctions, illustrated here for $^{4,6,8}$He, $^8$Be, $^{12}\mathrm{C}$, $^{20}\mathrm{Ne}$, and $^{48}$Ca. In this many-body approach, deformation and collectivity naturally emerge from the underlying chiral potential, and we show that the Sp(3,$\mathbb{R}$) basis concentrates the ground state in far fewer basis states than the SU(3) basis. We compute single-mode entropies and two-mode mutual information, and examine the organization of like-particle two-nucleon correlations into symmetry channels. Single-mode entropies are largest in the valence shells, while two-mode mutual information reveals substantial correlations both within and between shells. In all cases considered, the dominant pair correlations are captured by fewer SU(3) channels than angular-momentum channels, which reduces the number of variational parameters. Since the number of operator components within the channels is comparable in the two schemes for the heavier nuclei, this gives the SU(3)-coupling scheme a clear advantage. Fiedler ordering of the correlation graph with local refinement reduces the register ordering cost by a factor of 2 to 3 relative to the original shell ordering, and by 10 when two more shells are included in the register. Together, these measures link nuclear correlations to the selection of active modes and candidate excitation operators and to the design of quantum register layouts, offering guidance for the development of hybrid quantum-classical calculations of nuclei.
