Transition-Metal Substitutions in MOF-5: A Hybrid-DFT Study of Oxygen Electrocatalysis

Pedro H. Souza, Walter Orellana

arXiv:2611.27037·cond-mat.mtrl-sci·Published 2026-11-20

Hybrid density functional theory calculations were used to investigate the stability, electronic properties, and catalytic activity of transition-metal impurities substituting Zn sites (M\(_{\rm Zn}\)) in the MOF-5 metal-organic framework [Zn\(_4\)O(BDC)\(_3\), BDC = 1,4-benzenedicarboxylate], with M = Sc, Ti, V, Cr, Mn, Fe, Co, Ni, and Cu. Most substitutions introduce \(3d\) states within the MOF-5 band gap and induce localized magnetic moments. Partially occupied \(3d\) states promote orbital hybridization and charge transfer with oxygenated intermediates, activating the dopant sites toward oxygen electrocatalysis. The oxygen reduction and evolution reaction (ORR and OER) activities were evaluated using the computational hydrogen electrode model. Mn\(_{\rm Zn}\) and Fe\(_{\rm Zn}\) exhibit calculated ORR overpotentials of 0.57~V, while Mn\(_{\rm Zn}\) and Co\(_{\rm Zn}\) yield OER overpotentials of 0.42 and 0.51~V, respectively, approaching those of benchmark precious-metal electrocatalysts. These findings identify Mn substitution as a promising strategy for achieving bifunctional ORR/OER activity in MOF-5 and establish a link between dopant electronic structure and catalytic performance to guide the design of MOF-based electrocatalysts.
