Stacking, symmetry and exciton fine structure in boron nitride bilayers

Mehdi Arfaoui, Sylvain Latil, Lorenzo Sponza

arXiv:2611.09473·cond-mat.mtrl-sci·Published 2026-11-06

Engineering of two-dimensional material heterostructures offers a broad range of degrees of freedom through the selection of type and number of layers and their relative stacking configurations. In these compound materials, the stacking sequence plays a central role in determining electronic, mechanical, and optical properties. Here, we present a theoretical study of its signature on the excitons of hexagonal boron nitride bilayers based on first-principle calculations within the GW approximation and the Bethe-Salpeter equation. We explain how stacking-dependent symmetry variations lead to distinct electronic structures, particularly near the band edge around the K high-symmetry point, and consequently to markedly different excitonic landscapes. Indeed, we analyze the symmetry-governed character of the low-lying excitonic states, identifying their bright or dark nature for each stacking configuration and performing a systematic comparison. Our results clarify how van der Waals interlayer coupling reshapes excitons in hexagonal boron nitride bilayers and outline prospects for twist-controlled exciton engineering in this material.
