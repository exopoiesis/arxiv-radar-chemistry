Scaling up spin complexity in ultracold assembled molecules: association and quantum chemistry of Cr-bearing diatomics

Beatrice Restivo, Stefano Finelli, Andrea Simoni, Andreas Trenkwalder, Matteo Zaccanti, Alessio Ciamei

arXiv:2611.12159·cond-mat.quant-gas·Published 2026-11-09

The availability of ever more atomic elements in the ultracold regime potentially enables the extension of molecule assembly well beyond bi-alkali systems to targeted chemical species for novel quantum science and technologies. The concurrent need for single-quantum-state control and molecular constants from \emph{ab initio} calculations, especially for previously unexplored species, creates a trade-off between complexity and feasibility. Here, we address this challenge and exploit inter-isotope Feshbach spectroscopy as a tool to study three chromium-bearing diatomics ($^6$Li$^X$Cr, $^{50,52}$Cr$_2$, and $^{53}$Cr$_2$) at different levels of complexity. The extracted molecular properties, including the number of vibrational levels and the dispersion coefficients, allow us to assess quantum chemistry methods. Moreover, our understanding of atomic scattering allows us to assemble LiCr and Cr$_2$ molecules and clarify the observed association pathways with quantum state selectivity. Our results suggest unique prospects for the Li-Cr and Cr-Cr systems, and, more importantly, the experimental viability of a wider class of assembled high-spin molecules for many-body physics and precision measurements.
