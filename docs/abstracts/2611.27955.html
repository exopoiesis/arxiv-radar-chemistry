Molecular structure breaks universality in screening behavior of electrolyte solutions

Anna T. Bui, Connie J. Fairchild, Dominic P. W. Thomas, Stephen J. Cox

arXiv:2611.27955·cond-mat.stat-mech·Published 2026-11-20

Electrostatic screening is one of the most fundamental phenomena in electrolyte solutions. While dilute electrolytes are described by the universal Debye--Hückel theory, recent experiments have reported an apparently universal scaling law for screening lengths in concentrated electrolytes, fueling ongoing debate over anomalous underscreening. Yet, liquid state theory has been unable to determine whether molecular details of the solvent, the ions, or both should preserve or break such universality. Using machine-learned classical density functional theory, we compute numerically exact decay lengths across a hierarchy of electrolyte models of increasing molecular complexity. We find that molecular details qualitatively reshape screening: solvent attraction generates a new monotonic decay mode, solvent polarity produces re-entrant behavior, and ionic structure alone can drastically alter screening-length exponents. Consequently, no single scaling law describes screening universally across these molecularly distinct electrolytes. Our results challenge equilibrium interpretations of experimentally observed universality in concentrated electrolytes.
