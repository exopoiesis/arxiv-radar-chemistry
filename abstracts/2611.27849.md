# Interaction and confinement effects for a ${}^6\mathrm{Li}_2$ condensate in a shaken lattice

**Authors:** Yang-Hao Lee, Duc Trong Le, John Karnosh, Colin V. Parker

A shaken optical lattice can form Floquet-engineered band structures. For a one-dimensional lattice, this method can hybridize the ground and first excited bands into a double-well dispersion. A molecular Bose-Einstein condensate (mBEC) loaded into this band bifurcates into two clusters at opposite quasimomenta, but short cluster lifetimes (2 ms to 3 ms) have limited earlier work. We improve our $^6$Li mBEC experiment by aligning the lattice axis with the trap and using a multifrequency shaking sequence to counterdiabatically load into the double-well minima. Together, these improvements allow us to extend the experiment timescale beyond 10 ms. At these longer times the momentum distribution depends qualitatively on the confinement. With stronger confinement in the lattice direction, there is a peak in the momentum distribution at the Brillouin zone edge. Shaking-frequency spectroscopy confirms this zone edge population and shows it splits roughly equally between the ground and first excited bands. The longer lifetime also lets us ramp the magnetic field from 650 G, where molecules interact weakly, to 800 G, near the Feshbach resonance. As the interaction strengthens, the bifurcation weakens and disappears. We suggest that the increased molecular size causes this change.
