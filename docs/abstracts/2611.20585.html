Scaling a Universal Graph Neural Network Force Field to Billions of Atoms on Desk-Side GPUs

Jaehyung Lee, Kamal Choudhary

arXiv:2611.20585·cond-mat.mtrl-sci·Published 2026-11-16

Machine-learning force fields are capable of simulating billions of atoms, but usually require high-performance supercomputing clusters. We show that a universal graph neural network force field, ALIGNN-FF, evaluates energies and forces of more than one billion atoms on desk-side computers such as the NVIDIA GB10, which has 128 GB of unified memory, by using a cube-and-halo decomposition. The halo contains every atom within the model's 20 Å receptive field, which makes the result exact. One GB10 holds 216,000 atoms with forces as a single graph. The cube-and-halo decomposition removes this limit, enabling an evaluation of 2 billion atoms on nine GB10s. We also demonstrate three case studies: a billion-atom polycrystal, a 102-million-atom microelectronic gate stack and a 1.07-million-atom satellite tobacco mosaic virus in water. This brings single-point evaluations at experimentally relevant sizes within reach of one research group. The code is available at: https://github.com/atomgptlab/scalingmat.
