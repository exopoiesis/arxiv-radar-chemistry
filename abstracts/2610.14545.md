# DeepTelecom: A Differentiable Multimodal Framework for Channel Twinning in Dynamic Heterogeneous Wireless Environments

**Authors:** Bohao Wang, Tianen Yuan, William Yen, Zehua Jiang, Chongwen Huang, Li Wei, Chen Zhu, Jiguang He, Hang Zou, Lina Bariah, Brahim Mefgouda, Anis Bara, Yongliang Shen, Zhaoyang Zhang, Merouane Debbah

Large-scale multimodal data are a key enabler for integrating large generative artificial intelligence models into wireless networks. However, existing wireless datasets offer limited support for heterogeneous environments, particularly in terms of reusable high-fidelity scenes, scalable differentiable ray tracing, and synchronized multimodal data. These limitations are more pronounced in mobile and aerial scenarios with time-varying environments and channels. To address these limitations, we propose DeepTelecom, a material-aware, high-fidelity multimodal channel dataset and reproducible generation framework for networks involving unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) and reconfigurable intelligent surfaces. DeepTelecom refines coarse indoor/outdoor reconstructions and uses a large language model to map segmented surfaces to frequency-dependent electromagnetic parameters, yielding material-aware Level of Detail 3 digital twins. Then, a differentiable GPU-accelerated Sionna ray tracer simulates propagation across the sub-6 GHz/millimeter-wave/sub-terahertz bands, and specifically captures rotor-induced micro-Doppler signatures of stationary or moving UAVs at different pitch angles and translational velocities. The framework synchronously produces channel tensors, coverage maps, transmission videos, multi-view images, and weather-conditioned renderings, and supports differentiable material calibration from sparse channel anchors. The released dataset contains 10 material-aware indoor/outdoor scenes and 50,000 synchronized UAV samples; the framework supports scalable generation of additional data for user-defined scenes and system configurations. In 3D UAV localization, DeepTelecom reduces the 90th-percentile error from 31.05 m to 2.43 m compared with schemes considering only channel state information, demonstrating its significance for artificial-intelligence-enabled wireless systems.
