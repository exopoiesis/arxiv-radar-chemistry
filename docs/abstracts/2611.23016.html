Ab initio Engineering of Topological Superconductivity in Substrate-Supported Magnetic Chains

András Lászlóffy, Balazs Ujfalussy

arXiv:2611.23016·cond-mat.supr-con·Published 2026-11-17

In this paper, we employ a combination of several first-principles-based approaches to systematically scan chains of several types of magnetic atoms in different chain geometries on top of various overlayers on the Nb(110) surface, to develop a pathway to engineer topological Majorana edge states. Using atomistic spin-model calculations, we first identify a rich variety of magnetic configurations, including ferromagnetic, antiferromagnetic, spin-spiral, and exotic up-up-down-down states stabilized solely within a second-order Heisenberg model. In the next step, we solve the Bogoliubov-de Gennes equations using a band-theoretical method. Depending on the magnetic structure and atomic configuration, the superconducting spectra may exhibit topologically trivial minigaps, broad overlapping Shiba bands, or localized zero-energy edge states accompanied by sizable topological minigaps. In particular, Fe chains on Ag/Nb(110) are found to display pronounced signatures associated with topological superconductivity, including strongly localized zero-energy edge states and indications of Shiba band inversion. We further demonstrate that changes in the spin structure or a small amount of spatial relaxation can substantially modify the superconducting band structure and the localization properties of the edge states.
