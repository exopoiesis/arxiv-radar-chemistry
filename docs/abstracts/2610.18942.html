A quantum-assisted latent surrogate model for Bayesian inversion of heterogeneous material properties on NISQ hardware

Dewangga Alfarisy, Catherine F. Higham, Eky Febrianto

arXiv:2610.18942·physics.comp-ph·Published 2026-10-13

Bayesian inversion is widely used in engineering applications, including material characterisation, structural damage identification, and medical diagnostics. These inverse problems are typically ill-posed, because localised variations in material properties must be inferred from noisy observations, and they are computationally intensive, since they often require many evaluations of a high-fidelity forward solver. Quantum computing has recently attracted considerable interest, partly because some near-term algorithms are feasible on NISQ hardware. In particular, PQC combined with classical optimisation enable a hybrid quantum-classical surrogate model. A central obstacle to applying such circuits to inversion is that the discretised parameter and response fields are far too high-dimensional to be embedded in a near-term quantum register. In this work we propose a quantum-assisted latent surrogate model for the Bayesian inversion of inhomogeneous material properties, in which latent compression bridges these high-dimensional fields and the small registers accessible to near-term circuits. The low intrinsic dimensional structure of the spatially varying conductivity and potential fields is exploited by reducing the conductivity through PCA and the response through a convolutional autoencoder. A PQC is then trained to map the latent conductivity to the latent response, and stochastic variational inference is performed entirely in latent space. The methodology is demonstrated on one- and two-dimensional inverse problems under both informative and non-informative priors. We further evaluate the model on IonQ simulator and Rigetti Cepheus-1-108Q, and use a lightweight learned denoising stage that recovers the noiseless accuracy to within a small residual.
