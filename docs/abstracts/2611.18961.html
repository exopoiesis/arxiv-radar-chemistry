Accelerating Atomistic-Continuum Simulations of Multilayer 2D Materials with Functional Principal Component Analysis-Based Machine Learning

Jonathan Oleson, Abhishek Keripale, Shashank Pathrudkar, Susanta Ghosh

arXiv:2611.18961·physics.comp-ph·Published 2026-11-13

Atomistic-continuum simulations of complex wrinkling deformations in large-area multilayer two-dimensional (2D) materials are computationally expensive. To accelerate these simulations, this work presents a machine-learning (ML) framework that first constructs a low-dimensional representation of the deformation field, then learns the deformation in this reduced space. Dimensionality reduction of deformed 2D membranes is challenging because of the extremely high dimensionality of discretized deformation fields, the need to preserve surface continuity and prescribed geometric constraints, the stochastic nature of wrinkling patterns, and the limited availability of training data. We address these challenges using two-dimensional functional principal component analysis (FPCA). The proposed FPCA representation reduces deformation fields containing millions of degrees of freedom to latent spaces with only tens of dimensions while preserving the continuity of the deformed surface. In addition, prescribed homogeneous boundary constraints are satisfied exactly through an appropriate choice of basis functions in the FPCA representation. A neural network is then trained to predict the principal components from the system and loading parameters. The resulting ML-predicted deformation fields are used as high-quality initial configurations for the atomistic-continuum solver, which subsequently performs energy minimization to obtain equilibrium solutions. The framework is demonstrated for multilayer graphene subjected to uniaxial compression, biaxial compression, and combined biaxial compression and simple shear. For the most computationally intensive large-strain cases, the proposed approach achieves speedups of at least 6.8 times while converging to final energies comparable to, and in some cases lower than, those obtained using conventional atomistic-continuum initialization.
