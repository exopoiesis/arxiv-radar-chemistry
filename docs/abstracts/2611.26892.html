Length and Sequence-Dependent Dipolar and Electronic Properties of $α$-helical Polypeptides

Faezeh Shabani, Florian Günther, Sibylle Gemming

arXiv:2611.26892·physics.chem-ph·Published 2026-11-19

Helical polypeptides combine the chemical tunability of synthetic macromolecules with the highly ordered backbone architecture of natural proteins. Here, density-functional tight-binding calculations are used to determine how chain length and sequence chemistry govern the dipolar and electronic properties of three $α$-helical polypeptide families: polyalanine [AAAAA]$_n$, leucine-modified [AAAAL]$_n$, and cysteine-capped, lysine-containing C[AAAAK]$_n$. Increasing chain length produces systematic shifts and splitting of the molecular orbital levels together with an approximately additive increase in the molecular dipole. Analysis of the C[AAAAK]$_3$ helix shows that aligned backbone carbonyl groups provide the dominant contribution to the macro dipole, whose growing electrostatic potential is consistent with an intrinsic Stark-like contribution to the length-dependent orbital-energy evolution. Side-chain and terminal modifications introduce localized perturbations to frontier electronic states while preserving this global backbone-driven trend. Residue-resolved frontier levels additionally form a staircase-like energy landscape along the helical scaffold: individual peptide units retain large local HOMO-LUMO gaps of approximately 5 eV, whereas spatially proximate hydrogen-bonded residues exhibit smaller inter-residue energy separations. Application of external electrostatic fields modifies the frontier-level separation in a direction-dependent manner and induces structural distortions predominantly near the flexible termini, while the helical core remains comparatively robust. By establishing these fundamental sequence-structure-property relationships, this work provides a foundational blueprint for the rational design of peptide-based biomacromolecules tailored for next-generation bio-electronic interfaces, biosensors, and artificial electron-transfer systems.
