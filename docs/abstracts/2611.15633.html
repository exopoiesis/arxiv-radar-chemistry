Open Quantum System Embedded in an Optimized Reservoir of Molecules

Somayeh Ahmadkhani, Sara Panahian Jand, Luigi Delle Site

arXiv:2611.15633·physics.chem-ph·Published 2026-11-11

Open-boundary Quantum Mechanics/Molecular Mechanics (QM/MM) simulation provides a direct way to test whether a quantum subsystem and its surrounding classical reservoir are physically consistent. Here we use this framework to replace a standard flexible TIP3P reservoir by a dipole-renormalized (DR) Amber-compatible three-site water model optimized against a full \textit{ab initio} trajectory of liquid water. The renormalization targets the molecular dipole distribution and adjusts the atomic charges, bonded, and Lennard-Jones interaction parameters through a stochastic optimization procedure. In the case of a large QM region ($\langle N\rangle\approx 440$), where the surface-to-volume ratio is small, the DR reservoir induces, as expected, Grand Canonical statistics for the quantum subsystem. In smaller subsystems ($\langle N\rangle\approx 190$), where the surface-to-volume condition is deliberately less favorable, the same reservoir shows substantially improved molecular consistency with the QM region relative to the non-renormalized flexible TIP3P model. These results show that quantum information at molecular dipole level is crucial for the microscopic physics of liquid water. When this concept is transferred into a classical reservoir, it can be used to extend the reliable regime of adaptive open-QM/MM water simulations even beyond the physically dictated Grand Canonical conditions. \end{abstract
