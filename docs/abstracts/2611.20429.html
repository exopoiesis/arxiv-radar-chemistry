FiuMD: Heterogeneous Batching for Coarse-Grained Molecular Dynamics with Neural Network Potentials

Hugo D. Hernández R., Paola B. Torres, Lara Negri, Rocío L. Cecchini, Nicolás A. García

arXiv:2611.20429·physics.comp-ph·Published 2026-11-16

FiuMD is a GPU molecular dynamics engine that advances many independent biomolecular simulations as a single batch, even when the systems differ in size, topology, force-field parameters, periodic box, timestep, or thermostat parameters. All systems are concatenated into global tensors, so the classical interaction terms and a shared TorchMD-Net neural network potential are evaluated for the whole batch at once, while each system keeps its own integration and thermostat. FiuMD started as a fork of TorchMD and reworks its system representation, force evaluation, and integration to handle such heterogeneous batches. We test it with coarse-grained C$α$ models of chignolin, Trp-cage, BBL, and $λ$-repressor. In double precision, forces within a heterogeneous batch agree with isolated FiuMD and TorchMD evaluations to within the precision of repeated evaluations, with a maximum relative difference of $7\times10^{-16}$ across 60 systems. Over 1000 steps without a thermostat, batched trajectories deviate by at most $2.5\times10^{-11}$ Angstrom from isolated ones, even when periodic images lie within the neural network cutoff. With a different target temperature for each system of the ensemble, the mean and width of each system's kinetic temperature match the values expected from its own thermostat. We show that when individual simulations are too small to fill the GPU, batching can improve throughput several-fold over TorchMD. On a single GPU, batching four different protein systems achieves a 4.6-7.8-fold speedup per step compared with sequential TorchMD simulations. Compared with sequential homogeneous FiuMD batches, heterogeneous batching provides up to a 4-fold speedup for smaller batches while maintaining comparable performance for larger ones.
