# Higher-Group Statistics on Lattices

**Authors:** Chen Yang, Yu-An Chen

Crossing a domain wall can permute anyons, and intersecting strings can create a particle. These couplings between defects of various dimensions are specified by higher groups. We develop a lattice framework defining higher-group statistics as Berry phases of closed sequences of hopping operators, invariant under local modifications of the operators. Our central example is a closed Majorana chain, a 1-dimensional topological superconductor, embedded in 3 dimensions. We construct a loop-flip process with phase $+i$, refining the usual $\pm 1$ loop-flip invariant through intermediate transfer of fermion parity between the chain and physical fermions. In two dimensions, charge-wall statistics distinguish two $\mathbb Z_4$ toric codes enriched by $\mathbb Z_2$ charge-conjugation symmetry, with identical anyon statistics but distinct gauged theories: the quantum doubles of the dihedral group $D_4$ and the quaternion group $Q_8$. We provide two complementary constructions of statistical processes. The first extends the Smith normal form algorithm in [Phys. Rev. X $\textbf{16}$, 011010 (2026), arXiv:2412.01886] to higher groups, generating processes from lattice geometry and prescribed local moves. For a finite higher group $\mathbb G$, we prove that statistical processes on a $d$-sphere are classified by $H_{d+2}(B\mathbb G,\mathbb Z)$. The second constructs processes directly from homology classes without examining exponentially many defect configurations, enabling us to study statistics for complex higher-group structures where the previous method is computationally prohibitive. These processes detect boundary anomalies of higher-group symmetry-protected topological phases. For example, we distinguish 2-group anomalies in 1, 2, and 3 dimensions. Higher-group statistics provide new invariants for characterizing coupled defects in quantum many-body systems.
