# Automatic dual basis sets for self-consistent field calculations

**Authors:** Joonatan Huhtasalo, Susi Lehtola

Density functional theory (DFT) has long been the workhorse of electronic structure theory, and it is experiencing a new boom thanks to big data applications and machine learning. A DFT calculation requires the iterative solution of the self-consistent field (SCF) problem, whose cost is the product of (i) the number of SCF steps and (ii) the cost of a single step. The number of SCF steps depends strongly on the initial guess, regardless of the SCF algorithm, and quantitative DFT accuracy is known to require at least a polarized triple-$ζ$ basis set. However, a qualitatively correct solution may often be found already in a smaller \emph{dual basis} (DB), which can then bootstrap a calculation in the full basis set. Traditionally, the DB is chosen by hand for each application to enable robust convergence to the ground state. In this work, we pursue automatic generation of DBs for SCF calculations. Our generator constructs a pruned orbital basis from the target orbital basis for a predefined precision threshold and a given Fock matrix. As expected, the larger the basis set is, the smaller the fraction of kept basis functions. The DBs produced by our algorithm exhibit similar performance for a wide range of systems from the first 4 periods of the periodic table. Ultimately, we find that presently available initial guesses for the Fock matrix are not accurate enough to unlock the use of adaptive basis sets, and believe that the same limitation may hold for similar attempts for basis compactification.
