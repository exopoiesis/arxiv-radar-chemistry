2D coherent spectroscopy of topological insulators

Thaís V. Trevisan, Michał Papaj

arXiv:2611.10905·cond-mat.mes-hall·Published 2026-11-07

Multidimensional coherent spectroscopy probes the response of a system to a sequence of time-separated light pulses, and has attracted significant interest in recent years. In the frequency space, it generates multidimensional spectra, in which information about different degrees of freedom of the system is generically disentangled. In this work, we develop a quantum geometric formulation of multidimensional optical susceptibilities in insulating systems. Focusing on a two-pulse setup and working in the velocity gauge, we derive analytical expressions for second-order and third-order two-dimensional coherent spectra (2DCS). We focus on the third-order ABB response, which remains finite in centrosymmetric systems where the second-order susceptibility vanishes by inversion symmetry. Its paramagnetic contribution supports an echo signal that probes products of four interband Berry-connection matrix elements, which reduce in two-band systems to bilinear combinations of the quantum geometric tensor of the occupied band. Using continuum and square-lattice models of two-dimensional Chern insulators, we show how the echo resolves the momentum-space sign structure of these geometric quantities. For the $xxyx$ response of the models considered, the number of nodes in the echo signal is related to the band topology. Our results extend the quantum-geometric interpretation of multidimensional spectroscopy to higher-order responses, showing that third-order 2DCS provides access to geometric structures beyond those probed at lower orders.
