Real-time dynamics with bead-Fourier path integrals. III. Atomistic condensed-phase simulations

Nathan London, Mohammad R. Momeni

arXiv:2611.18749·physics.chem-ph·Published 2026-11-11

Accurately accounting for nuclear quantum effects (NQEs), including zero-point energy and nuclear tunneling, is essential for a reliable prediction of the structural, thermodynamic, and dynamical properties of chemical systems, particularly those involving light atoms. However, incorporating NQEs could render simulations of large-scale chemical systems computationally prohibitive, particularly when combined with expensive electronic-structure calculations. One potential avenue for reducing cost is to use bead-Fourier path integrals (BF-PIs). In Paper I, we introduced the BF centroid molecular dynamics (BF-CMD) method, in which BF-PIs were used to compute an effective potential for CMD, and in Paper II, we introduced BF ring polymer MD (BF-RPMD), taking the BF-PI and using it for approximate real-time dynamics. Both papers featured a series of model systems and were able to reduce the number of beads needed to converge dynamical properties by a factor of 2 to 4, depending on the system. In this work, we report the first implementation and application of the BF-PI framework to atomistic simulations using liquid bulk water and hexagonal ice as test cases. As in our previous papers, we find that adding just a single Fourier component to the path yields a 4- and 8-fold reduction in the number of beads while achieving results that agree with reference PI simulations for liquid water and ice, respectively. We also demonstrate that we can reduce simulation costs by up to 5.5x compared to standard PIs by reducing the number of force calculations and normal-mode transformations.
