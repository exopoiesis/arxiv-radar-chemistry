The phase diagram of fluid hydrogen near melting from machine-learned interatomic potentials trained on quantum Monte Carlo

Shubhang Goswami, Hongwei Niu, Ilnur Saitov, Mathieu Istas, Yubo Yang, Scott Jensen, Markus Holzmann, Carlo Pierleoni, David Ceperley

arXiv:2611.25178·cond-mat.str-el·Published 2026-11-18·Updated 2026-11-22

Dissociation of molecular hydrogen increasing temperature or pressure may either occur gradually or abruptly, via a phase transition. Despite considerable experimental and theoretical efforts, the phase diagram of dense hydrogen at temperatures above solid melting is not precisely known. Here, we perform classical and quantum Path Integral molecular dynamics simulations of dense hydrogen using two machine-learned interatomic potentials both trained on quantum Monte Carlo energies and forces. The resulting phase diagram is constructed using state-of-the-art techniques. Both models show a first-order phase transition from the molecular to the atomic liquid terminating in critical points whose locations were determined using finite-size scaling techniques. Triple points, located at the highest temperature of the melting curve, are found at the intersection of the liquid-liquid lines with the melting curve and should lead to clear signatures in ramp compression experiments.
