# How anion composition governs structural frameworks and Li-ion transport in oxychloride solid-state electrolytes

**Authors:** Jiho Lee, Seungwoo Hwang, Seungwu Han, Jisu Jung, Youngho Kang, Sungwoo Kang

Mixed-anion lithium oxychlorides exhibit high Li conductivity, but the relationship between oxygen content, structural framework, and Li-ion transport remains unclear. Here, crystal structure prediction and molecular dynamics simulations with machine-learning interatomic potentials are used to examine $\mathrm{Li}_yM\mathrm{O}_x\mathrm{Cl}_{6-2x}$ ($M$ = Nb and Ta [Group 5] and $M$ = Ti, Zr, and Hf [Group 4]). Increasing oxygen content changes isolated chloride-rich polyhedra into one-dimensional chains and subsequently more highly connected two- and three-dimensional frameworks. In the Group 5 series, fast Li diffusion occurs over $x \approx 0.67$--1.5, where the Li sites form a three-dimensional percolating network. Potential of mean force maps and nudged elastic band calculations reveal that fast Li-ion transport requires a low-dimensional framework supporting long-range migration through undercoordinated Li sites with low barriers, together with similar energies across Li sites. Finally, we perform fine-grid compositional search, which identifies $\mathrm{LiNbO}_{0.67}\mathrm{Cl}_{4.67}$, $\mathrm{LiTaO}_{0.75}\mathrm{Cl}_{4.5}$, $\mathrm{LiNbO}_{1.5}\mathrm{Cl}_3$, and $\mathrm{LiTaO}_{1.5}\mathrm{Cl}_3$ as stable candidates with predicted room-temperature self-diffusivities of up to $\sim 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{cm}^2/\mathrm{s}$. These findings establish how oxygen content controls framework and Li-ion transport, guiding the design of lithium oxyhalide electrolytes.
