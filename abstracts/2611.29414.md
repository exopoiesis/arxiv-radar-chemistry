# Nonempirical Determination of the Optimal TAO-DFT Fictitious Temperature for Molecular Systems in the Lowest Singlet State

**Authors:** Bing-Ying Li, Jeng-Da Chai

Thermally-assisted-occupation density functional theory (TAO-DFT) [J.-D. Chai, J. Chem. Phys., 2012, ${\bf 136}$, 154104] has been developed to investigate the ground-state properties of large electronic systems exhibiting varying multi-reference (MR) character. As a central parameter in TAO-DFT, the fictitious temperature $θ$ is closely linked to the degree of static correlation in an electronic system. While the conventional system-independent $θ$-scheme is computationally efficient and broadly applicable, a fixed, preassigned $θ$ can be overly large for single-reference (SR) systems yet too small for strongly MR systems. To overcome this limitation, we propose a nonempirical scheme based on spin-symmetry restoration to determine the optimal system-dependent fictitious temperature $θ^{*}$ for molecular systems in their lowest singlet states. Across benchmark tests encompassing both MR systems (the bond dissociation of H$_{2}$, N$_{2}$, Li$_{2}$, and LiH, as well as the torsional rotation of twisted ethylene) and pure SR systems (the dissociation of He$_{2}$ and Ne$_{2}$), our nonempirical approach demonstrates superior overall accuracy compared with the conventional fixed-$θ$ scheme. Furthermore, relative to standard Kohn-Sham density functional theory (TAO-DFT with $θ= 0$), TAO-DFT employing $θ^{*}$ yields substantial improvements for MR systems while preserving comparable accuracy in SR systems.
