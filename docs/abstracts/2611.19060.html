Interplay of interfacial energetics and selective Ni segregation in the morphology of co-precipitates in low-alloy steels

Jia-Hong Ke, Anshul Kamboj, Mukesh Bachhav

arXiv:2611.19060·cond-mat.mtrl-sci·Published 2026-11-14

Coupled precipitation and interfacial segregation in low-alloy steels change precipitate stability and morphology, which influence strengthening and embrittlement. Cu-rich precipitates (CRPs) and Mn-Ni-Si-rich precipitates (MNSPs) frequently form core-shell or appendage morphologies, but the interfacial energetics controlling this change are not well quantified. We combine a density-functional-theory-trained cluster expansion with lattice Monte Carlo simulations to evaluate the Fe/CRP, Fe/MNSP and Cu/MNSP interfaces within one Hamiltonian. The three coefficients based on excess internal energy follow gamma_Cu/MNSP < gamma_Fe/CRP <~ gamma_Fe/MNSP and give a positive work of adhesion and a negative spreading parameter, consistent with favorable Mn-Ni-Si attachment to a CRP and partial coverage. Only Ni segregates to the Fe/CRP boundary. Mn and Si segregate weakly on their own but become more enriched when Ni is present and co-segregate with Ni at the boundary. Ni forms an interfacial layer about one monolayer thick with an excess of 7.4-9.6 atoms/nm^2, develops B2-like local order and shows an energy reduction that increases with Ni coverage. At 1 at.% matrix Ni, a Gibbs adsorption analysis gives a Fe/CRP free-energy reduction of about 0.15 J/m^2. These interface coefficients and segregation trends connect segregation, interface energy and co-precipitation and support compositional optimization in low-alloy steels.
