# Cascading LO-phonon-mediated resonant energy transfer from excitons to Eu3+ for ultrahigh color purity red luminescence in europium-doped GaN

**Authors:** Shizhao Fan, Yue Xu, Yuhao Yin, Shitao Dong, Shaoqiang Chen, Xueyuan Chen, Zhan Su, Datao Tu, Qian Sun, Hui Yang

High-efficiency, wavelength-stable red micro-LEDs with ultrahigh color purity remain a critical bottleneck for monolithic III-nitride full-color displays. Europium-doped GaN (GaN:Eu) offers a compelling solution, yet the microscopic energy transfer mechanism from the GaN host to Eu3+ ions has remained unresolved for three decades, impeding rational performance optimization. Here we report polaron-mediated resonant energy transfer in high-crystalline-quality GaN:Eu epilayers grown by plasma-assisted molecular beam epitaxy. Eu3+ doping stabilizes a distinct bound exciton (BX3) state at 3.478 eV, which couples strongly with longitudinal optical (LO) phonons via the Frohlich interaction to form cascading polarons with up to seven phonon replicas at a uniform energy spacing of 92.0+/-0.7 meV. The BX3 polaron coupled with 5LO phonons matches precisely the 7F0-->5D3 intra-4f transition energy of Eu3+ at 3.019 eV. Temperature-dependent photoluminescence reveals non-equilibrium phonon emission, evidencing directional resonant energy transfer. After rapid nonradiative relaxation from the 5D3 excited state to the 5D0 metastable state, the hypersensitive 5D0-->7F2 transition yields sharp 622 nm red emission. Time-resolved spectroscopy confirms a single dominant Eu3+ luminescent site with reduced local symmetry, attributed to off-axis EuGa-VN complexes. This work identifies a key energy transfer channel and provides a clear framework for designing high-efficiency GaN:Eu red emitter.
