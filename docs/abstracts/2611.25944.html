Alloying effects on the interface cohesion of grain boundaries in the Ni--Fe--Cr system

Aljoscha Felix Baumann, Reyhaneh Ghassemizadeh, Daniel F. Urban

arXiv:2611.25944·cond-mat.mtrl-sci·Published 2026-11-19

Grain boundaries (GBs) in polycrystalline Ni-based alloys strongly influence mechanical strength and fracture behavior, yet the effect of alloying elements such as Fe and Cr on GB cohesion remains difficult to quantify systematically. Using density functional theory (DFT), we compute rigid-body separation (RBS) curves for six symmetric-tilt Ni GBs, extracting the work of separation $W_{\mathrm{sep}}$ and maximum traction $σ_{\max}$ via the universal binding energy relation (UBER). We find that substitution of Cr or Fe into the GB plane increases both quantities relative to pure Ni GBs. To overcome the size limitations of DFT, we compare several machine-learning interatomic potential (MLIP) foundation models against the DFT dataset and identify a model achieving quantitative agreement with DFT for $W_{\mathrm{sep}}$ and $σ_{\max}$. This MLIP enables systematic sampling of alloying configurations at finer concentration steps. We show that alloying-induced changes in GB cohesion depend on both, element type and local structures, revealing trends that are inaccessible with DFT alone. Extending the analysis to a ternary $\mathrm{Ni}_{56}\mathrm{Fe}_{20}\mathrm{Cr}_{24}$ composition, inspired by the principal constituents of the widely used commercial Alloy 718, shows that the binary trends provide a useful basis for interpreting the combined effect of both substitutes, while explicit ternary calculations are required to capture their interplay quantitatively. Together, these results provide a validated workflow for quantifying the effect of alloying elements on GB cohesion in multicomponent alloys. The resulting values may serve as input for continuum-scale fracture models, paving the way toward atomistically informed multiscale modeling.
