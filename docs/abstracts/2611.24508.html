Solid-state formation of H$_2$CS and CH$_3$SH in molecular clouds: Carbon-initiated reactions with atomic and molecular hydrogen

F. Kruczkiewicz, J. C. Santos, G. Fedoseev, S. Ioppolo, C. M. R. Rocha, K. -J. Chuang

arXiv:2611.24508·astro-ph.GA·Published 2026-11-18

The solid-state formation pathways of thioformaldehyde (H$_2$CS) and methanethiol (CH$_3$SH) in dense molecular clouds remain poorly constrained. We investigate carbon-initiated reactions in hydrogen sulfide (H$_2$S) ice in the presence of atomic or molecular hydrogen. We co-deposited H$_2$S, ground-state atomic carbon, and H or H$_2$ at 10 K under ultra-high vacuum. We monitored the reaction products using reflection absorption infrared spectroscopy (RAIRS) and characterized their thermal desorption using temperature-programmed desorption (TPD) with quadrupole mass spectrometry. Gas-phase ab initio calculations were used to assess pathways to CH$_3$SH formation. Atomic carbon reacted with H$_2$S at 10 K to form H$_2$CS, detected as both monomers and H$_2$CS-derived oligomers. With atomic H, successive H addition to H$_2$CS leads to CH$_3$SH. With molecular H$_2$, CH$_3$SH also forms efficiently. The results support an alternative pathway in which C reacts with H$_2$ to form CH$_2$, which abstracts H from H$_2$S. The resulting CH$_3$ and SH radicals can then recombine to form CH$_3$SH. The infrared bands and TPD fragmentation patterns support the formation of H$_2$CS and CH$_3$SH in the ice at 10 K. These findings expand the set of viable solid-state pathways to sulfur-bearing molecules and constrain models of sulfur chemistry in prestellar environments.
