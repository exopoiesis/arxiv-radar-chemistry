Self-Assembled Triangular Sc-rich Nanodomains: The Orign of Anomalous Alloy Scattering in ScAlN/GaN Heterostructures

Yuhao Yin, Shizhao Fan

arXiv:2611.33868·cond-mat.mtrl-sci·Published 2026-11-25

Wurtzite scandium aluminium nitride (ScAlN) alloys hold substantial potential for GaN heterostructure high-electron mobility transistors, owing to their large spontaneous polarization gradient and the ability to achieve precise lattice matching at heterointerface. Nevertheless, despite continuous advances in epitaxy and device fabrication, the two-dimensional electron gas (2DEG) mobility in ScAlN/GaN heterostructures remains persistently limited, and the underlying microscopic origin remains elusive. Here, we uncover an anomalous electron scattering mechanism from laterally correlated alloy disorder that spontaneously develops within the ScAlN barrier driven by strain-field undulations imposed by threading dislocations. Dislocation strain field partitions the growth front into nanoscale domains with distinct strain states, driving anisotropic diffusion of Sc adatoms and yielding crystallographically oriented triangular Sc-rich nanodomains. Reducing the threading dislocation density from 2.8E10 to 4.4E8 cm^-2 suppresses areal density of nanodomains by more than two orders of magnitude. As a result, low-temperature Hall mobility increases from 1800 to 11995 cm^2*V^-1*s^-1. These findings establish dislocation-mediated Sc-rich nanodomains as the dominant source of anomalously strong alloy scattering, resolving the mobility bottleneck for high-performance nitride electronics.
