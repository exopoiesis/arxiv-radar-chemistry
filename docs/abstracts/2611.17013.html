DiffSSMEye: Conditional High-Frequency Eye Movement Synthesis with Structured State-Space Diffusion

Kamrul Hasan, Mehedi Hasan Raju, Oleg V. Komogortsev

arXiv:2611.17013·cs.HC·Published 2026-11-12

Synthesizing high-frequency eye movements requires preserving long-range temporal dependencies and subject-specific oculomotor dynamics. However, methods based on generative adversarial networks (GANs) and variational autoencoders (VAEs) can struggle to preserve these characteristics simultaneously. We introduce DiffSSMEye, a conditional denoising diffusion probabilistic model with bidirectional Structured State Space Sequence (S4) layers for generating five-second gaze-velocity sequences at 1,000 Hz. Generation is conditioned on a coarse gaze reference, user identity, and task, where identity and task embeddings are incorporated through feature-wise linear modulation. Identity-preservation, multi-resolution spectral, and integrated-drift losses complement noise prediction to constrain biometric consistency, frequency structure, and displacement error. Subject-disjoint evaluation on GazeBase across seven tasks yields mean real--synthetic embedding cosine similarities of 0.91--0.95. DiffSSMEye also achieves the lowest median spatial errors among these baselines for horizontal and random saccade tasks. These results demonstrate improved spatial accuracy and biometric feature consistency for subject-specific gaze synthesis.
