Spatiotemporal Energy Consistency Enables Reliable Coarse-Grained Molecular Dynamics from Diffusion Models

Yang Nie, Lingyun Wang, He Zhang, Chang Liu, Dan Hu

arXiv:2611.10517·physics.chem-ph·Published 2026-11-07

Coarse-grained molecular dynamics (CGMD) extends molecular simulations to length and time scales inaccessible to atomistic models, but building thermodynamically faithful CG potentials remains a longstanding challenge. Energy-based diffusion models (EBDMs) provide a promising route for learning CG potentials through their zero-time energy. However, CGMD driven by diffusion-learned potentials can produce equilibrium ensembles that deviate substantially from the target distribution. We show that the zero-time distribution error is bounded by the sum of the spatial score error and temporal energy-derivative error, whereas conventional diffusion objectives directly supervise only the spatial component. Based on this finding, we introduce Spatiotemporal Energy Consistency (STEC) supervision, which complements spatial score matching by explicitly constraining temporal energy derivatives. We derive an exact and tractable objective for this temporal component, avoiding the substantial computational overhead associated with previous regularization schemes. Across peptide and fast-folding protein systems, STEC-trained EBDMs accurately reproduce equilibrium distributions with free-energy errors within chemical accuracy, while supporting stable long-time simulations that recover key metastable transition patterns. On held-out octapeptides, STEC reduces free-energy errors by more than 5-fold relative to the strongest competing baseline, while achieving a median production-cost reduction of approximately 1,700-fold relative to atomistic molecular dynamics under a defined structural-coverage benchmark. These results establish STEC as a scalable framework for learning thermodynamically reliable CG force fields for increasingly complex biomolecular systems.
