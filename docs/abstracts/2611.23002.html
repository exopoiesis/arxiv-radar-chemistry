Indentation of pre-tensioned cortex-like fibre networks: tension, local stretching and the signature of defect architecture

Shubham Agarwal, Olivier Noël, Martin H. Müser

arXiv:2611.23002·cond-mat.soft·Published 2026-11-17

Indentation is widely used to infer the mechanical state of biological materials like the actomyosin cortex. It reduces a single force-indentation curve to an apparent modulus. The cortex, however, is a pre-tensioned, heterogeneous fibre network. We show that this inference is ambiguous. The same amount of weakening can leave the indentation force unchanged or lower it tenfold, depending on where it lies relative to the probe. The force reflects how the probe couples to the tensioned, load-bearing structure, not the amount of weakening. We combine molecular dynamics simulations of a pressure-loaded, crosslinked fibre network indented by a rigid sphere with a continuum membrane solution. We represent the simulated force-depth response by a two-term fit, $F\propto aδ+bδ^{3/2}$, with $a$ reflecting the global tension, and $b$ the stiffening with depth. At a fixed fraction of weakened bonds, diffuse weakening lowers the force by a factor of about 1.5. A weak cluster beneath the probe lowers it 8-12-fold, because the slack cluster decouples the probe from the tension. When the clusters are displaced from the contact, the force returns to within 15% of the healthy value. Two opposite effects cancel. The clusters soften the whole layer, which lowers its tension and the force. But they also channel the load through the intact network beneath the probe, which stiffens it locally and raises the force. The shape of the curve retains what the force level hides. The coefficient $a$ reflects the softened layer when clusters beside the probe keep the force near the healthy value. The local exponent $α=\mathrm{d}\ln F/\mathrm{d}\lnδ$ increases with depth in every configuration except when a cluster lies beneath the probe, where the two-term form no longer applies. Detecting localised weakening requires spatial sampling and analysis of the curve shape, not a single fitted modulus.
