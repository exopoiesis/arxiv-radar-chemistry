# The effect of isotopically-enriched 70Ge on the Raman scattering in Cr2Ge2Te6

**Authors:** Grzegorz Krasucki, Katarzyna Olkowska-Pucko, Tomasz Woźniak, Mihai I. Sturza, Holger Kohlmann, Adam Babiński, Maciej R. Molas

The highest-energy A5g Raman-active vibrational mode in chromium germanium telluride (Cr2Ge2Te6, CGT) exhibits a unique lineshape comprising a series of remarkably narrow peaks. While this multiplet was attributed to the natural isotope distribution of germanium affecting Ge-Ge pseudo-molecular vibrations, direct evidence has been lacking. Here, we present a Raman spectroscopy study of CGT isotopically enriched with 70Ge to validate this hypothesis. Upon isotopic enrichment, both the E5g and A5g modes exhibit a pronounced blue-shift. At room temperature, the A5g peak undergoes significant narrowing, while low-temperature measurements reveal a single, well-defined line instead of the previously observed multiplet. These observations align precisely with density functional theory calculations, confirming that the multi-line profile originates from Ge isotope distribution. Our findings demonstrate that similar isotope-induced Raman splitting may occur in other structurally analogous layered tellurides containing Ge or Si.
