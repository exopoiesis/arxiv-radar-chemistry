Harmonic origin of local symmetry breaking in PbTe revealed using machine-learned potentials

Seongjoo Jung, Nicola A. Spaldin

arXiv:2611.12598·cond-mat.mtrl-sci·Published 2026-11-09

We use machine-learned interatomic potentials combined with higher-order interatomic force constant models to show that finite-range polar correlations in PbTe are driven by long-range harmonic interactions rather than lattice anharmonicity. Our molecular dynamics simulations reveal a spatial polar correlation range exceeding 50 Å at low temperatures and decreasing with increasing temperature. Selective interatomic force constant mapping demonstrates that crystal anharmonicity usually associated with the formation of polar nanoregions suppresses polar correlations. Instead, long-range harmonic interactions extending to 13 Å are necessary to reproduce the spatial coherence of local dipoles. In reciprocal space, these extended harmonic interactions correspond to high curvature of the transverse optical (TO) phonon branch at the $Γ$ point. Comparison with isostructural NaCl connects these extended interactions to local underscreening of predominantly Coulombic forces. Our results provide a key microscopic description of finite-range polar correlations and a framework for understanding local symmetry breaking in centrosymmetric materials.
