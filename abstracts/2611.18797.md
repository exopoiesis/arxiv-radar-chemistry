# A Practitioner's Guide to Materials Theory and Computation in the Age of Big Data and Artificial Intelligence

**Authors:** Jack Yang

Computational modelling has become a cornerstone of materials science, with advances in atomistic simulations, high-throughput computation, and artificial intelligence transforming how materials are understood, discovered, and designed. These developments build upon generations of theoretical innovations that have translated complex mathematical and physical principles into practical predictive tools. However, the growing diversity of computational methods presents challenges for practitioners seeking a coherent understanding of their theoretical foundations. Drawing upon the author's research experience, this monograph provides a practitioner-oriented, pedagogical overview of computational materials science, unified through the concepts of potential-energy landscapes and statistical sampling. It explores how these concepts underpin structural stability, phase behaviour, and electronic and magnetic properties, through a systematic treatment of empirical force fields, density-functional theory, many-body electronic-structure methods, statistical sampling, and property prediction. It further examines high-throughput workflows, materials databases, and the probabilistic foundations of artificial intelligence. Particular emphasis is placed on atomistic representations, graph neural networks, and generative models, highlighting the roles of symmetry, group theory, differential geometry, and stochastic processes in modern materials modelling. By connecting established physical theories with emerging data-driven approaches, this monograph offers a unified perspective on computational materials science and identifies opportunities and challenges toward physics-informed, efficient, and reliable materials discovery.
