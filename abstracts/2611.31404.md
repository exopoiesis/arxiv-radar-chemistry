# Three-phase contact lines as distinct molecular adsorption environments

**Authors:** Fabio Staniscia, Matej Kanduč

In wetting and multiphase systems, three-phase contact lines form where liquid, solid, and gas phases meet, creating effectively one-dimensional boundaries with distinct molecular environments. Despite their ubiquity in droplets, liquid menisci, capillary bridges, porous materials, and confined multiphase systems, molecular adsorption at these lower-dimensional boundaries has remained largely unexplored. Here, we use molecular dynamics simulations to show that the contact line on a hydrophobic substrate is not merely the geometric intersection of two interfaces but a distinct thermodynamic adsorption environment with a substantially stronger affinity for amphiphilic molecules than either neighboring interface. The investigated amphiphiles of different molecular structures exhibit a common scaling relation between line and surface adsorption in the dilute regime, while their behavior becomes molecule-specific at higher concentrations. Furthermore, contact-line affinity increases exponentially with alkyl-chain length, leading to crowding at progressively lower concentrations and making contact lines extraordinarily sensitive to trace amphiphilic impurities. Because line, surface, and bulk populations scale differently with system size, contact-line adsorption becomes increasingly important at small length scales, where it can substantially alter molecular partitioning. Contact-line enrichment is predicted to reduce the line tension by an amount comparable to its intrinsic magnitude, with potentially substantial consequences for heterogeneous nucleation. Our results establish molecular adsorption as a fundamental property of three-phase contact lines with implications for wetting and multiphase systems across length scales.
