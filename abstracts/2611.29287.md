# Individuality from information and intervention: lineages, separation and persistence in cellular automata

**Authors:** Konstantin Doubrovinski

We propose a notion of individuality for deterministic dynamical systems, developed for cellular automata, based on algorithmic information and interventions. A lineage is a sequence of regions of space-time whose contents determine each other up to a few bits, and into which perturbations of earlier regions are carried forward. Two exact copies of an object cannot be told apart by their contents; they are distinguished by their causal relations, since perturbations of one need not reach the other. Individuals are classes of lineages between which perturbations pass, and they form a genealogy with fission, fusion and reticulation. Chains of cheap steps define a directed bottleneck ultrametric, so the threshold yields a filtration and a persistence structure that grades individuality. No region is prescribed: the descendants of a region are the regions that can be located by a short program, recover its perturbations, are connected, and are of nearly minimal size. Considering all source regions at once gives a canonical nested structure, and an autonomy condition marks preferred scales. All notions are invariant under computable isomorphisms of the intervention structure and use no metric on space. We show that, up to constants, the interventional conditions are pointwise versions of known interventional Shannon quantities, while continuity of lineages is genuinely algorithmic: whether it localizes an object depends on the complexity of that object. We prove that neither ingredient suffices alone: systems with identical runs, and even identical dependence structure, can differ in their individuals. For the parity rule on the square lattice we prove that a parent of side q splits into four separated individuals by time q, that the split persists over a range of thresholds of order q^2, and that at large powers of two all descendant regions lie at the four fronts.
