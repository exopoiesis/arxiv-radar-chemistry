Equilibrium Elongation of Charged Nanoclusters

Efstratios M. Kritikos, William A. Goddard, Paul M. Bellan

arXiv:2611.17513·physics.plasm-ph·Published 2026-11-12

When internal electrostatic stresses from electron repulsions overcome surface tension, charged nanoclusters elongate. However, the exact shape they reach at equilibrium has been unknown. We present an analytical model for the equilibrium structure of charged ellipsoidal nanoclusters via a free energy analysis. Energy maps are constructed that determine the equilibrium states as a function of cluster size, net charge, surface tension, and local curvature. The model is compared with molecular dynamics simulations of ice nanoclusters having solvated electrons. Despite the discreteness of the simulated atoms and electrons, both the predictions of the continuum analytical model for elongation and the equilibrium elongated shape show good agreement. The model could therefore predict nanocluster shape evolution in astrophysical and laboratory complex plasmas and atmospheric aerosols.
