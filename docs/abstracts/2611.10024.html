Experimentally Grounded Machine Learning of PrecursorSurface-Reactant Chemistry in Atomic Layer Deposition

Bonwook Gu, Trinh Ngoc Le, Zunair Masroor, Minhyeok Lee, Han-Bo-Ram Lee

arXiv:2611.10024·physics.chem-ph·Published 2026-11-05

Whether a precursor-substrate-reactant combination yields an atomic layer deposition (ALD) process is still settled by trial-and-error experiments. Here we introduce AJIN (ALD Joint Interpretable Network), a machine-learning framework that learns this decision from experimental outcomes. We compiled 560 experimentally observed ALD and area-selective ALD outcomes spanning 76 precursors, 27 substrates, 20 reactants and 25-620 degrees Celsius. Following the ALD cycle, AJIN predicts adsorption from graph-neural-network embeddings of the precursor and substrate with temperature, and deposition by adding the reactant. The best model (CHGNet-MLP) reaches balanced accuracies of 79.2% for adsorption and 84.8% for deposition and transfers partially to unseen precursors. SHAP and ablation analyses show that substrate chemistry governs adsorption, whereas precursor, substrate and reactant all contribute to deposition. Tested against our laboratory's ALD experiments, AJIN correctly predicted 83% of new-substrate cases and 71% of new precursor-substrate adsorption cases, providing an experimentally grounded tool for prioritizing ALD and area-selective ALD chemistries.
