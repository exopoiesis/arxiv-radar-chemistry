The Rise of Zirconium Metal-Organic Polyhedra

Joohan Nam, Seohyeon Kim, Eunjie Ju, Shinhyeok Moon, Wonyoung Choe

arXiv:2611.18217·cond-mat.mtrl-sci·Published 2026-11-13

Zirconium metal-organic polyhedra (Zr-MOPs) assembled from cyclopentadienyl (Cp)-capped Zr$_3$ clusters and carboxylate linkers form a structurally diverse family. Which MOP forms depends on the connectivity of the Zr$_3$ node, the geometry of the linker, and assembly conditions. This review classifies Zr-MOPs into edge- and face-directed tetrahedra, a cube, and cocoons according to the connectivity and geometry of their building blocks. From reported structures, we map which linkers give which MOPs and examine how the Zr$_3$ cluster precursor and the reaction conditions decide between the tetrahedron and the cocoon when one linker can give both. We describe how hydrogen bonding and ionic association organize the MOPs into reticular nets and porous salts in the crystal. Together, these comparisons relate cage geometry to node-linker connectivity and crystal packing to the geometry and distribution of external interaction sites, providing a structural basis for designing Zr-MOPs and their supramolecular assemblies.
