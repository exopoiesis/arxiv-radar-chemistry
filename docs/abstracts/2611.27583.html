Edge-dislocation interactions with sub 3 nm vacancy and interstitial clusters in FeCr alloys: Mechanisms, Obstacle strength and Fate

Layatmika Sahoo, Suchandrima Das

arXiv:2611.27583·cond-mat.mtrl-sci·Published 2026-11-20

Fe-Cr alloys harden at irradiation doses where transmission electron microscopy (TEM) shows no defects, implicating defects too small to be resolved. Their obstacle strength is unknown, so hardening models ignore them and fit measured hardening to visible defects alone. Here, molecular dynamics drives an edge dislocation through relaxed 1-3 nm vacancy and mixed Fe-Cr interstitial clusters in Fe-3Cr, Fe-5Cr and Fe-10Cr at 300 K. Vacancy clusters relax into platelets whose strength rises with size to about 350 MPa at 2.9 nm, following strong-obstacle scaling independently of Cr content, and always survive dislocation passage. Interstitial clusters relax into $\frac{1}{2}\langle 111 \rangle$, mixed or $\langle 100 \rangle$ loops. They are mostly weaker than platelets of the same size, their strength varies up to fivefold with the local Cr arrangement, and most are absorbed. Only $\langle 100 \rangle$ loops in Fe-5Cr and Fe-10Cr often survive. During pinning, Cr collects at the junction between these loops and the dislocation; atom tracking for one loop shows that more than half comes from the loop itself. Surviving loops are not stronger than absorbed ones: strength and survival are thus distinct. Computed dispersed-barrier strengths ($α\approx$ 0.02-0.25) are upper bounds that lie below the $α$ values obtained in earlier studies by fitting hardening to TEM-visible loops. These earlier values are therefore inflated by TEM-invisible defects. Wherever small vacancy clusters form, they are more effective obstacles than interstitial clusters of the same size. These defect-specific strengths and fates provide a physical basis for models of low-dose hardening.
