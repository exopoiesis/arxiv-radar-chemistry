X-ray parametric down-conversion probes radiation-induced dynamics in hybrid perovskite MAPbBr3

S. Sofer, O. Sefi, G. J. Man, H. N. Barad, N. P. Jasti, D. Cahen, S. Shwartz

arXiv:2611.12233·physics.optics·Published 2026-11-09

We report minute-timescale time-resolved measurements of X-ray parametric down-conversion (PDC) of X-rays into ultraviolet photons in a MAPbBr3 single crystal under continuous irradiation. Despite the known radiation sensitivity of hybrid perovskites and the high photon flux required to detect the intrinsically weak PDC signal, the nonlinear response remains measurable throughout the irradiation sequence. Both PDC and Bragg diffraction exhibit a slow decay consistent with diffusion-driven damage dynamics on timescales reported for bromide-vacancy and proton migration. However, despite their similar decay rates, the PDC and Bragg signals evolve toward markedly different steady states, while an additional fast decay component is observed in the Bragg response but absent in the PDC signal. Density functional theory calculations identify N 2s states of the methylammonium cation as the most likely contributors to the measured PDC response and show that their Fourier component is only weakly affected by bromide-vacancy formation. These results establish nonlinear X-ray diffraction as a channel-selective probe of radiation-induced dynamics and demonstrate that radiation damage can couple differently to nonlinear and linear X-ray responses.
