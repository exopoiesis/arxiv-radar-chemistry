Decoding Composition-Structure-Performance Relations in Sodium Layered Oxide Positive Electrode Materials

Yunyeong Choi, Hasindhi H. Lokuge, Joshua D. Bocarsly, Pieremanuele Canepa

arXiv:2610.30591·cond-mat.mtrl-sci·Published 2026-10-22·Updated 2026-10-26

Layered sodium transition-metal (TM) oxides, Na$_x$TMO$_2$, are among the most promising sodium-ion positive electrode materials, yet their Na/vacancy orderings, layer stacking sequences, and cooperative Jahn--Teller distortions are known only for a few single-TM compounds. We compile the Na/vacancy orderings reported in earlier studies into a single library of 41 unique orderings and apply this uniformly to five single-TM and all ten Na$_x$TM$_{0.5}$TM$'_{0.5}$O$_2$ oxides (TM = Ti, Mn, Fe, Co, Ni), screening more than 120,000 structures with machine-learning interatomic potentials prior to 1,025 accurate first-principles calculations. The phase diagrams explain the observed O3/P3/O1 sequences and voltages, and the O3-to-P3 competition hinges on ordering energies of a few meV per atom that scalar descriptors, such as the cationic potential and the interlayer-slab distance, miss. Our extensive analysis reveals that the redox couples form a nearly partner-independent ladder, Ti$^{3+/4+}$ $<$ Mn$^{3+/4+}$ $<$ Co$^{3+/4+}$ $<$ Ni$^{2+/3+}$ $<$ Fe$^{3+/4+}$, explaining pristine charge transfer and the oxidation sequence. Van Vleck analysis of every TMO$_6$ octahedron reveals that TM mixing suppresses the cooperative Jahn--Teller distortions of Mn$^{3+}$ and Ni$^{3+}$ through charge transfer to Jahn--Teller-inactive states or dilution of Jahn--Teller-active centers. We formulate an energy-density-versus-strain map that yields a design rule: a high-voltage couple without Jahn--Teller strain, which Mn$^{4+}$/Ni$^{2+}$ charge transfer realizes in Na$_x$Mn$_{0.5}$Ni$_{0.5}$O$_2$, the most practical composition. We release the ordering-resolved dataset as a training set for subsequent training of accurate machine-learning interatomic potentials.
