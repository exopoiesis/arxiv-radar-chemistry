# Aptamers as a sandbox for lab-in-the-loop agentic molecular discovery

**Authors:** Zachary Cutts, John Coller, Rachel Chen, Hua Tang, Mathias Bellaiche, Ryder Easterlin, Dina Zverinski, Petar Sirkovic, Avinatan Hassidim, Yossi Matias, Pooja Rao, Annalisa Pawlosky

Despite breakthroughs in AI-driven molecular generation, existing discovery pipelines operate largely in silico, with wet-lab feedback reserved for validation rather than iterative improvement. Whether large language model (LLM)-based reasoning agents can accelerate de novo discovery by iterating with lab data remains unproven.   Here we use protein-binding aptamer design as a sandbox (1-2 week cycles, \$1-\$3 per candidate) to demonstrate a wet lab in a closed loop with AI systems. We tasked agentic pipelines with developing aptamer binders against $α$-thrombin and myoglobin across three design-test-improve cycles ($\sim$1,000 candidates per round; $\sim$4,400 constructs). Statistically significant, bead-corrected on-target hit rates among new candidates rose across rounds from 0.0% in Round 1 to 26.0% in Round 2 and 42.1% in Round 3 on $α$-thrombin (37.1% excluding literature motif descendants), and from 10.2% in Round 1 to 3.7% in Round 2 and 33.5% in Round 3 on myoglobin. Single-clone bead-binding titrations of eight full-length $α$-thrombin candidates confirmed concordance with pooled sequencing enrichment (Pearson $r = 0.90$). In these assays, literature-derived motifs engineered by the agents to reduce off-target reactivity in the pooled screen reached apparent affinities of $K_D = 24.6$--$133$ nM, whereas de novo scaffolds showed low-micromolar binding ($K_D > 1\ μ$M).   We also report how our pipelines used myoglobin binding data to exploit the G-quadruplex binder space, diagnose cross-reactivity as on-target specificity dropped from 75.0% to 19.1%, and recover by developing strategies that restored specificity to 72.4%. We demonstrate that empirical lab data is more valuable than existing computational proxies, showing how iterative experimental feedback enables AI agents to diagnose assay artifacts and refine molecular designs across rounds.
