Improved screening of doped ZrO$_2$ catalysts for CO$_2$ hydrogenation from graph-based machine learning

Karsten Walz Jensen, Mie Andersen, Raffaele Cheula

arXiv:2610.13795·cond-mat.mtrl-sci·Published 2026-10-09

Dopants can tune the activity of zirconia-based CO$_2$ hydrogenation catalysts through changes in adsorbate and transition-state (TS) energetics. Computing these quantities with density functional theory (DFT) becomes expensive when several dopants, reaction intermediates, and TSs must be screened. Here, we further develop the graph-based machine learning method Wasserstein Weisfeiler-Lehman Gaussian Process Regression (WWL-GPR): we introduce additional electronic-structure and local-environment features relevant for oxide surfaces and use the resulting model to predict adsorption and TS energies on ZrO$_2$(101) surfaces doped with Al$^{3+}$, Cd$^{2+}$, Ga$^{3+}$, In$^{3+}$, and Zn$^{2+}$. Adsorbate-surface structures are represented as attributed graphs that combine bonds connectivity and features. We benchmark WWL-GPR against thermodynamic scaling relations (TSR) for reaction intermediates and Bronsted-Evans-Polanyi relations (BEP) for TSs, and then propagate the predicted energies to microkinetic CH$_3$OH-yield estimates. WWL-GPR consistently improves the prediction of reaction intermediate energetics relative to linear scaling approaches and provides TS estimates that are competitive with BEP-based models. These improvements become especially relevant when the predicted energetics are propagated through the reaction network. Microkinetic calculations show that WWL-GPR-derived energies give CH$_3$OH yields closer to DFT-derived yields than the linear-relation workflow. Degree-of-rate-control analysis links the activity errors to dopant-dependent rate-controlling steps, including HCOO* formation, H$_2$COOH* dissociation, and H$_3$CO* formation. These results support WWL-GPR as a data-efficient surrogate for connecting atomistic energetics with catalyst-screening observables on doped oxide surfaces.
