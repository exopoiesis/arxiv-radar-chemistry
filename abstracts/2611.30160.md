# Generation and non-thermal annealing of dislocations and atomic-scale roughness by nonlinear surface acoustic waves

**Authors:** Yuan Xu, Maxim V. Shugaev, Leonid V. Zhigilei

Surface acoustic waves (SAWs) are widely used to probe near-surface material properties and microstructure, but their potential to drive permanent structural modification remains largely unexplored. Large-scale molecular dynamics simulations reveal the mechanisms of defect generation and evolution induced by nonlinear Rayleigh waves in Pt. Strong crystallographic dependence of the nonlinear evolution is observed. SAWs on Pt(111) undergo rapid nonlinear sharpening and shock formation, with localized elastic energy and increasing near-surface stresses, whereas much weaker sharpening on Pt(001) allows dissipation to compete effectively with shock formation. For sufficiently strong SAWs on Pt(111), Shockley partial dislocations are emitted from the surface when the resolved shear stress approaches the theoretical shear strength. The highly localized and rapidly changing stress field at the shock front activates multiple slip systems and promotes crossslip and reactions between Shockley partials, producing complex dislocation structures with sessile stair-rod segments. Subsequent SAW interactions activate dislocation sources on slip systems where the stress is insufficient for homogeneous nucleation, leading, in one case, to sequential emission of Shockley partials and formation of a subsurface twinned domain. Repeated SAW passages progressively reduce dislocation density, demonstrating SAW-driven nonthermal annealing. Dislocation interactions with the free surface generate atomic-scale steps, terraces, and other under-coordinated configurations, providing a microscopic pathway for permanent surface modification that may contribute to the acoustically enhanced catalytic activity observed on Pt surfaces. These results establish nonlinear SAWs as drivers of nonthermal defect generation, restructuring, and annealing, opening opportunities for acoustic processing of near-surface microstructure.
