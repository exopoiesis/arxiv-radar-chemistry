JCMT POL-2 observations of NGC 253: evidence for large-scale reordering of magnetic fields in giant molecular cloud complexes

Kate Pattle, Walter K. Gear

arXiv:2611.24584·astro-ph.GA·Published 2026-11-18

In this paper we present observations of the magnetic field of the starburst galaxy NGC 253 made in polarized 850 $μ$m dust emission using the POL-2 polarimeter on the James Clerk Maxwell Telescope (JCMT). While the magnetic field typically traces the galactic spiral structure, we see significant deviations in field direction associated with the presence of stellar and/or supernova feedback, with the mean plane-of-sky magnetic field direction offset 24$^{\circ}$ from the galactic plane. Polarization fraction is anticorrelated with total 850 $μ$m intensity, with a power-law index of 0.72$\pm$0.03. Using the Fasano-Franceschini test, we find that the positions of POL-2 detections and radio sources in the spiral arms are inconsistent with one another, indicative of depolarization resulting from magnetic fields being tangled on scales smaller than the 240 pc JCMT 850 $μ$m beam in sites of star formation and feedback. Moreover, in both the starburst superwind and in one star-forming clump in the spiral arms, we observe a magnetic field direction consistent with being directed out of the plane of the galaxy. We thus hypothesise that magnetic fields in the giant molecular clouds (GMCs) of NGC 253 are undergoing an evolutionary sequence from a relatively ordered field parallel to the spiral arms in the pre-star-formation gas, to a complex field caused by the interplay of turbulence, gravity and small-scale feedback in relatively young GMCs, to a field that is reordered out of the plane of the galaxy as large-scale HII region expansion reshapes the gas and dust of galactic spiral arms.
