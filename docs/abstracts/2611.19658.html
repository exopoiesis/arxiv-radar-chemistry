Mesoscale Neural Operators: Learning Nonlinear Constitutive Laws from Molecular Dynamics for Scalable Fracture Predictions

Yue Yu, Jihong Wang, Stewart Silling, Ziwei Yang

arXiv:2611.19658·cs.LG·Published 2026-11-14

Molecular dynamics (MD) simulations can capture the nanoscale physics of fracture at the atomistic level, but their computational cost prohibits predictions at the length scales and time scales needed for many applications. Machine learning approaches, on the other hand, have been proposed as heuristic surrogates for material fracture, yet they cannot guarantee the physical consistency of the predicted response. To address this shortcoming, we propose the mesoscale neural operator (MNO): a data-driven nonlocal constitutive model that is learned directly from MD loading and displacement data. After training, it can be used in continuum fracture simulations on much larger domains and grid sizes. The key idea of the MNO is to exploit the built-in characteristic length of a nonlocal model, called the horizon, to travel across scales. Physical consistency across scales is obtained by building two structural ingredients into the MNO: a normalized kernel that renders the strain energy density invariant under horizon rescaling, and a rescaled critical bond stretch that keeps the energy release rate consistent across scales. Through tests on synthetic data, we show that the MNO and its learning workflow indeed discover the nonlocal continuum multiscale model in the form of the nonlocal kernel and the nonlinear constitutive relation. We then apply the framework to coarse-grained MD data and find that in a test problem, the learned model matches the MD strain energy density and critical energy release rate within 3%. It also predicts the peak transmitted forces within 6% in out-of-distribution tension tests. As a further demonstration, the model is applied to a plate with 4000 times the area of the training specimens and containing an initial defect. In this problem, the model reproduces the main features of dynamic brittle fracture, including branch and cascading branch networks.
