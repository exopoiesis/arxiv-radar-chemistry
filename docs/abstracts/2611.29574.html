MagPot: A magnetoelastic potential generator for molecular dynamics simulations

P. Nieves, I. Korniienko, S. Arapan, A. Fraile, R. Iglesias, D. Legut

arXiv:2611.29574·cond-mat.mtrl-sci·Published 2026-11-22

Magnetoelastic phenomena originate from the coupling between atomic displacements and magnetic degrees of freedom, and their atomistic simulation requires interatomic models in which magnetic interactions depend explicitly on interatomic distance. This paper presents MagPot, a Python program that generates Bethe-Slater parameter sets for spin-lattice molecular dynamics simulations using the SPIN package of the Large-scale Atomic/Molecular Massively Parallel Simulator (LAMMPS). From a compact set of material properties, the program determines the radial functions describing Heisenberg exchange and the dipole and quadrupole contributions of the two-ion Néel interaction. Version 1.0 supports simple-cubic, body-centred-cubic and face-centred-cubic lattices with ferromagnetic and antiferromagnetic configurations. A central feature of MagPot is its modular architecture, which allows the magnetic contribution it generates to be combined additively with a broad range of compatible non-magnetic interatomic potentials. The parameterisation workflow is assessed through equation-of-state, Curie-temperature, volume-magnetostriction, pressure-dependent magnetocrystalline-anisotropy and anisotropic-magnetoelastic tests. By automating the construction of the magnetic contribution to the interatomic model, MagPot offers a straightforward and reproducible route from material properties to LAMMPS-ready magnetic interactions, facilitating large-scale atomistic simulations of magnetoelasticity and magnetostriction, as well as physical phenomena associated with magnon-phonon coupling.
