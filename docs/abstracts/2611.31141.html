Emulator-enabled calibration and uncertainty quantification of $β$-decay calculations within the energy density functional theory

Yukiya Saito, Ante Ravlić, Witold Nazarewicz

arXiv:2611.31141·nucl-th·Published 2026-11-23

Predictions of $β$-decay half-lives of neutron-rich nuclei and their uncertainties critically impact our understanding of the astrophysical rapid neutron capture process. To provide quantified predictions within nuclear energy density functional theory, as customarily done, we perform Bayesian calibrations of two coupling constants: the isoscalar proton-neutron pairing strength and the isovector spin-density coupling. We apply an E-optimal experimental design for choosing the calibration sets. We employ the proton-neutron finite-amplitude method with Skyrme energy density functionals using emulators of half-lives and Gamow--Teller resonance energies. The calculated $Q_β$ values are fine-tuned to experimental values. The contributions to the half-life uncertainty from the theory error, $Q_β$ values, and the parameter uncertainty are all accounted for in the Bayesian framework. Our predictions for the even-even $N=126$ isotones provide the shortest half-lives for neutron-rich nuclei among the existing theoretical models, which would affect the flow of heavy-element nucleosynthesis. The proposed framework allows quantified theoretical uncertainties to be propagated to astrophysical observables such as abundance patterns and electromagnetic emission.
