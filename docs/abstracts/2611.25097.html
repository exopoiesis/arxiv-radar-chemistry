Mixtures of a fluid with competing interactions and a simple solvent near a hard wall

Ivan Kravtsiv, Oksana Patsahan, Taras Patsahan

arXiv:2611.25097·cond-mat.soft·Published 2026-11-18

We study a binary mixture of soft particles near a planar surface. The main component interacts through a competing short-range attraction and long-range repulsion (SALR) potential, modelled by a sum of three Yukawa terms, while the solvent and the cross interactions are described by two Yukawa terms. Using a field-theoretical mean-field approach we derive the coupled equations for the density profiles of both species, formulate the contact theorem for the two-component system, and obtain closed-form profiles in the mesoscopically homogeneous region by linearizing the theory and solving the associated eigenvalue problem. Their amplitudes are fixed by exact boundary conditions following from the continuity of the Yukawa potentials at the wall, which supply as many relations as there are unknowns. Although these conditions are implicit in the derivation of the contact theorem, they have not previously been used to fix the amplitudes of the linearized profiles, and they bring those profiles into quantitative agreement with the nonlinear solution. The equations are solved numerically for a fluid confined by hard walls. The theory predicts mixing of the two species far from the surface at high temperatures, and their microphase separation into alternating layers upon cooling or upon increasing the density. The mean-field profiles agree well with molecular dynamics data once the mean-field overestimate of the onset temperature of microsegregation is taken into account, and the analytical ones reproduce them closely except in the immediate vicinity of the wall.
