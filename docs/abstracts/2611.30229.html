Accelerating interatomic potential development through student-driven exploration and reliability-aware distillation

Ruiqi Dong, Haotian Huang, Yubin Hu, Guixiang Zeng

arXiv:2611.30229·physics.chem-ph·Published 2026-11-23

Conventional two-stage distillation first establishes a teacher through teacher-driven configuration exploration, a costly first stage for complex materials, and then uses its predictions to supervise students, potentially propagating teacher-label errors. Here, we propose distillation-driven teacher-student co-evolution (DICE), which uses a fast student to accelerate configuration exploration and replaces the fixed teacher-to-student sequence with alternating student and teacher updates. A representation-based support-assessment algorithm routes high-support configurations to teacher labelling and selects low-support configurations for density functional theory (DFT) calculations to update the teacher, thereby reducing the risk of teacher-label error propagation to students. Across Al2O3 (structural ceramics), yttria-stabilised zirconia (solid electrolytes) and MIL-120(Al) (a metal-organic framework), DICE develops reliable compact potentials using only 140-494 target DFT reference configurations per system; total recorded student exploration times per system are just 2.6-5.1% of the corresponding teacher-driven estimates. Combining computational efficiency with broad applicability across diverse material systems, DICE provides a practical route to developing reliable compact interatomic potentials.
