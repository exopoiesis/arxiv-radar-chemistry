Uncovering the Structural, Electronic, and Electrostatic Landscapes of SrTiO$_3$ Grain Boundaries via Grand Canonical Monte Carlo with a Machine-Learning Interatomic Potential

Xing He, Kairi Masuda, Aaron M. Schankler, Songsong Zhou, Andrew M. Rappe

arXiv:2611.24097·cond-mat.mtrl-sci·Published 2026-11-18

Grain boundaries in perovskites exhibit a rich variety of electronic, polar and magnetic properties, yet identifying their atomic structures and compositions remains challenging. Here, we combine the Grand Canonical Monte Carlo (GCMC) method with a universal machine-learning interatomic potential to explore the coupled compositional and structural phase space of $Σ$5(310)[001] SrTiO$_3$ grain boundaries. The resulting chemical potential phase diagram identifies ten stable structures belonging to four motif families: large Sr kite, small Sr kite, incomplete Sr large kite, and Ti-rich structures. Extending the GCMC method to the Sigma5(210)[001] and Sigma17(410)[001] grain boundaries reveals how grain boundary plane and inclination angle influence the competition between small- and large-kite motifs. Density functional theory calculations of the predicted Sigma5(310)[001] grain boundaries show that the composition and atomic structure strongly govern the spatial distribution of conduction and valence band edge states, interfacial band alignment, and carrier localization. Four distinct electronic regimes emerge: localized electron-doped, delocalized electron-doped, insulating, and hole-attracting states. Our electrostatics analysis reveals the coexistence of the short-range electric fields at the reconstructed GB core associated with local atomic density and bonding, and the longer-range fields outside the core arising from the space charge. These fields can also couple to local polar distortions and generate composition-dependent polarization patterns. These results establish an efficient framework for exploring oxide perovskite grain boundary structures and reveal how stoichiometry and atomic reconstruction couple to electronic structure, electrostatics, and local polarization, providing a route toward predictive grain boundary engineering in functional perovskites.
