# Learning Electronic Band Structures Across the s, p, d Blocks with an Empirical Pseudopotential Network

**Authors:** Qiang Zhu

Electronic band structures govern how crystals conduct electrons and absorb light. Density-functional theory (DFT) computes them through costly self-consistent iterations, while existing machine-learning models either predict isolated scalar properties or output Hamiltonians that still require DFT solvers. Here we present EpNet, a learned empirical pseudopotential for crystals spanning 63 elements across the \textit{s}, \textit{p} and \textit{d} blocks. An equivariant neural network builds its Hamiltonian from physics-motivated local potentials and nonlocal projectors. Trained solely on band energies, EpNet predicts complete band structures directly from atomic coordinates. On unseen chemical systems, its median errors are \winAll\ eV for band energies and \gapAll\ eV for band gaps. On 600,000 structures from an independent database, its band-gap errors are overall less than half those of existing models, at about 1\% of DFT cost. EpNet thus enables fast screening of electronic properties for materials discovery and electronic-structure simulations of large systems.
