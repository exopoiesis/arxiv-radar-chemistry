# Molecular shape controls dipolar correlations in polar liquids

**Authors:** Marceau Hénot

The dielectric constant of polar liquids depends not only on the magnitude of the molecular dipole moment but also on short range orientational correlations between neighboring dipoles. How molecular structure determines these correlations, quantified by the Kirkwood correlation factor $g_\mathrm{K}$, remains unclear. Using molecular dynamics simulations of model polar liquids without hydrogen bonding, we show that molecular shape plays a central role. We interpret $g_\mathrm{K}$ as resulting from the combination of dipolar interactions and steric effects, which together favor either alignment or antialignment of neighboring molecules. Depending on molecular shape, $g_\mathrm{K}$ can either increases or decreases upon cooling, or change monotony. A minimal model of dipoles on a cubic lattice reproduces these three regimes and attributes the nonmonotonic case to a competition between the first layer of neighbors, which favors alignment, and the second layer, which favors antialignment.
