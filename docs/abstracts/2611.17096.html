High-pressure behavior of the low-frequency mode of water

Maria Rescigno, Guillaume Radtke, Umbertoluca Ranieri, Richard Gaal, Luigi Paolasini, Andrea Piovano, Ferdinando Formisano, Paola Giura, Livia Eleonora Bove

arXiv:2611.17096·cond-mat.other·Published 2026-11-12

The low-frequency (LF) mode observed in the THz spectrum of liquid water near 10 meV has been known for decades, yet its microscopic origin remains debated. Leading interpretations assign it to a transverse acoustic-like excitation, a transverse optic-like mode, or a mode-mode coupling mechanism. We address this question by probing the collective dynamics of H$_2$O and D$_2$O at room temperature and pressures from 0.1 to 1 GPa using high-resolution (1.6 meV) inelastic X-ray scattering (IXS). The LF mode remains heavily damped, close to critical damping, throughout the explored range, while its frequency hardens markedly with pressure, much more than expected for a transverse acoustic mode. Comparison with IXS measurements and \textit{ab initio} simulations of crystalline Ice VI reveals a cluster of weakly dispersive modes in the same energy range, suggesting that the low-frequency mode in liquid water reflects a mixed character arising from the superposition of several branches rather than a single well-defined excitation, providing a coherent microscopic picture of water's anomalous THz dynamics under compression.
