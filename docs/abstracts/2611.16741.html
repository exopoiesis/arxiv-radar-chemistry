Active Learning for Molecular Discovery: A Retrospective Test toward TMC-1

R. I. El-Kholy, Z. M. Hayman

arXiv:2611.16741·astro-ph.GA·Published 2026-11-12

Machine-learning recommendation lists can help prioritize searches for new interstellar molecules, but whether reranking such a list as detections accumulate improves on it has not been tested. We present a pre-registered retrospective test toward TMC-1, using a published machine-learning list of 1,510 recommended candidates, consolidated to 1,434 candidates, and the species reported there through September 2026. At four annual origins from 2022 to 2025, a logistic-regression ranking on seven released features, refit on the detections known at each origin, separates later detections from never-detected candidates with a pooled AUC of 0.867, against 0.706 for the best single-criterion baseline (difference 0.160; 95\% interval 0.095-0.230). The advantage persists under a shifted date boundary and two alternative feature regimes, but is not established among the top 100 candidates (12 versus 7 detections). A searcher that refits on its own observations reaches half of the 63 detected species on the list in a median of 200 observations, against 332 for the best fixed ordering and 717.5 expected for random search. Uncertainty sampling, the standard active-learning rule, performs worse than random (AUC 0.210): later detections lie at low predictive uncertainty, close to known chemistry. Of 101 species in our validation set, 37 are absent from the list, and only 13 of these appear in a pool rebuilt from its source databases, including 2 of 11 radicals and neither ion. Candidate generation, not ranking, is the main constraint. We release 100 recommended targets, with code and frozen inputs, for prospective testing.
