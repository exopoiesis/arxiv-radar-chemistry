Ab initio calculations of ${\barδ_\text{NS}}$ and extraction of ${V_{ud}}$ using effective field theory

Samuel Novario, Graham Chambers-Wall, Vincenzo Cirigliano, Wouter Dekens, Jordy de Vries, Stefano Gandolfi, Martin Hoferichter, Emanuele Mereghetti

arXiv:2611.18843·nucl-th·Published 2026-11-13

Radiative corrections limit the precision of $V_{ud}$ extractions from superallowed $β$ decays. In particular, the model dependence in the nuclear matrix elements (NMEs) is difficult to assess without a clear organizing principle, which is provided by effective field theory (EFT). We report substantial progress in this program based on ab initio nuclear calculations using the coupled cluster method. Benchmarking results against Quantum Monte Carlo calculations for light nuclei, we evaluate all two-body NMEs that enter the correction $\barδ_\text{NS}$ across 12 superallowed transitions relevant for $V_{ud}$, including an estimate of truncation and Hamiltonian uncertainties. We find that certain three-body NMEs at ${\mathcal O}(α^2)$ play an important role, due to a combination of large numerical prefactors and coherent scaling with $Z^2$. We develop a formalism to estimate such NMEs based on nuclear densities to complete the EFT evaluation of $\barδ_\text{NS}$, validating the corresponding two-body NMEs against ab initio calculations. We further identify the corrections in the traditional phase-space factor that are captured by the EFT and remove them consistently, which renders the individual transitions mutually consistent. Profiting from a recent calculation of ${\mathcal O}(α^2 Z)$ ultrasoft photons, we find that the scale uncertainty in the EFT is now negligible, while the remaining uncertainty is dominated by contact terms required to renormalize the magnetic Gamow-Teller NMEs. One combination of low-energy constants can be determined from a global fit to the superallowed data, but the second one proves strongly correlated with $V_{ud}$, necessitating additional input to resolve the nuclear-structure effects. We argue that such short-distance contributions currently imply an uncertainty in $V_{ud}$ at the level of $δV_{ud}\simeq 6\times 10^{-4}$.
