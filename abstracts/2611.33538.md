# ILMlip: fast and universal machine-learning interatomic potentials

**Authors:** Jonas Hänseroth, Christian Dreßler

Large-scale pretrained machine-learned interatomic potentials keep growing more accurate as new models appear at a rapid pace, and more expensive: the current leader of Matbench Discovery as of November 2026 (F1 score 0.935) has $9.9\times10^7$ parameters and took 3.6 thousand GPU-hours to train. The ILMlip potentials presented here are optimised for speed as well as zero-shot accuracy. They are equivariant, conservative message-passing potentials in which every component is arranged for throughput: little work per pair of atoms and as much as needed per atom. Through ASE on one GPU, they run molecular dynamics up to about $10^3$ times faster than state-of-the-art message-passing potentials such as MACE (medium) and up to five times faster than NEP89 and DPA-4.1C. At the same time, six sizes with $1.4\times10^5$ to $2.8\times10^7$ parameters, pretrained on materials, molecules and solid acids, reach F1 scores of 0.75 to 0.91 on Matbench Discovery, placing the largest within 0.03 of the current leader. They therefore suit high-throughput, large or long simulations, and, being transferable, they are strong starting points for fine-tuning. Fine-tuned to six materials, the model of size S comes close to the ab initio force errors of a fine-tuned MACE, so it reaches reference-level accuracy at about 50 ns per atom and step (Cu fcc). Their components can be adopted separately, as a starting point for architectures with even better ratios of speed to accuracy. The ILMlip implementation is available at [https://github.com/jhaens/ilmlip](https://github.com/jhaens/ilmlip)
