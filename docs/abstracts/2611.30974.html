Interface-Engineered Piezoelectricity and Electromechanical Coupling in Two-Dimensional van der Waals Heterostructures

Shivanand Yadav, Raihan Ahammed, Umer Ali, B. S. Bhadoria, Yogesh S. Chauhan, Amit Agarwal, Somnath Bhowmick

arXiv:2611.30974·cond-mat.mtrl-sci·Published 2026-11-23

Interfaces between dissimilar two-dimensional (2D) materials provide a route to engineer electromechanical responses that are not intrinsic to the individual constituent layers. Here, we use high-throughput density functional theory (DFT) calculations to investigate 461 van der Waals heterostructures constructed from 57 2D monolayers and establish a materials-design framework linking work-function mismatch, interfacial charge redistribution, dipole formation, and strain-dependent electromechanical response. We find that heterostructure formation generates out-of-plane interfacial dipoles via charge redistribution between the constituent layers, while biaxial in-plane strain systematically modulates them. The resulting heterostructures exhibit two distinct regimes: metallic interfaces with strain-tunable interfacial dipoles and semiconducting heterostructures with well-defined piezoelectric responses. For the latter, Berry-phase (BP) calculations independently validate the charge-density-difference (CDD) approach, which is generally applicable to all representative systems, with piezoelectric coefficients calculated via two methods agreeing to within 10%. Among 24 selected heterostructures, effective biaxial interfacial electromechanical coefficients reach 83.35 pCm-1 . These results demonstrate that the interface can serve as the active element governing electromechanical coupling and provide a computationally guided strategy for designing strain-tunable out-of-plane electromechanical responses in 2D van der Waals heterostructures.
