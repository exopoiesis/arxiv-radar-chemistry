The MC-QTAIM: Why, How, and When?

Shant Shahbazian

arXiv:2611.23159·physics.chem-ph·Published 2026-11-17

The multi-component quantum theory of atoms in molecules (MC-QTAIM) generalizes the original QTAIM framework developed by Richard Bader and colleagues, extending atoms-in-molecules analysis beyond ab initio electronic wavefunctions. MC-QTAIM facilitates the examination of the AIM structure in systems and states that require MC wavefunctions, which describe systems containing multiple distinguishable classes of quantum particles. This advancement broadens the applicability of AIM analysis to a wide range of quantum systems and states that were previously inaccessible. These include exotic molecules, such as positronic species; exotic quantum states, such as superposed proton states in molecules with two- or multi-well potential energy surfaces; and non-adiabatic states of conventional molecules. In this review, I aim to explain the fundamental principles of MC-QTAIM analysis in accessible language and explore some of its potential applications. Additionally, the general formalism for energy partitioning in MC systems, called MC Interacting Quantum Atoms (MC-IQA), is introduced. This scheme unifies the energy partitioning of numerous quantum systems and encompasses the IQA method originally developed by Pendas and colleagues, as well as the previously developed two-component IQA (TC-IQA) scheme - developed by our group to analyze positronic systems - as special cases.
