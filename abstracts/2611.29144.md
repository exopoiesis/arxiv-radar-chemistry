# Structure-Preserving Analysis of a Nonlinear Reaction--Diffusion Model for Homogeneous Redox Catalysis

**Authors:** Guangming Yao, Michael Shaw, Nazia Nimmy, Adriana Silva

We study a reduced nonlinear reaction--diffusion model for homogeneous redox catalysis under a low-scan-rate, frozen-potential approximation motivated by cyclic voltammetry. The reduced system contains two concentration fields coupled through the same nonlinear reaction term and therefore satisfies, exactly within the reduced model, \[ (a-q)_τ=(a-q)_{yy}. \] This identity yields an exact reconstruction of the substrate concentration from the electroactive concentration and provides the organizing principle for the structure-preserving approximations developed below. Formulation I applies the homotopy perturbation method (HPM) to the catalyst field using a constant-coefficient Green-function hierarchy and reconstructs the substrate field from the exact heat equation. Formulation II linearizes the nonlinear reaction about a space- and time-dependent reference profile while preserving the same difference structure and the physical far-field state. The methods are evaluated using fourth-order method-of-lines discretizations, direct Green/heat-kernel formulas for the electrode current, and an independently refined nonlinear PDE benchmark. For the baseline problem, the published substrate approximation has maximum error \(1.69\times10^{-1}\), whereas the \(N=3\) errors are reduced to \(2.09\times10^{-5}\) for Formulation I and \(3.61\times10^{-6}\) for Formulation II. At \(N=1\), Formulation II improves the errors in the catalyst, substrate, and current by factors of approximately \(18.2\), \(46.9\), and \(42.1\), respectively, relative to Formulation I. The results show that preserving the exact coupled structure substantially improves accuracy and yields an efficient low-order analytical framework for nonlinear electrochemical reaction--diffusion problems.
