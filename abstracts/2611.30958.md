# Toward the Centennial of Wigner's Supermultiplet Symmetry: an Ab Initio Perspective

**Authors:** Phong Dang, Feng Pan

As the centennial of Wigner's supermultiplet theory approaches in 2037, this review offers a modern perspective through the recently advanced ab initio Symmetry Adapted Model (SAM). We begin with the symmetry's empirical foundations in two-nucleon data and electroweak processes, and trace its emergence from the strong force through effective field theories, the QCD large-$N_c$ limit, the similarity renormalization group, and lattice calculations. We then follow its historical arc, from early limited engagement, through its revived interest, to the modern algebraic and first-principles resurgence. Central to this renaissance are new nullspace-based algorithms for the U(3) and U(4) coupling and recoupling coefficients, which remove a bottleneck spanning nearly nine decades and are released as the open-source libraries u3lib and u4lib. Building on them, the SAM is the first large-scale ab initio shell model to encode both Elliott's spatial U(3) and Wigner's spin-isospin U(4) symmetries, resolving individual supermultiplets that share the same quadratic Casimir eigenvalue. Early results show that the spin-isospin symmetry strongly dominates high-fidelity chiral interactions and suppresses spin-isospin polarizability in light nuclei. We close with potential directions that the SAM is poised to advance within the next decade.
