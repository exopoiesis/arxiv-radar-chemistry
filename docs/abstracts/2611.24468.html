Hidden topological signatures in a van der Waals ferromagnet revealed by spin-resolved circular-dichroic photoemission

Jay R. Paudel, Federico Mazzola, Lidia A. Lapinski, Barun Ghosh, Valeria Rosa Rocha, Abigail M. Derrico, Nikola Maksimovic, Iulia Cojocariu, Daniel Baranowski, Christoph Klewe, Vitaliy Feyer, Ivana Vobornik, Claus M. Schneider, Giancarlo Panaccione, Arun Bansil, James G. Analytis, Alexander X. Gray

arXiv:2611.24468·cond-mat.str-el·Published 2026-11-17

Layered ferromagnets with strongly correlated electrons provide a platform for exploring the interplay among magnetism, spin-orbit coupling, and nontrivial band topology. $\mathrm{Ni}_{1/4}\mathrm{TaSe}_2$ is a correlated van der Waals ferromagnet with a transition temperature of 58 K that lies close in composition to a superconducting phase that emerges upon Ni depletion. Here, we combine spin- and angle-resolved photoemission spectroscopy, circular-dichroism measurements, and density functional theory calculations to investigate its spin-resolved electronic structure. We observe spin-polarized states crossing the Fermi level and signatures of broken time-reversal symmetry in the ferromagnetic state. Spin-resolved circular-dichroism measurements further reveal a helicity-dependent spin response consistent with a finite spin Berry curvature and suggestive of local topological character. Together, these results establish an experimental connection between ferromagnetism, spin-polarized electronic states, and topological signatures in $\mathrm{Ni}_{1/4}\mathrm{TaSe}_2$, providing a foundation for investigating how these properties may relate to the emergence of superconductivity upon Ni depletion.
