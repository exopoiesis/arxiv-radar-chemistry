# Competing lattice instabilities in epitaxially strained BaSnO3

**Authors:** Benyao Sun, Anna Grünebohm

We use density functional theory to map the lattice-dynamical landscape of BaSnO3 under biaxial compressive strain and uncover the hierarchy of structural instabilities. Our results demonstrate that the phase stability of strained BaSnO3 cannot be inferred from the softening of the polar mode alone. Instead it is governed by the competition among lattice instabilities and their anharmonic coupling to structural relaxation. Two-mode energy-surface analyses show that the polar and rotational order parameters interact predominantly competitively. While A- and M-point octahedral rotations become unstable before the zone-center polar mode and dominate the harmonic response for moderate values of strain, they are related to a continuous transition without anharmonic lattice coupling and a moderate gain of energy with strain. Large strain may induce a first order transition to a supertetragonal polar state. Once this state is stabilized, the magnitude of polarization and ferroelectric energy well depth are comparable to BaTiO3 under compressive strain. Furthermore, we discuss how an electric field or electron doping may tip the balance between the different modes and favor of the supertetragonal structure over antipolar and rotational alternatives at a reduced critical strain.
