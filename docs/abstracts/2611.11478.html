Nuclear form factor measurements can be used to extract the nuclear charge radius. These can be compared to ab initio nuclear structure calculations, with the difference between 3H and 3He charge radii providing information on the spatial distribution of protons vs neutrons in these nuclei. Extractions of the 3H-3He charge radius difference are limited by the modest amount of data for tritium elastic scattering at low momentum transfer. The experiment made a precise measurement of the 3H/3He form factor ratio at low momentum transfer which is combined with the absolute 3He measurements to provide improved 3H data. This provides an improved determination of the absolute normalization corrections of world data sets, giving improved precision for the low-Q^2 measurements that are most sensitive to the radius. We extract the elastic cross sections and the 3H/3He ratio at Q^2=3.03 fm^{-2} and evaluate the impact of this point on a global fit of previous form factor extractions. We examine both the impact on the normalizations of the other data sets and the fit parameters, including the charge radius and its uncertainty. Our sensitivity study shows that the new precision data points constrain the normalization of previous data sets, reducing the uncertainties by up to 12% and yielding a 4% improvement in the radius. Including projected data based on an approved experiment yields improvements in the normalization factors of up to 20% and a 27% improvement in the 3H RMS charge radius extraction, allowing for an improved extraction of the 3H-3He radius difference from a direct fit to the form factor ratios over a range in Q^2.
