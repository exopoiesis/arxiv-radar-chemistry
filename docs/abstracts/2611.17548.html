Mechanism-Level Chemical Reaction Simulation with Electron Bookkeeping Transformer

Sarah Cao, Shitong Luo, Connor W. Coley

arXiv:2611.17548·cs.CE·Published 2026-11-12

Modeling chemical reaction mechanisms is central to understanding and predicting chemical reactivity. We present Electron Bookkeeping Transformer (Bookkeeper), a data-driven model that simulates chemical reactions at mechanistic resolution by generating electron movement trajectories. Following the symbolic arrow-pushing formalism in chemistry, Bookkeeper explicitly models each electron as a token and predicts its movement sequentially, ensuring mass and charge conservation during both elementary step and reaction pathway predictions by construction. On reaction-size-balanced test sets, Bookkeeper improves top-1 elementary-step accuracy, top-1 pathway rank accuracy, and top-1 to top-5 product prediction accuracy over FlowER. On the entire test set, it achieves higher elementary-step top-1 accuracy but lower pathway accuracy than FlowER. Bookkeeper also samples elementary steps substantially faster, adapts to several unseen reaction types with minimal fine-tuning, and generates candidate chemical transformations for further screening. These results suggest that explicit electron bookkeeping is a useful framework for virtually screening chemical reactions across diverse reagents, validating computer-assisted synthesis planning predictions, and providing mechanistic interpretations of reactivity.
