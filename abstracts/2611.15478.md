# Defect Topology Governs the Optical Response of Doped Amorphous Silicon

**Authors:** Ridwan Hussein, Chinonso Ugwumadu, Louise A. M. Rosset, Roxanne M. Tutchton

The influence of defect topology and chemical doping on the optical response of amorphous silicon (a-Si) remains unresolved. In this study, hybrid-functional density functional theory and linear-response calculations are employed to examine four structurally distinct continuous random networks with substitutional boron and phosphorus doping. The calculated optical properties reproduce key experimental trends and demonstrate that dopant sensitivity is determined not only by defect concentration, but also by the character and spatial organization of defects. A configuration with fully extended 4-fold coordination shows the strongest dielectric perturbation to doping, while dopant effects in networks with pre-existing defect states are more varied. For most networks, the dielectric response is more strongly perturbed by boron than phosphorus. Notably, a network having both 3- and 5-fold defects with a total defect fraction of 7.4% reverses the typical boron-dominant response of other cases, becoming more sensitive to phosphorus. Tauc and optical sum-rule analyses further indicate that network topology defines the broader optical landscape, whereas doping primarily affects states near the gap and redistributes spectral weight. These findings establish defect topology as a microscopic control parameter that links amorphous structure, dopant activity, and optical response in amorphous silicon.
