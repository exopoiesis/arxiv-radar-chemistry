From a High-T$\rm_C$ Spin-Gapless Semiconductor to a Spin-Glass: The Role of Disorder in NiMnCrAl

Arka Patra, Tufan Roy, Nilofar Kurawle, Tamaghna Maitra, Souvik Banerjee, Mantu Modak, Shuvankar Gupta, Sudip Chakraborty, Soumya Bhowmik, Papri Dasgupta, Rajashri Urkude, Biplab Ghosh, D. Bhattacharyya, A. Sundaresan, Sudhindra Rayaprol, Masafumi Shirai, Chandan Mazumdar

arXiv:2611.22784·cond-mat.mtrl-sci·Published 2026-11-17

We combine first-principles calculations with structural, magnetic, and transport measurements to investigate the influence of chemical disorder on the quaternary Heusler alloy NiMnCrAl. For the ordered Y-type structure, density-functional-theory calculations predict a ferrimagnetic spin-gapless-semiconducting state with a total magnetic moment of $1.99\,μ_{\mathrm{B}}/\mathrm{f.u.}$ and a mean-field ordering temperature of approximately $1300~\mathrm{K}$. Polycrystalline NiMnCrAl forms in a single average cubic phase; however, combined x-ray diffraction, neutron diffraction, and extended x-ray absorption fine-structure measurements reveal substantial antisite disorder, characterized by predominantly B2-like disorder with additional cross-sublattice mixing. No long-range ferrimagnetic transition is observed up to $700~\mathrm{K}$. Instead, the compound exhibits a broad magnetic anomaly near $37~\mathrm{K}$, frequency-dependent ac susceptibility, slow stretched-exponential relaxation, and field-cooled and zero-field-cooled memory effects, establishing a glassy magnetic state. The electrical resistivity is metallic and the Seebeck coefficient is negative, consistent with the loss of the ideal spin-gapless electronic structure. Exchange-interaction calculations show that antisite disorder introduces short Mn--Mn separations and competing antiferromagnetic Mn--Mn and Mn--Cr interactions, providing a microscopic basis for magnetic frustration. These results demonstrate the strong sensitivity to the predicted spin-gapless and ferrimagnetic states of NiMnCrAl to chemical disorder.
