Emergence of Phonon Hydrodynamics as a Non-Hermitian Symmetry-Breaking Phase Transition

Xin Qian, Guanda Quan, Ning Wang, Yaoming Chu, Run Hu, Te-Huan Liu, Xiangfan Xu, Jianming Cai, Ronggui Yang

arXiv:2611.16161·cond-mat.mes-hall·Published 2026-11-12

Phonon hydrodynamics enables collective heat flow beyond the classical diffusive limit and offers a promising route to controlling thermal transport in low-dimensional materials and nanoscale devices. However, quantitative criteria for determining when this hydrodynamic state emerges and where its boundaries lie remain a longstanding challenge in condensed matter physics. Here, we show that the onset of phonon hydrodynamics can be formulated as a non-Hermitian dynamical transition that converts microscopic phonon scattering properties into predictive boundaries for wavelike heat transport. Starting from the Peierls-Boltzmann transport equation, we derive an effective Schrodinger equation for the coupled dynamics of temperature and phonon drift fields, allowing the hydrodynamic regime to be identified directly from material transport properties. Within this framework, a pair of exceptional points quantitatively define the lower and upper boundaries of the hydrodynamic window, providing a critical design criterion beyond the qualitative scattering-rate inequalities proposed by Guyer and Krumhansl six decades ago. Furthermore, we derive an analytical criterion for the existence of hydrodynamic transport, showing that a finite hydrodynamic window can be supported only when the effective thermal diffusivity exceeds the phononic momentum diffusivity. Using ab initio phonon properties as inputs, our theory accurately captures the experimentally observed temperature- and length-dependent ultrafast second-sound dynamics. These results establish a symmetry-based framework for understanding collective energy transport and provide quantitative design principles for identifying the materials, temperatures, and characteristic length scales that support wavelike heat transport in low-dimensional materials and functional devices.
