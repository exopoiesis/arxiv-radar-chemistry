Cation-intermixing driven evolution from a narrow avoided crossing to an enlarged inverted gap in Ge$_2$Bi$_2$Te$_5$

Alexander S. Frolov, Alexander V. Fedorov, Nadezhda V. Vladimirova, Sergey M. Aksenov, Mark A. Naumov, Savely V. Domenko, Artem A. Minaev, Vasily S. Stolyarov, Denis V. Vyalikh, Lada V. Yashina

arXiv:2611.15631·cond-mat.mtrl-sci·Published 2026-11-11

Combining the building blocks and structural motifs of the topological insulator Bi$_2$Te$_3$ and the ferroelectric Rashba semiconductor GeTe, we synthesized and comprehensively characterized the resulting Ge$_2$Bi$_2$Te$_5$ compound, which exhibits pronounced cation intermixing. Using momentum-resolved photoemission spectroscopy and ab initio calculations, we demonstrate how cation intermixing drives the evolution of its electronic structure from a narrow avoided crossing to a substantially enlarged inverted band gap. We establish the microscopic origin of this behavior in the redistribution of Ge and Bi atoms within the cation sublattice, which increases the contribution of Bi-derived states near the valence-band maximum and enhances the effective contribution of spin-orbit coupling, thereby stabilizing the topological electronic structure. Our findings demonstrate that cation disorder, which can be controlled during crystal growth, provides an additional degree of freedom for engineering topological electronic structures, opening new opportunities for the development of functional quantum materials.
