Stable but Hidden: Identifiability and Experimental Design under Missing Compositional Support

Harry Sevi, Joonatan E. M. Laulainen

arXiv:2611.30070·cs.LG·Published 2026-11-23

AI-guided scientific discovery often uses inexpensive predictors to allocate scarce verification measurements. We study a structural failure when relevant components have been observed separately but never jointly: distinct admissible response functions can agree on all observed compositions while disagreeing on the missing combinations. Additional same-support data cannot resolve this ambiguity, and agreement among fitted models need not expose it. We characterize the unresolved directions as an invisible subspace $\mathcal{N}_T(\mathcal{F}) = \ker \mathcal{R}_T$, whose geometry determines oracle zero-shot ambiguity and minimax risk under a symmetric, bounded ambiguity class. When the missing response lies in an $r$-dimensional, well-conditioned linear basis, optimally designed joint measurements achieve minimax integrated risk $Θ(σ^2 r/m)$, with classical $I$-optimality the fixed-design criterion. We validate on materials. In electrocatalysis, a six-dimensional support-derived structural basis (a prior on candidate directions, not an identified physical model) reduces held-out MSE against a dimension-matched generic basis (median paired ratio 0.75, improving 98% of 60 element pairs at $m = 15$), while $I$-optimal and same-basis posterior-variance acquisition show no detected difference. In Materials Project, removing joint property labels reduces $E_{\text{above hull}}$ ranking on 90% of 30 element pairs, more than matched data-removal controls, under a frozen pretrained representation. Pair difficulty is reproducible across learners but poorly predicted by zero-label ensemble disagreement; 20 joint measurements enable transferable pair-level rankings without reliable absolute calibration. In practice, experiment selection should ask which relationships the observed support leaves unidentified before asking where a fitted model is least certain.
