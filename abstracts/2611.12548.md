# Direct imaging of vortex lattices across fractional fillings in two-dimensional Josephson junction arrays

**Authors:** Cequn Li, Austin R. Kaczmarek, Omri Lesser, Maciej W. Olszewski, Katja C. Nowack

Josephson junction arrays are predicted to host a rich sequence of commensurate vortex lattices. However, direct imaging of these configurations remains limited, while transport measurements cannot resolve the underlying vortex arrangements or how order breaks down. Using scanning superconducting quantum interference device (SQUID) microscopy, we resolve individual vortices and directly image this long-predicted hierarchy spanning filling factors $f$ = 1/8 to 1/2 in two-dimensional Au-Nb Josephson junction arrays, including several states with no discernible transport signatures. At $f$ = 1/4, two structurally distinct vortex orders with 4$\times$4 and 2$\times$4 unit cells coexist, consistent with their energetic degeneracy in the frustrated XY model. Away from commensurate filling, we resolve defect structures tied to the underlying vortex-lattice order: the $f$ = 1/2 checkerboard retains its order around isolated excess vortices and vacancies, whereas the $f$ = 1/3 staircase fragments into domains separated by domain walls. These results reveal how frustration, competing configurations, and defects shape the spatial coherence of commensurate vortex order in engineered superconducting lattices.
