Pressure-driven evolution of crystal and molecular structures reshapes excited-state competition in coronene

Wenju Zhou, Shuhua Yuan, Dominique Laniel, Yoshifumi Hashikawa, Philip Dalladay-Simpson, Jun Deng, Jianbo Zhang, Xinyu Wang, Kejun Bu, Akun Liang, Simon Parsons, Dominik Daisenberger, Egor Koemets, Hirofumi Ishii, Weiwei Dong, Huiyang Gou, Qingyang Hu, Yang Ding, Martina Vrankić, Takeshi Nakagawa

arXiv:2611.17698·cond-mat.mtrl-sci·Published 2026-11-13

Molecular nanocarbons exhibit unique optoelectronic properties and considered as promising candidates for organic device applications, however, understanding how molecular geometry and crystal packing jointly govern physical properties remains a fundamental challenge in π-conjugated molecular materials. Here, we demonstrate how these structural factors modulate the photoluminescence of coronene under high pressure. Combining high-pressure single-crystal X-ray diffraction, Raman spectroscopy, optical spectroscopy, and density functional theory calculations, we establish a relationship between distinct structural changes and the evolution of competing emissive states. Our results reveal that crystal polymorphism modifies the balance between free-exciton (FE) and self-trapped-exciton (STE) emission, while pressure-induced molecular bending is associated with a nonmonotonic evolution of STE emission. As intermolecular distances shorten, enhanced intermolecular electronic interactions give rise to excimer-related emission, introducing an additional emissive pathway. Although pressure-dependent optical properties of coronene have been linked to changes in crystal packing, our findings highlight that the optical response of a molecular solid is not governed by molecular deformation or crystal packing alone, but emerges from their interplay with intermolecular electronic coupling. By directly resolving molecular bending alongside changes in excited-state emission, this work provides insight into how distinct structural degrees of freedom can reshape the photophysical behavior of π-conjugated molecular solids.
