Burgers vectors from Voronoi topology

Emanuel A. Lazar

arXiv:2611.22488·cond-mat.mtrl-sci·Published 2026-11-17

Automated dislocation analysis is standard in atomistic simulations, yet current tools are limited to a handful of crystal structures. We introduce an algorithm without adjustable parameters, based on Voronoi topology, that identifies dislocations in a broad class of crystals. Each face shared by two cells that match the ideal crystal is taken as a step in it, and summing these steps around closed circuits of such cells yields exact Burgers vectors, without geometric templates. Tested on dislocations inserted into all 9,610 Materials Project structures whose Voronoi cells are stable with a wide margin, the algorithm finds every dislocation with its correct Burgers vector. In raw molecular-dynamics snapshots, including eleven dislocation types in $θ$-Al$_2$Cu and bcc iron up to its melting point, every Burgers vector it returns is correct, although near melting the dislocations merge into one cluster. Voronoi topology thus provides a natural language in which a crystal, its defects, and their Burgers vectors are all described.
