Scaling laws in machine learning on non-ideal experimental data enable inverse design of radiative-cooling coatings

Kunihiko Shizume, Jiawen Li, Yaerim Lee, Yuki Asano, Tuwei Liao, Koji Tsuda, Junichiro Shiomi

arXiv:2611.19596·cond-mat.mtrl-sci·Published 2026-11-14

Materials informatics increasingly targets multicomponent materials, yet experimental datasets inevitably contain non-ideal variations arising from imperfect uniformity and reproducibility in composition, coating formation, and measurement. Whether machine-learning prediction accuracy scales with amount of data under these conditions remains unclear. Here, we develop an automated platform that produced and optically characterized 11,520 radiative-cooling coatings spanning nine material and processing parameters. Forward and inverse neural networks, and a spectrum-conditioned diffusion model were trained on subsets of 10 to 10,000 samples. All models exhibited power-law scaling, with prediction errors decreasing with dataset size. Experimental inverse-design validation showed that larger datasets produced spectra closer to a broadband-emitter target. The diffusion model generated multiple candidate parameter sets, allowing selection among alternative solutions based on ease of fabrication, cost, and scalability. These results demonstrate predictable learning-curve scaling in non-ideal experimental data and provide a quantitative basis for estimating the benefit of additional experimental data, and thus the cost-effectiveness of materials-informatics.
