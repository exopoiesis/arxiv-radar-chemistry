# Phase transitions and sharp localisation in anomalous localised resonance

**Authors:** Konstantinos Alexopoulos

We study how detuning and absorption determine the strength and spatial extent of anomalous localised resonance in a two-dimensional quasistatic coated structure with shell permittivity $-1+c\ell^p+i\ell$, where $\ell>0$ is the loss and $c\ne0$, $p>0$ are fixed. Detuning can carry the material path through narrow resonances, producing intermittent rather than sustained power growth. For an explicit family of noncircular shells and a smooth exterior dipole source, both fixed, we classify the dissipated power under a quantitative smallness condition on the deformation. Depending on the detuning exponent and conformal source distance, the power diverges along the entire path, exhibits divergent peaks with vanishing gaps, or decays, including the threshold cases. We also identify the sharp conformal boundary beyond which the field, normalized by the square root of the dissipated power, converges locally to zero along every divergent-power sequence. For resonant sources, tangential reference crossings yield an explicit nonzero limit for the exterior gradient energy divided by the dissipated power at the critical radius.
