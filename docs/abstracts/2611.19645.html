Formulation of Brownian chain molecular dynamics and the path-resolved quasi-density operator

Motoyuki Shiga

arXiv:2611.19645·physics.chem-ph·Published 2026-11-14

Brownian chain molecular dynamics (BCMD) [M. Shiga, J. Comput. Chem. 43, 1864 (2022)] was introduced as a practical path-integral-based approach to approximate quantum vibrational dynamics in molecular and condensed-phase systems. Its equations of motion combine Newtonian dynamics of the centroid variables with overdamped Brownian dynamics of the non-centroid path variables. Here, we introduce a path-resolved quasi-density-operator (path-QDO) representation by extending the QDO construction underlying centroid molecular dynamics to the full discretized imaginary-time path. For two position-diagonal observables, this representation gives an exact expression for the Kubo-transformed correlation function in the fine-discretization limit, without requiring either observable to be linear. BCMD can be viewed as a phenomenological, time-homogeneous Markov approximation to the exact reduced propagation in these path variables, although its generator is not derived from the path-QDO formulation. We also formulate and validate a multiple-time-step algorithm that allows pseudo-Hamiltonian errors in the deterministic propagation to be controlled by separating the Brownian refresh interval from the inner integration time step. We test the path-QDO representation on one-dimensional models and present BCMD infrared spectra of q-TIP4P/F ice, ambient water, and hot water, comparing them with published results from other approximate quantum-dynamics methods.
