How to calculate temperature and energy in Molecular Dynamics

Søren Toxvaerd

arXiv:2611.21282·physics.class-ph·Published 2026-11-16

Molecular Dynamics (MD) simulation of the dynamics of classical objects has become incredibly popular in natural sciences since the method was developed in the sixties. A conservative estimate is that it involves many hundreds of thousands of scientific articles and all subdisciplines of natural sciences. The earliest MD simulations also introduced a simple time-symmetric expression for the momentum and velocity, which has since been used uncritically in almost all MD simulations. The expression is incorrect and can lead to significant errors. But it is straightforward to replace the expression with the correct one, and the motive of the article is to draw attention to how to calculate velocity, kinetic energy, temperature, and energy in an MD simulation correctly. Newton's algorithm of the discrete dynamics has the same quality as his analytical dynamics, and Newton's discrete dynamics conserves the energy exactly. That is the second motive for the article, namely to show that conservation of energy in a conservative system is only obtained by the correct expressions for the velocity and kinetic energy.
