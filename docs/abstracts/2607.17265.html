First-principles electron-phonon scattering in real-time TDDFT

Zhengwei Nie, Subhojit Pal, Marti Lüders, Alexander Buccheri, Hannes Hübener, Shunsuke A. Sato, Umberto De Giovannini

arXiv:2607.17265·cond-mat.mtrl-sci·Published 2026-07-19

Real-time time-dependent density functional theory provides a first-principles description of coherent electron dynamics in laser-driven solids, but its unitary formulation cannot capture the irreversible scattering, relaxation, and decoherence processes that drive excited carriers toward equilibrium. Here, we develop a dissipative rt-TDDFT framework in which first-principles electron-phonon interactions enter the evolution of the reduced one-body density matrix through self-energy-derived collision integrals within the Born-Markov approximation. The approach retains the quantum-coherent real-time propagation of the electronic system while introducing phonon-mediated transitions that redistribute carriers in energy and crystal momentum, thereby incorporating the microscopic momentum-transfer processes responsible for relaxation in real materials. The resulting framework provides a practical first-principles route to simulate relaxation, decoherence, and time-resolved spectroscopic signatures in realistic crystalline materials.
