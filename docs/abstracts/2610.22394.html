Visualizing surface-reaction-driven chloride migration and cascading failure in sulfide solid electrolytes

Xiaoya He, Haoran Zhang, Xinyang Yue, Hang Liu, Zian Mao, Jialong Wang, Min Liu, Shufen Chu, Liwei Chen, Zhaoxin Yu, Xi Liu, Chuying Ouyang, Ziyang Ning, Yujun Xie

arXiv:2610.22394·cond-mat.mtrl-sci·Published 2026-10-14

Moisture instability of sulfide solid electrolytes limits practical use in all-solid-state batteries. Spatially resolving gas--solid reactions during moisture exposure remains challenging. Here, cryogenic four-dimensional scanning transmission electron microscopy (cryo-4D-STEM) maps nanoscale degradation in low- and high-Cl argyrodite electrolytes, Li\(_6\)PS\(_5\)Cl and Li\(_{5.5}\)PS\(_{4.5}\)Cl\(_{1.5}\). Li\(_6\)PS\(_5\)Cl undergoes predominantly surface-localized degradation that progressively extends inward, whereas Li\(_{5.5}\)PS\(_{4.5}\)Cl\(_{1.5}\) exhibits rapid interior amorphization accompanied by outward chloride redistribution. Density functional theory calculations indicate a lower chloride-migration barrier along the modeled pathway in the higher-Cl composition, while chloride removal and vacancy formation distort the PS\(_4^{3-}\) framework and may destabilize the lattice. Surface treatment suppresses detectable large-scale chloride segregation and improves moisture tolerance, retaining 99.9% of the capacity measured at the start of 0.33 C cycling after 150 cycles. These results identify chloride redistribution as a key process in degradation propagation and suggest a route toward moisture-tolerant solid-state batteries.
