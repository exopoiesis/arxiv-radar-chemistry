Using potential derivatives to reduce the cost of rectangular collocation: a general and versatile approach for computational vibrational spectroscopy without a potential energy surface

Moksh Bhateja, Tucker Carrington, Sergei Manzhos

arXiv:2611.28125·physics.chem-ph·Published 2026-11-18

We modify the exact Cartesian kinetic energy operator, rectangular collocation approach of Manzhos and Carrington [J. Chem. Phys. 145 (2016) 224110] by using information contained in potential derivatives to decrease the number of required potential points. We show that this makes it possible to substantially reduce the number of collocation points, which is important, in particular, when energies are computed directly ab initio. Vibrational band intensities are also computed. We first demonstrate the method on Cl2CO and compare to high-accuracy Smolyak collocation calculations on an accurate potential energy surface (PES). Sub-cm-1 accuracy is obtained with a few thousand Gaussian basis functions and potential points. A spectrum computed directly from DFT energies and a comparison to GVPT2 (generalized vibrational perturbation theory) are also presented. We then consider LiCO2 as a more anharmonic case, for which a quartic force field potential is less reliable, and which also serves as a surrogate model for a catalytic system (mimicking CO2 bond weakening by charge donation). The rectangular collocation method is used to compute the spectrum of LiCO2 directly from ab initio energies and to compare to GVPT2. The method is implemented as a Claude Skill and is easily usable by anyone on any molecule. The method is a general and versatile tool for anharmonic computational vibrational spectroscopy. It does not require a PES and no coupling is neglected.
