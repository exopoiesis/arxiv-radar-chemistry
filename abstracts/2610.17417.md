# High-Resolution X-ray Spectro-Microscopy of Au Microcrystallites

**Authors:** Chaitali Sow, Martin Seyrich, Johannes Hagemann, Giridhar U. Kulkarni, Thomas F. Keller, Christian G. Schroer, Andreas Schropp

Understanding the functionalities of heterogeneous catalysts requires high-resolution nanoscale chemical imaging tools. X-ray spectro-ptychography offers this capability by enabling X-ray absorption near edge structure (XANES) based amplitude and phase contrasts imaging with high spatial resolution. However, such demonstrations at energies above 10 keV, such as the Au L-edges, remain limited due to weak X-ray absorption cross-sections and correspondingly, low signal levels. Here, multimodal spectro-microscopy has been performed on Au microcrystallites that host both conventional face-centered cubic (fcc) and unconventional non-cubic lattices, particularly important for catalysis. Amplitude and phase contrasts across the $\mathrm{L}_3$ edge are resolved on thin Au crystallites (150 nm to 1.71 $μ\mathrm{m}$) with 45 nm spatial resolution. The resulting XANES spectra exhibit excellent agreement with standard transmission and fluorescence contrasts, while providing complementary chemical insights with improved spatial resolution. Quantitative retrieval of anomalous phase variations ~12 keV further demonstrates the suitability of this method for high-resolution chemical imaging across a wide range of functional materials.
