# Improving Nonequilibrium Thermal Conduction in CaF2 by Breaking the Phonon Bottleneck Effect

**Authors:** Mingde Qu, Minghuan Song, Yuxing Wang, Wudi Wang, Chenbo Zhang, Jiamin Quan, Jun Xu, Zhongwei Zhang, Jie Chen

Heat dissipation in laser optical materials depends not only on their thermal conductivity but also on how absorbed energy is redistributed among various phonon modes. In calcium fluoride (CaF2), rapid energy transfer from hot electrons to weakly propagating longitudinal optical (LO) phonons can create a nonequilibrium phonon bottleneck effect that limits efficient heat removal. Here, we propose selective phonon excitation as an effective strategy to break this bottleneck. Combining electron-phonon scattering calculations, phonon Boltzmann transport analysis, and nonequilibrium molecular dynamics simulations, we identify LO phonons as the primary modes that preferentially receive energy from hot carriers, leading to energy accumulation due to their inefficient transport and relaxation. Guided by three-phonon scattering phase-space analysis, selective excitation of transverse acoustic (TA) modes can accelerate LO relaxation and promote energy transfer toward heat-carrying acoustic modes. While sustained LO-phonon excitation reduces the thermal conductivity by approximately 12%, additional TA-mode excitation improves heat conduction by approximately 21% compared to the state limited by the LO-phonon bottleneck, surpassing the unexcited value. These findings demonstrate that controlling interbranch energy redistribution can counteract phonon-bottleneck-induced heat accumulation and reveal a working route for active thermal management in laser optical materials through selective control of phonon populations.
