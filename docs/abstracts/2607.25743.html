Physical Mechanism of Vacuole Formation in Liquid Droplets

Pranay Jaiswal, Ivar S. Haugerud, William Verstraeten, Kerstin Göpfrich, Job Boekhoven, Christoph A. Weber

arXiv:2607.25743·cond-mat.soft·Published 2026-07-28

Vacuoles have been observed in liquid droplets across variety of experimental systems, ranging from biomolecular condensates composed of proteins and RNA, to synthetic coacervates formed by charged polymers or synthetic nanostars. These vacuoles are long-lived domains depleted of droplet material, and their formation is puzzling because the associated increase in interfacial area is thermodynamically unfavorable. Using theory, we show that vacuoles form through a generic mechanism: a local spinodal instability within the droplet. We demonstrate this mechanism in several experimentally relevant scenarios, including temperature quenches and droplets coupled to chemical processes occurring either inside or outside the droplet. Using non-equilibrium thermodynamics, we develop a theoretical framework that identifies the physicochemical conditions controlling whether vacuoles form and how big vacuoles can become. Our results suggest molecular designs and chemical pathways that promote vacuolation, enabling multi-compartment formation with engineered functions such as enhanced surface catalysis and compartment fission.
