Thermal-pulse-resolved Binary Population Synthesis with Detailed Stellar Models: Implications for Long-period WD+MS Formation

Yanxu Shi, Hongwei Ge, Diogo Belloni, Dengkai Jiang, Hailiang Chen, Zhanwen Han

arXiv:2611.13502·astro-ph.SR·Published 2026-11-10

The origin of the long-orbital-period white dwarf--main-sequence (WD+MS) binaries revealed by Gaia challenges the traditional association of common-envelope evolution (CEE) with short orbital periods. We develop a thermal-pulse-resolved binary population synthesis framework based on 51 detailed MESA evolutionary tracks with initial masses of 1--6 $M_{\odot}$. Applying these tracks to $5\times10^6$ initially sampled binaries, we follow the time-dependent stellar radius, wind mass loss, angular momentum, and tidal interactions, while calculating the envelope binding energy directly from the donor structure at the onset of Roche-lobe overflow. We find that thermal pulses can trigger binary interaction through envelope expansion and tidal orbital shrinkage, while the weakly bound envelopes of highly evolved thermally pulsing asymptotic giant branch (TP-AGB) donors allow CE ejection with limited orbital contraction. For our standard model, with $α_{\mathrm{CE}}$ = 0.3 and $α_{\mathrm{th}}$ = 0.5, systems with orbital periods above 30 days constitute approximately 11.5\% of the resulting TP-AGB-initiated post-CE binaries (PCEBs). Long-period PCEBs can form across a large range of CE parameters and can reach periods of several hundred days even without any internal-energy contribution when $α_{\mathrm{CE}}$ = 1. Their WD masses peak near 0.6 $M_\odot$, whereas systems containing more massive WDs are rare. Moreover, long-period PCEBs closely follow the single-star initial--final mass relation, providing a potential diagnostic of their formation channel in clusters, where progenitor masses can be independently constrained. Our results identify late TP-AGB CEE as a viable contributor to the observed long-period WD+MS population.
