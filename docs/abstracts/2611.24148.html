Machine learning interatomic potentials and orientational defects in polymers

Arnold Kiv, Volodymyr Soloviev, Andrii Tuzhykov, Andrii Bielinskyi, Taras Kavetskyy, Nataliia Hoivanovych, Ondrej Šauša, Jaroslav Rusnák, Helena Švajdlenková, Jolita Ostrauskaite, Mykola Slusarenko

arXiv:2611.24148·cond-mat.mtrl-sci·Published 2026-11-18

Photopolymerization of acrylated epoxidized soybean oil (AESO) is a convenient model of a self-organizing process in a soft, bio-based material: ultraviolet light starts a free-radical chain reaction, the growing network releases heat, and the mobility of molecular groups changes on several time scales at once. We follow this process from two sides. Experimentally, the real and imaginary parts of the dielectric permittivity of an AESO:VDM (1:0.5) mixture with 3 mol% of the photoinitiator DMPA were recorded at 500-5000 Hz under an intermittent on/off illumination protocol, and the resulting time series were analysed with a sliding-window Shannon entropy and the Grassberger-Procaccia correlation integral. Both measures show that short light pulses at the start of curing trigger strongly correlated, collective relaxation, which fades as the network forms, leaving only localized reorientations active. We describe these reorientations as orientational defects and quantify their energetics with the MACE-OFF23 machine learning interatomic potential, using relaxed dihedral scans of five rotational modes ($α$, $α/β$, $β$, $γ$, $δ$) of an AESO fragment. Compared with density functional theory (DFT) reference profiles, MACE reproduces the shape of all five profiles (Pearson $r=0.95$-$0.98$) and the hierarchy of barriers - from 2.9 eV for the constrained main-chain rotation, through 0.54-0.81 eV for the acrylate rotations, to 0.16-0.18 eV for the methyl and 0.06 eV for the hydroxyl rotation - while underestimating the barrier heights by 3-20%. A simple Arrhenius estimate shows that the acrylate rotations are the modes closest to the frequency window of the dielectric measurement.
