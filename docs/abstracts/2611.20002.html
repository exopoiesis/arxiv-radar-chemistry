Self-assembly of dipolar nanoclusters on an attractive substrate: A coarse-grained simulation approach

Denis Gessert, Harshita Chauhan, Thi Ngoc Ha Nguyen, Christoph Tegenkamp, Michael Mehring, Varsha Banerjee, Martin Weigel

arXiv:2611.20002·cond-mat.soft·Published 2026-11-15

Recently, Thomas et al. [Nanoscale 17, 18291 (2025)] showed that bismuth oxido nanoclusters (BiO-NCs) of the type [Bi$_{38}$ O$_{45}$ (Boc-L-Phe-O)$_{24}$] form chains when deposited onto gold or pyrolitic graphite substrates. This chain formation is significantly enhanced when the bismuth in the BiO-NCs is partially replaced by cerium through doping. In this work, we study a simplified, coarse-grained model of this process, namely (magnetic) Stockmayer particles in the presence of an attractive substrate. The model is investigated by means of molecular dynamics simulations. The deposition experiment is mimicked by a rapid quench from high temperature to a low temperature below the bulk coexistence curve. The results are found to be qualitatively similar to the experimental observations reported earlier. In our simulations, we find significant chain formation in the bulk even at rather low concentrations, which is in contrast to the BiO-NCs in ethanol for which dynamic light scattering reveals negligible aggregation in the absence of the substrate. This lends support to a scenario, conjectured in the work of Thomas at al., where the dipole moments are induced by the particle-substrate interaction and hence there is little dipole moment in the bulk. In a second part, we study the system in equilibrium. We consider the scaling of chains, the number of layers populated as a function of two-dimensional density, and we draw analogies to polymers on interfaces, which have been extensively studied over the past decades. Most notably, it is found that the chains behave as self-avoiding walks at low densities, which gives way to rod-like scaling at higher densities.
