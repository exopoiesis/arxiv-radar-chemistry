Nanoscale origin of thermal anisotropy in monoclinic \texorpdfstring{$β$-Ga$_2$O$_3$}{beta-Ga2O3}

Kai Xu, Shuo Zhao, Luca Sung-Min Choi, Nils Bernhardt, Pouria Emtenani, Felix Nippert, Moritz Meißner, Hans Tornatzky, Oliver Bierwagen, Piero Mazzolini, Riccardo Mincigrucci, Laura Foglia, Danny Fainozzi, Filippo Bencivenga, Zbigniew Galazka, George Fytas, Bartlomiej Graczykowski, Riccardo Rurali, Matthias Scheffler, Christian Carbogno, Juan Sebastian Reparaz, Markus R. Wagner

arXiv:2610.31976·cond-mat.mtrl-sci·Published 2026-10-22

Monoclinic $β$-Ga$_2$O$_3$ is a promising ultra-wide-bandgap semiconductor for power electronics, but its low and anisotropic thermal conductivity limits heat removal, making it a compelling model system for nanoscale thermal transport studies. Here, we demonstrate a pronounced nanoscale enhancement of thermal anisotropy and uncover the underlying phonon mechanism by combining complementary thermal and acoustic spectroscopy techniques with first-principles calculations. Anisotropic thermoreflectance thermometry determines the full thermal-conductivity tensor from angular measurements within the (100), (010), and (001) crystallographic planes. Angle-resolved Brillouin light scattering reveals that low-frequency acoustic phonons exhibit only weak velocity anisotropy, insufficient to account for the measured thermal anisotropy. Frequency-resolved ab initio Green--Kubo calculations show that the bulk anisotropy pattern emerges only when the accumulated conductivity includes phonon modes above 4 THz, whereas the sub 3 THz acoustic contributions are nearly isotropic. Extreme-ultraviolet transient thermal grating spectroscopy reveals that reducing the grating period to 28 nm strongly enhances the in-plane thermal anisotropy, most markedly in the (001) plane, where it reaches nearly twice its macroscopic value. Reconstructed and calculated mean-free-path (MFP) accumulation functions link this enhancement to the stronger directional contrast of short-MFP phonons. Together, these results identify high-frequency, short-MFP phonons as the dominant origin of thermal anisotropy in $β$-Ga$_2$O$_3$, while low-frequency phonons attenuate the directional contrast at macroscopic scales.
