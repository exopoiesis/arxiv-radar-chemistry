Chemical Programming of Stacking Order and Superconductivity Dimensionality in NbSe2-xTex Polytypic Heterostructures

Xiangqi Liu, Haonan Wang, Ziguang Liu, Peihan Sun, Xiang Xu, Jing Jiang, Zhengyang Li, Ziyi Zhu, Wei Xia, Xia Wang, Leiming Chen, Ke Qu, Yulin Chen, Cheng Chen, Kai Liu, Zhenzhong Yang, Yanfeng Guo

arXiv:2611.27220·cond-mat.supr-con·Published 2026-11-20

Layered transition-metal dichalcogenides exhibit electronic states highly sensitive to lattice symmetry, yet elucidating the structure-superconductivity relationship has been challenging due to the lack of a continuous structural evolution within a single material system. Here, we establish NbSe2-xTex as a model system, where Te substitution drives a sequential polytypic transition across the full composition range-from the low-Te 2H phase, through mixed 1H/1T heterostructured 4H and 6R polytypes at intermediate substitution, to ideal 1T and distorted 1T' structures at high Te content. Combining comprehensive characterizations, we map both the structural evolution and the accompanying changes in spectral weight and orbital character near the Fermi level. Electrical transport measurements reveal a striking phase-dependent superconducting dimensionality: the 4H phase (NbSe1.4Te0.6) exhibits enhanced anisotropy and an anomalous boost in the in-plane upper critical field, while the 6R phase (NbSe1.2Te0.8)shows suppressed Tc (~ 2.8 K) and three-dimensional-like behavior. The combined electronic-structure and superconducting results connect the stacking sequence to effective interlayer coherence and the local spin-orbit environment. SOC band calculations resolve the distinct symmetry constraints on the 4H and 6R electronic states. These mechanisms fundamentally differ from conventional approaches by leveraging intrinsic, composition-driven polytypism to modify interlayer coupling and band structure. Thus, by establishing a definitive phase diagram that directly correlates polytypism with superconducting dimensionality, our work provides a clear roadmap for the rational design of low-dimensional superconducting devices through stacking-sequence engineering.
