Atomically abrupt sp-sp$^2$ all-carbon heterojunction preserving the electronic identity of constituent allotropes

Alice Cartoceti, Masoumeh Alihosseini, Gianni Conti, Eliecer Pelaez-Sifonte, Alessio Orbelli Biroli, Francesco Sedona, Paolo D'Agosta, Francesco Tumino, Andrea Li Bassi, Jorge Lobo-Checa, Carlo S. Casari, Simona Achilli

arXiv:2611.10272·cond-mat.mtrl-sci·Published 2026-11-06

Covalently integrating distinct carbon allotropes with atomic precision remains, to date, elusive, despite the clear advantage of combining complementary functionalities while preserving their electronic identities. Here, we realize an all-carbon lateral heterojunction combining ($sp^2$) armchair graphene nanoribbons (GNRs) and $sp$-$sp^2$ hydrogenated graphdiyne (hGDY) on Au(111) through Fe-mediated hierarchical on-surface synthesis. Low-temperature scanning tunnelling microscopy and spectroscopy combined with density-functional theory reveal an atomically abrupt (<0.5 nm), asymmetric type-I interface. Redistribution of an edge-localized GNR state onto the first hGDY unit demonstrates local electronic continuity, whereas their characteristic electronic structures re-emerge immediately beyond the interface. Calculations further indicate that heterojunction formation weakens the interaction of both constituents with the underlying Au(111) substrate, widening their gaps and reducing adsorption-induced interfacial polarization. These results establish lateral covalent integration as a route to engineer atomically defined electronic interfaces and substrate coupling in all-carbon nanostructures.
