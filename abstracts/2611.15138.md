# High-Throughput Surface Screening Reveals Energetically Competitive Low-Symmetry Facets in Wurtzite Semiconductors

**Authors:** Gustavo Madrigal, Cibrán López, Jordi Llorca, Claudio Cazorla

Surfaces and interfaces govern the performance of wurtzite semiconductors in electronics, sensing, and solar-fuel conversion. To date, first-principles studies have focused mostly on high-symmetry low-index facets, owing to the computational cost and complexity of modelling low-symmetry high-index surfaces. In practice, however, surface formation energy is determined by the planar atomic density and the strength of the bonds severed upon cleavage, neither of which inherently excludes high-Miller-index surfaces from being energetically competitive. Here, we combine automated surface generation, machine-learning interatomic potential screening, and density functional theory to explore 226 clean, stoichiometric surface models of wurtzite GaN, LaN, and ZnO, spanning all Miller orientations with $|h|,|k|,|l| \le 3$ and their inequivalent terminations. We find that low-symmetry high-index prismatic facets are energetically competitive with respect to the conventional $m$- and $a$-planes: nine of the fifteen lowest-energy nonpolar surfaces are high-index, and the five best candidates of each compound lie within only 2.5-5.3% of the minimum surface energy. Hybrid-functional calculations reveal, however, that this energetic proximity does not translate into similar electronic alignment. In GaN, the two most stable surfaces differ by just 0.08 J m$^{-2}$ in formation energy but by almost 0.8 eV in vacuum-referenced band-edge positions, whereas in LaN the nearly degenerate $m$- and $a$-planes are separated by 0.16 eV. Wurtzite facets with similar formation energies may therefore exhibit very different ionization potentials and electron affinities. Our results enable the joint, systematic assessment of stability and band alignment across the facets that can actually occur in wurtzites, and provide a map of candidate surfaces for the rational design of related nanostructure and heterointerfaces.
