Design and Evaluation of Epira: An Automated Interview System for Case Investigation and Contact Tracing

Ishita Asnani, Shaghayegh Fazliani, Zachary Ryan, Kriyoshig Prasai, Chirag Kumar, Indra Gonzalez Ojeda, Marc Lipsitch, Madeleine Udell

arXiv:2611.25577·cs.LG·Published 2026-11-19

Objective: Case investigation and contact tracing are labor-intensive. Web forms struggle when respondents need clarification before disclosing contacts, and open-ended chatbots may deviate from protocols. We developed and evaluated Epira, a protocol-controlled large language model (LLM) interviewer for multi-disease case investigation and contact tracing. Materials and Methods: Epira pairs LLM dialogue with a rule-based controller enforcing required questions, safety handoffs, and a structured case record, and adapts to new diseases by questionnaire upload. Local (Qwen2.5) and cloud (GPT-5.4) backends were tested. Study 1 used 108 simulated cases with ground-truth fields and contacts for measles and gonorrhea under Epira and open-ended conditions (864 sessions). Study 2 enrolled 72 volunteers in a 2$\times$2 disease-by-model interview assessing completion, usability, acceptability, and trust. Results: Across Study 1, Epira with a local LLM captured 88.9% of required fields and 60.1% of ground-truth contacts. Open-ended interviewers recovered 31.1-41.9 percentage points fewer required fields than the same models in Epira. In Study 2, completion was 100%; mean adapted System Usability Scale (SUS) was 82.95 $\pm$ 16.17 on a 0-100 scale and mean trust was 3.85 $\pm$ 0.77 on a 1-5 scale. Discussion: Protocol control drove completeness more than model choice. Local and cloud deployments produced similar records, supporting privacy-preserving local use with human review of routed interviews and missed contacts. Conclusion: Epira recovers required fields and contacts under diverse disclosure conditions, works with local and cloud LLMs, and received favorable usability and trust ratings.
