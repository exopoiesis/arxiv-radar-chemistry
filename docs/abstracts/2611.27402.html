Quantum computing for gearing down complex many-fermion problems

Weijie Du, Filippo Fornetti, James P. Vary

arXiv:2611.27402·quant-ph·Published 2026-11-20

Quantum computing unlocks exciting opportunities to extend first-principles calculations of quantum many-body systems beyond classical computing limits. Despite advances in algorithms and proof-of-principle experiments, large-scale {\it ab initio} calculations on noisy intermediate-scale quantum (NISQ) devices remain challenging. We introduce a quantum-classical framework that identifies compact single-particle bases to bring otherwise intractable many-fermion problems within classical reach while preserving an accurate ground-state description. We develop an efficient adaptive search scheme driven by simulated annealing that jointly optimizes natural-orbital-guided transformations and active-space selections. A quantum-assisted ground-state estimator provides search guidance using low-depth circuits and relaxed accuracy requirements, making the framework promising for NISQ devices. We demonstrate the framework's applicability for the nuclear structure of the oxygen isotopes. Our framework opens a new route to tackling complex {\it ab initio} many-fermion problems across disciplines through quantum-assisted basis reduction.
