A Density Functional Tight Binding Model for Rapid Screening of Spin-Orbit-Coupling-Driven Topological Properties in Bismuth

Nir Goldman, Ben Hourahine

arXiv:2611.21244·cond-mat.mtrl-sci·Published 2026-11-16

We present a transferable Density Functional Tight Binding (DFTB) model for bismuth with explicit spin-orbit coupling that enables exploration of the structural degrees of freedom governing topological properties with minimal computational resources. We have used our DFTB model to run high-throughput calculations at a scale that would be significantly more expensive with standard Kohn-Sham Density Functional Theory (DFT) to determine a strain-buckling map of electronic and topological properties for bismuthene, where we identify regions of band gap closure and band inversion as a function of structural parameters. We show that our approach yields an accurate band structure for the rhombohedral phase, where small band gaps exist at or near time-reversal-invariant momentum (TRIM) points. We then demonstrate the transferability of our model to bismuth properties not included in our training data through determination of the rhombohedral phase phonon density of states and energetic ordering of additional bismuth solid phases. Finally, we have validated our topological predictions through comparison to DFT results for the optimized bilayer geometries, a band inversion order parameter, and the $\mathbb{Z}_2$ invariant as a function of monolayer unit cell length. Our efforts establish DFTB as a rigorous approach for topological characterization while yielding approximately three orders of magnitude increase in computational efficiency relative to DFT. Our model can thus serve as a computational tool for topological materials discovery by enabling large-scale computational screening to address the potentially complex relationship between physical-chemical properties and the resulting topological classification.
