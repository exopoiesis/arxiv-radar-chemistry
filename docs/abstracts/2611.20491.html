Rashba Effect and Type-III Broken-Gap Band Alignment in MoTe$_2$/HfS$_2$ van der Waals Heterostructures for Tunnel Field-Effect Transistors

Daniel S. Lopes, Bruno P. e Silva, Diego R. da Costa, Alexandre C. Dias, Rodrigo G. Amorim, Teldo A. S. Pereira, Jeanlex S. de Sousa

arXiv:2611.20491·cond-mat.mtrl-sci·Published 2026-11-16

Two-dimensional van der Waals heterostructures (vdWHs) based on transition metal dichalcogenides (TMDs) have emerged as promising platforms for next-generation low-power electronics and spintronics. Here, we investigate the structural, electronic, and spin properties of MoTe$_2$/HfS$_2$ vdWHs using first-principles density functional theory calculations. Our results reveal an intrinsic type-III (broken-gap) band alignment, making this heterostructure highly attractive for tunnel field-effect transistor (TFET) applications. In addition, inversion-symmetry breaking at the interface, combined with strong spin-orbit coupling, induces a pronounced Rashba spin splitting around the $Γ$ point, accompanied by characteristic helical spin textures. We further show that external vertical electric fields, twist engineering, and both out-of-plane and in-plane strain provide efficient means to tune the band alignment, charge transfer, and Rashba parameters. In particular, electric fields and mechanical strain can drive reversible transitions between type-II and type-III band alignments while simultaneously modifying the Rashba spin texture and the band-to-band tunneling (BTBT) window. The twisted heterostructure with $θ= 21.79^\circ$ exhibits similar tunability, including strain-induced transitions between distinct band-alignment regimes. These findings establish MoTe$_2$/HfS$_2$ vdWHs as versatile multifunctional platforms combining electrically tunable Rashba spin splitting with controllable type-II/type-III band alignment, offering promising opportunities for next-generation TFETs and spintronic devices.
