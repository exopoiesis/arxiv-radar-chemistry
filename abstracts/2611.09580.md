# Hostages

**Authors:** Ernesto Mordecki

Our house has been taken over. It is not completely destroyed, but many of its essential functions and facilities no longer work as they used to. Essentially, they are out of our control. Artificial intelligence, through its large language models (LLMs), has taken it. And we, the inhabitants of the house, without really noticing what was happening, and surprised by the effectiveness of LLMs, have handed over our most sacred belongings: the capacity to think about a problem, the joy of having an idea that solves it, the beautiful effort needed to consolidate an idea in our common language. And there is no turning back.   We are mathematicians, and our whole world is in check. We wonder: is it checkmate? Can we take back control of our house, which will undoubtedly not work in the same way?   Paradoxically, the weapons that attacked us are built on some of our own ideas. Of course, it is not entirely our work. The computer science knowledge needed to develop these large models evolved in both its theoretical and its material aspects. As technology evolved (infinitely small processors, infinitely large computing centers), turning quantity into quality, computer scientists encoded into software the ideas needed to produce these tools (let us not call them weapons).   These notes look into the past and the future of the mathematical profession. What will inevitably happen? What will remain and what will disappear? And, in consequence, what is our line of action? To organize the ideas, after some introductory historical comments, we try to answer the questions: (1) Is mathematics necessary for the development of our society? (2) Will mathematicians be necessary in the near future? (3) How the profession will change, through a list of exercises: open questions and possible actions.
