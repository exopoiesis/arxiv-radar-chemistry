Chemistry by Design (CbyD): A Machine-Readable Dataset of Expert-Curated Total Synthesis Routes

Ryan E. Williams, Suong B. A. Tran, Hayden M. Leatherwood, Jihye Roh, Aquila Wolff, John G. Federice, Nicholas R. Lauta, Kevin Yu, Steven J. Nick, Zlatko Jončev, Philippe Schwaller, Connor W. Coley, Jon T. Njardarson

arXiv:2611.24996·q-bio.OT·Published 2026-11-18

Chemistry by Design (CbyD) is an open-access educational database of published total syntheses launched in 2011. It was designed to help students and practitioners learn organic reactions and synthetic strategies by depicting every step of published natural product and drug syntheses. Motivated by advances in machine learning for organic synthesis and by requests from academic and industrial researchers, we present a machine-readable version of CbyD for use in cheminformatics and computer-aided synthesis planning (CASP). The released dataset (v2026.11) contains 3,502 routes comprising 56,640 reactions toward 2,902 unique targets. Compared with patent-derived datasets, CbyD reactions are more complex, more frequently change stereochemistry, and are less likely to be covered by patent-derived reaction templates, and CbyD routes are longer and more convergent than those in commonly used CASP benchmarks. We also analyze how molecular complexity changes along these routes and identify transformations associated with large complexity increases. The CbyD dataset will be expanded through versioned public releases that incorporate new routes and community-contributed corrections.
