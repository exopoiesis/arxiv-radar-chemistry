Double-Randomized Quantum Singular Value Transformations For Early Fault-Tolerant Quantum Computers

Joel Bierman, Pawan Poudel, Yuan Liu

arXiv:2611.18881·quant-ph·Published 2026-11-13

The Quantum Singular Value Transformation (QSVT) is an algorithmic framework which encompasses a wide range of problems, including Hamiltonian dynamics, ground state preparation, Gibbs state preparation, amplitude amplification, and matrix inversion. For many areas of scientific interest such as quantum chemistry and condensed matter, the circuit depth of the block-encoding subroutine in QSVT can be formidable, limiting the scope of problems to which QSVT can be applied on early fault-tolerant hardware. We split linear operators $A$ into a core operator $A_0$ and a tail operator $A_ε$, block-encoding the normalized sum of $A_0$ and the average of $n_T$ randomly sampled terms from $A_ε$. We show that using randomized block-encodings of this form allows one to lower the circuit depth of QSVT while incurring a diamond norm error that scales as $\mathcal{O}(C_Pδ^2/n_T$), where $C_P$ is a prefactor which depends on the QSVT polynomial $P$ and $δ$ is the ratio of the $\ell_1$ norms of the coefficient vectors of $A_ε$ and $A$. We then go on to show that if one samples QSVT circuits from a joint probability distribution of both random block-encodings and random polynomials (Double-Randomized QSVT), then the circuit depth savings of these two randomization techniques combine multiplicatively, with only additive error contributions. We numerically calculate error bounds for long-range Heisenberg and iron-sulfur complex Hamiltonians, showing that for a diamond norm error bound of $10^{-3}$, RBE-QSVT can reduce the number of Pauli terms needed for the block-encoding by an order of magnitude or more in several cases. This paves the way for demonstrations of QSVT on early fault-tolerant hardware.
