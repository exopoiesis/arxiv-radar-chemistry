# High-Resolution Quantitative MRI Using Contrast-Domain Multiscale Energy (MuSE) Regularization

**Authors:** Rushdi Rusho, Jyothi Rikhab Chand, Steve R. Kecskemeti, James H. Holmes, Mathews Jacob

Quantitative MRI methods can offer tissue parameter maps (e.g. T1) that are comparable across scanners, sessions and subjects, together with multiple contrast-weighted images from a single scan. However, most data are acquired at 1 mm, which limits their use in neurodegenerative diseases that affect fine brain structures. We introduce, to our knowledge, the first diffusion-model-based reconstruction for quantitative MRI at 0.5 mm isotropic resolution from scans of less than 5 minutes, which has eightfold smaller voxel volume than clinical protocols. The key idea is to apply the prior to the synthesized contrast images rather than to the subspace coefficients. The prior is then independent of the temporal basis and the sequence, which improves generalization and allows a 2-D multiscale energy (MuSE) model trained on grayscale CelebA face images to be used without any MRI training data. The explicit energy makes reconstruction MAP estimation by a step-size-free majorize-minimize algorithm that regularizes a rotating subset of contrasts along different axes, which makes 3-D multi-contrast reconstruction tractable. For 3-D radial MPnRAGE at 7 T in 4.3 min, MuSE reduced the phantom NRMSE from 0.133 (CG-SENSE) and 0.091 (L1-wavelet) to 0.074 and the WM T1 interquartile range from 117 and 47 ms to 38 ms, and it was demonstrated in vivo at 7 T.
