Intermolecular Packing and Cooperative Stabilization in Nucleotide Monophosphate Crystals

Parimala Devi Duraisamy, Raghunathan Ramakrishnan

arXiv:2610.26068·physics.chem-ph·Published 2026-10-19

We examine the known polymorphs of cytidine 5$^\prime$-monophosphate (CMP) using dispersion-corrected density functional theory. The monoclinic phase is predicted to be the most stable, lying 1.76 and 3.00 kcal/mol per molecular unit below the orthorhombic and triclinic forms, respectively. Monoclinic CMP contains a ribose-based H-bonding motif along the crystallographic $\vec{b}$ direction, comprising alternating intra- and intermolecular interactions, motivating an investigation of cooperative stabilization along this pathway. We employ chain-of-molecules (COM) and chain-of-slabs (COS) models to quantify the direction- and size-dependent stabilization and extend the analysis to the corresponding 5$^\prime$-monophosphates of thymidine, uridine, adenosine, and guanosine. Successive dimer-addition energies reveal the strongest size-dependent cooperative stabilization along the $\vec{b}$ direction, whereas growth along $\vec{a}$ and $\vec{c}$ exhibits substantially weaker size dependence. Among the nucleotides examined, adenosine 5$^\prime$-monophosphate shows the strongest chain-growth stabilization in the COM model, whereas the uridine variant shows the strongest stabilization in the COS model. Guanosine 5$^\prime$-monophosphate exhibits the weakest response in both models. Minimal-basis iterative stockholder (MBIS) analysis further supports molecular polarization along the growing assemblies. These results demonstrate how nucleobase identity and the surrounding crystal environment modulate direction-dependent cooperative stabilization associated with extended hydrogen-bond networks.
