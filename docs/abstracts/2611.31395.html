Origin of quadrupolar order in the 5d$^1$ double perovskite Ba$_2$MgReO$_6$

Dario Fiore Mosca, Leonid V. Pourovskii

arXiv:2611.31395·cond-mat.str-el·Published 2026-11-23

Despite extensive experimental and theoretical studies of quadrupolar order in Ba$_2$MgReO$_6$, the microscopic origin and relative energy scales of the interactions driving it remain unresolved. In particular, the observed ferroic ordering of the $z^2$ quadrupole has remained difficult to explain, as the relative contributions of superexchange, classical quadrupole-quadrupole interactions, vibronic couplings, and virtual excitations to higher-energy levels have not been established quantitatively. Here, we determine all these interactions from first principles and solve the resulting microscopic Hamiltonian. The calculated ground state reproduces the experimentally observed quadrupolar order parameters and the associated structural distortions with quantitative accuracy. Disentangling the individual contributions, we find that ferroic $z^2$ order originates from a linear-quartic coupling, generated by virtual excitations to the $J_{\mathrm{eff}}$= 1/2 doublet and driven by vibronic interactions. Our results establish the hierarchy of microscopic interactions underlying quadrupolar order in Ba$_2$MgReO$_6$ and provide a unified $ab \ initio$ description of its coupled electronic and lattice degrees of freedom.
