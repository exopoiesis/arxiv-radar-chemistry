An infrared divacancy color center in bulk hexagonal diamond for quantum communication

Xuanxuan Zhang, Chen-Min Dai, Cong Li, Chunlan Ma, Zenghua Cai

arXiv:2611.13736·cond-mat.mtrl-sci·Published 2026-11-10

Intrinsic color centers are investigated in bulk hexagonal diamond as potential emitters for fiber-based quantum communication. Hybrid density functional theory and constrained-occupation delta SCF calculations identify five carbon-vacancy and divacancy configurations with stable optical excited states. A comparison of their radiative and nonradiative properties within a three-point configuration-coordinate model identifies the positively charged axial divacancy, VVa1+, as the most promising candidate. It exhibits the highest calculated zero-phonon-line (ZPL) emission rate of 43.9 MHz and ZPL quantum efficiency of 11.8% among the five candidates. Its weak structural relaxation yields a Huang-Rhys factor of 0.395, while a multimode photoluminescence calculation gives a Debye-Waller factor of 69.37%, indicating a dominant zero-phonon contribution. The predicted ZPL at 0.603 eV (2057.3 nm) also has the smallest photon-energy separation from the 1310 and 1550 nm telecom targets among the candidates. These results identify VVa1+ as a promising intrinsic color center for fiber-based quantum communication, with quantum frequency upconversion offering a route from its strong infrared ZPL to the telecom O and C bands.
