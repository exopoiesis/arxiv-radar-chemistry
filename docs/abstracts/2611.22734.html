Catalytic imaginarity transformations

Ying-Hui Yang, Chen-Xi Wang, Xiao-Li Wang, Shao-Ming Fei

arXiv:2611.22734·quant-ph·Published 2026-11-17

State conversion is a central problem in quantum resource theories. In this work, we investigate catalytic state transformations within the resource theory of imaginarity. Catalytic imaginarity transformations enable otherwise impossible state transformations using only real operations, assisted by an auxiliary system with imaginarity that is not consumed. For deterministic catalytic conversion, we show that a maximally imaginary pure state is the unique catalyst. This result contrasts with the resource theories of entanglement and coherence, where maximally entangled and maximally coherent catalysts cannot enable such deterministic conversions under their respective free operations. For stochastic-approximate catalytic conversion, if we do not require catalyst recovery upon failure, any pure state with imaginarity can serve as a catalyst, and we derive the optimal success probability. If the catalyst is required to be recovered exactly regardless of whether the catalytic state transformation succeeds or fails, the optimal success probability coincides with that without a catalyst, and thus no advantage arises from catalysis. If exact catalyst recovery upon failure is relaxed, we find a tradeoff relation between the success probability of a single conversion and the catalyst's residual imaginarity after transformation. When the initial state is re-prepared and the catalyst's residual imaginarity can be reused after failure, the success probability can be further improved over $N$ ($N \geq 2$) rounds of catalytic conversion, and we obtain the corresponding maximum overall success probability.
