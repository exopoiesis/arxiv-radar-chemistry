Thermal investigation of buffer and transition layers in AlGaN HEMT stacks

Simon Fernbach, Lisa Mitterhuber, Egbert Zojer, Natalia Bedoya-Martínez

arXiv:2611.25928·cond-mat.mtrl-sci·Published 2026-11-19

In this work, we investigate thermal transport in a set of thin-film layers used in modern HEMT designs. Our theoretical analysis employs three phonon-transport models of increasing sophistication, ranging from a relatively simple Debye-approximation model to a first-principles framework that incorporates Density Functional Theory (DFT)-derived harmonic and anharmonic phonon properties. To describe ternary semiconductors, alloy scattering is also considered, and to account for the properties of non-ideal, polycrystalline thin films, scattering at grain and film boundaries is taken into account. For modeling the specific samples at hand, the latter are fitted to thermal conductivity data measured with Time-Domain Thermoreflectance (TDTR). Overall, we find that the first-principles model performs best and produces the most physically consistent results. Special emphasis is placed on establishing a reliable procedure for determining intrinsic transport properties dominated by Umklapp scattering. Building on these, it is then possible to systematically add additional scattering terms to the models and assess how model-inherent factors, such as the treatment of scattering rates or the description of the phonon dispersion, affect model accuracy and overall predictive capabilities.
