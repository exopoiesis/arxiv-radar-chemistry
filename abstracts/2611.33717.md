# Crystal-Symmetry-Dependent Field-Free Superconducting Diode Effect in CoPt/Nb Heterostructures

**Authors:** He Yu, Jiaxin Chen, Yi Zhang, Gan Zhao, Yizhen Shao, Jiaxu Li, Yu He, Qiusha Wang, Yuanliang Zhu, Bin Han, Junda Qu, Haochang Lyu, Bowen Dong, Jie Qi, Dapeng Zhu, Daoqian Zhu, Yunchi Zhao, Liang Liu, Shouguo Wang, Weisheng Zhao, Guang Yang

Superconducting diodes convert nonreciprocal critical currents into current-polarity-selective switching between zero-resistance and resistive states. However, realizing the superconducting diode effect often requires complex device architectures and an external magnetic field to break spatial-inversion and time-reversal symmetry, substantially limiting its integration into cryogenic electronics and superconducting quantum circuits. Here, we demonstrate precise control of the field-free superconducting diode effect in epitaxial [111]-oriented A1-CoPt/Nb heterostructures. The critical-current asymmetry obeys the magnetic-point-group symmetry relation: it is finite along low-symmetry directions, suppressed along magnetic mirror axes, and reverses under either a 60° in-plane rotation or reversal of the CoPt magnetization. The same threefold symmetry dependence is observed in field-free current-induced magnetization switching in A1-CoPt films, indicating a common crystallographic origin for the superconducting and normal-state transport responses. Current- and temperature-dependent second-harmonic transport measurements are consistent with vortex-mediated nonreciprocity arising from an orientation-dependent interfacial edge landscape. We further exploit the magnetization-dependent diode polarity for proof-of-concept superconducting readout of electrically written magnetic states. These results establish crystallographic symmetry as an effective degree of freedom for engineering the superconducting diode effect, expanding the accessible materials space and providing a practical route toward field-free superconducting electronics.
