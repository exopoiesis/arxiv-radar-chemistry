Binary-induced mass increase and helium enrichment of the envelopes of Type~IIP supernova progenitors

Caroline Mannes, Norbert Langer, Andrea Ercolino

arXiv:2607.25849·astro-ph.SR·Published 2026-07-28

Binary interactions are expected to play a major role in shaping the progenitor population of core-collapse supernovae, particularly in the era of deep, high-cadence transient surveys. Using ComBinE, a rapid binary population synthesis code combining tabulated stellar structure models with physically motivated mass-transfer stability criteria, we find that ~40% of Type II supernova progenitors at Milky Way metallicity originate from mergers between a post-main-sequence star and a main-sequence companion following unstable mass transfer. Compared to single-star progenitors with the same helium-core mass, these merger products have more massive hydrogen-rich envelopes and broader envelope-mass distributions. Their envelopes are helium-enriched, with relative enhancements of up to 80% and helium mass fractions reaching 0.4. These differences are expected to affect Type IIP light curves and should be considered in future progenitor and explosion models. Constraining the merger-induced Type IIP fraction with facilities such as LSST, Roman, and the ELT will provide insights into binary evolution physics.
