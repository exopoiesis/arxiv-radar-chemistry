GenerativeQC: A Generative, GPU-Accelerated Quantum Chemistry Program

Shengrui Xu, Longhan Shen, Wenxi Zhai, Tong Zhu, Jinzhe Zeng

arXiv:2611.24744·physics.chem-ph·Published 2026-11-18·Updated 2026-11-20

GenerativeQC is an open-source, GPU-accelerated quantum chemistry program that computes energies and analytic nuclear forces from Hartree-Fock (HF) and density functional theory (DFT) to closed-shell density-fitted CCSD(T) and an MP2-assisted CCSD(T)/complete-basis-set (CBS) composite. Its scientific compiler translates symbolic equations into C++ and CUDA code and uses automatic differentiation to generate the derivative and response programs required for analytic forces. A native runtime executes these programs together with hand-written iterative solvers and integral routines. Across Direct and density-fitted HF and DFT benchmarks for eight methods on water clusters of up to 96 atoms, GenerativeQC is faster than GPU4PySCF in every tested case on both RTX 5090 and H100 GPUs. For correlated wave functions, compiler-generated derivative contractions and transpose Jacobian operators enable complete density-fitted CCSD(T) and CCSD(T)/CBS analytic forces on GPUs; a CCSD(T)/CBS energy-and-force calculation for heptane (23 atoms) takes 4.5 min on a single H100 GPU and 17 min on an RTX 5090.
