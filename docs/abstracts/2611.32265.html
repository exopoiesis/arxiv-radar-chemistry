First-principles phase diagram of monolayer CrS$_2$ polymorphs under biaxial strain and its sensitivity to the Hubbard U parameter

Rashmi Ranjan Routaray, Ali Ghojavand, Eric Bousquet

arXiv:2611.32265·physics.comp-ph·Published 2026-11-24

In correlated two-dimensional magnets the balance between competing electronic and magnetic phases is often set by the strength of the on-site Coulomb interaction, so that the predicted ground state can hinge on a parameter that is not uniquely defined. Monolayer CrS$_2$ lies squarely in this regime, hosting several energetically close polymorphs whose character ranges from non-magnetic semiconducting to magnetically ordered metallic. We therefore investigate, using DFT$+U$ within the generalized gradient approximation, the structural, magnetic, and electronic properties of the 1H, 1T, 1T$'$, and 1T$''$ polymorphs of monolayer CrS$_2$ under varying Hubbard interactions ($U=0$--$8$~eV) and biaxial strain ($-10\%$ to $+10\%$). The 1T-derived structures remain metallic and magnetic throughout, with $U$ acting mainly as a spectral renormalization. The 1H phase behaves differently, passing from a non-magnetic semiconductor through an antiferromagnetic semiconducting regime before metallizing, so that the gap closes as $U$ increases while the local Cr moment grows monotonically. Biaxial tensile strain at fixed $U$ induces a similar sequence of transitions. Along both tuning pathways, the two inequivalent nearest-neighbor Cr--Cr exchange channels progressively separate and cross, with the parallel-spin channel becoming dominant, accompanied by a breathing-type bond disproportionation. Independent calculations with a localized-orbital implementation reproduce the overall evolution but yield shifted critical values of $U$ and of strain, demonstrating the robustness of the phase diagram while highlighting the implementation dependence of the transition thresholds.
