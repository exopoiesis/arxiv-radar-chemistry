Metallic or insulating magnetic phases of transition-metal oxides in light of the Slater vs Mott controversy

Xiuwen Zhang, Jia-Xin Xiong, Alex Zunger

arXiv:2611.19888·cond-mat.mtrl-sci·Published 2026-11-15

Magnetic d-electron oxides usually have a long-range-ordered ground state and an above-ground-state disordered para phase. The historical Slater-Mott controversy still reverberating to date pertains to understanding the origin of the insulating vs metallic nature of these two phases. Slater suggested that the insulating character evolved from the long-range order (LRO) antiferromagnetic (AFM) cell-doubling, leading to energy band repulsion and thus to forming a band gap. The reliance on LRO to explain gapping suggests that the absence of LRO in the spin-disordered para-phase would deprive the system from band repulsion, thus predicting a metallic paramagnetic (PM) phase. This, however, conflicts with numerous current observations. The alternative Mott-Peierls-Hubbard view abandons the mean-field-like band structure view emphasizing instead the local interelectronic repulsion U as a strong correlation signature of gapping both at the presence or absence of LRO. However, U is not an easily measured or calculated quantity in periodic systems, a situation often leading in practice to treating U as an adjustable parameter. The current work uses a parameter-free mean-field-like approach (Density Functional Theory, DFT), allowing energy-lowering symmetry breaking. This is done by considering local magnetic motifs as building blocks of the macroscopic AFM or PM phases. It is found that even when such motifs are isolated electronically from each other (i.e. vanishing LRO) by embedding them in quantum barriers (such as magnetic NiO6 octahedra surrounded locally by wide-gap MgO) they exhibit an intrinsic band gap. Thus, the origin of the band gaps in AFM or PM phases need not be LRO or strong correlation and can be understood within the mean-field-like band structure approach with symmetry breaking.
