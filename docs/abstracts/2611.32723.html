Mixing and Preserving Properties of Non-van der Waals 2D Materials in Sandwich-like Heterostructures

Anastasiia Nihei, Rico Friedrich

arXiv:2611.32723·cond-mat.mtrl-sci·Published 2026-11-24

Heterostructure interfaces created by stacking two-dimensional (2D) materials outline an appealing route towards achieving advanced electronic and magnetic functionalities at the nanoscale. Non-van der Waals (non-vdW) sandwich-like heterostructures (HSs) are build out of non-vdW 2D materials derived from non-layered crystals and establish a new frontier enabling strong interfacial coupling. Employing ab initio density functional theory (DFT), we investigate two classes of such trilayer HSs: purely non-vdW assemblies and hybrid vdW/non-vdW/vdW stacks. We show that non-vdW sandwiches undergo pronounced geometric reconstruction forming chemically bonded interfaces with large absolute binding energies up to an order of magnitude larger than those of conventional vdW HSs. These strong interactions drive extensive electronic hybridization, band gap renormalization, and tunable interlayer magnetic coupling. In contrast, vdW encapsulation largely protects the intrinsic atomic and electronic structure of the non-vdW sheet from the environment , while enabling charge transfer doping of the vdW layers. Our results thus establish sandwich HSs a versatile interface design platform, spanning interaction regimes from weakly perturbed encapsulation to strongly interacting chemically bonded systems with emergent electronic and magnetic behavior.
