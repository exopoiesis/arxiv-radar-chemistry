What Counts as Evidence? Epistemic Cue Utilization in Large Language Models

Jinchang Zhang, Kaijun Jiang, Guoyu Lu

arXiv:2610.12788·cs.CY·Published 2026-10-08

Which information actually shapes a language model's judgment when sources disagree? We study epistemic cue utilization through a controlled benchmark of 500 scenarios and five complementary tracks separating cue identification, cue influence, and competition between source, temporal, and presentation cues. Across 13 LLMs, nearly identical recognition of relative source reliability can accompany effects ranging from negligible to complete choice reversal when source histories are exchanged. The resulting profiles distinguish models that rely on source history from those more responsive to evidence lineage, event time, or presentation order. We then intervene on three Qwen checkpoints using matched sham material, fact restatement, structured evidence records, and an additional weighting principle. Restatement increases reliability sensitivity in the two 27B models, and adding the principle increases it by a further 39.2 and 47.2 percentage points relative to the ledger. The 9B model instead changes its sensitivity to evidence lineage without a comparable reliability response. Verified-state accuracy remains 100\% throughout these interventions. Together, the benchmark and interventions show that identifying an epistemic cue and allowing it to influence judgment are distinct, experimentally measurable aspects of model behavior.
