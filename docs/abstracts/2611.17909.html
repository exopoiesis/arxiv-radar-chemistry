CardioTrace: Motion-Aware Cine Synthesis via Coupled Tracing and Segmentation

Zhi Chen, Le Zhang

arXiv:2611.17909·cs.CV·Published 2026-11-13

Cardiac cine MRI provides time-resolved visualization of cardiac motion and is essential for evaluating ventricular function and anatomy. In routine clinical acquisition, however, cine sequences are frequently temporally sparse or degraded by arrhythmia, motion, and scan-time constraints, which can hinder reliable quantification and downstream analysis. We present CardioTrace, a motion-aware cine synthesis framework that generates a full cardiac cine sequence from limited input while explicitly modeling and tracing cardiac motion. CardioTrace couples two complementary pathways: a pixel-translation branch that directly predicts cine frames in a residual formulation, and a flow-field branch that estimates time-resolved deformation fields from a reference phase to provide interpretable motion correspondences across frames. To further preserve anatomical plausibility, a segmentation branch supervises clinically meaningful cardiac structures at key phases, and a cross-branch consistency objective encourages the synthesized appearance dynamics to follow the explicit motion guidance, mitigating temporal drift and unrealistic shape transitions over long sequences. The framework also supports prompt-style conditioning to incorporate auxiliary patient or acquisition information, improving robustness across heterogeneous multi-center data. Experiments on public cardiac cine MRI datasets demonstrate that CardioTrace improves perceptual quality and temporal coherence over representative GAN- and diffusion-based baselines, while better preserving ventricular morphology throughout the cardiac cycle. \textit{Our codes are available after review}.
