Oxirane Ring-Opening Dynamics Mediated By The First Singlet Excitation Using Time-Dependent \textit{GW} Molecular Dynamics

Aaditya Manjanath, Ryoji Sahara, Thi Nu Pham, Yoshiyuki Kawazoe, Kaoru Ohno

arXiv:2611.20723·physics.chem-ph·Published 2026-11-16

Oxirane (ethylene oxide, \ce{C2H4O}) is a prototypical system for investigating photoinduced bond cleavage and ring-opening dynamics. Previous time-dependent density functional theory studies identified the initial HOMO $\rightarrow$ LUMO (\ce{S1}) excitation as nonreactive and attributed ring opening to higher-energy excitations. Subsequent constrained density functional theory simulations, however, reported ring opening following an \ce{S2} $\rightarrow$ \ce{S1} transition. Here, we investigate photochemistry of oxirane using time-dependent $GW$ molecular dynamics (TD$GW$-MD), a many-body approach based on extended quasiparticle theory [\textit{J. Chem. Phys.} \textbf{160}, 184102 (2024)]. Our simulations reveal ring opening initiated by asymmetric \ce{C-O} bond cleavage following direct HOMO $\rightarrow$ LUMO (\ce{S1}) excitation. Following the selected surface hop, separate conditional trajectories on the broken-symmetry open-shell singlet and triplet potential energy surfaces lead to distinct products. The singlet trajectory yields acetaldehyde via intramolecular hydrogen transfer, while the triplet trajectory produces formaldehyde and methylene via \ce{C-C} bond fragmentation. These products are consistent with those identified in previous photolysis experiments. Our results demonstrate that direct excitation to the lowest singlet excited state can initiate ring opening within the present TD$GW$-MD framework. They further reveal distinct subsequent reaction pathways under the selected spin conditions. This study highlights the potential of TD$GW$-MD for elucidating photoinduced reaction mechanisms from a many-body perspective.
