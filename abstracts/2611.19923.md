# Bowed-String-Mechanism-Inspired Quantum Dissociation Control of Molecules

**Authors:** Julian Juhi-Lian Ting

Inspired by Guettler's mechanics of accelerating Helmholtz motion, we present a predictive laser pulse-shaping strategy to rapidly initiate the multiphoton dissociation of vibrationally excited diatomic molecules. In intense infrared fields, NO molecules often exhibit unpredictable control windows due to the chaotic fragmentation of intensity thresholds. By incorporating a bowing-analogue transient shaper enforced by a boundary-matching constraint with symmetric derivative slope transitions and vanishing initial boundary conditions ($T(0) = 0, T'(0) = 0$), we demonstrate that a pulse engineered with non-linear front-flank acceleration reduces the multi-valued threshold topology into a single, predictable linear dissociation corridor. Wigner quasiprobability distributions confirm that this temporal acceleration trajectory opens cantori bottlenecks and suppresses wave-packet fragmentation, restricting the quantum non-classicality index to $M_{\text{nc}} \le 0.15$. Our simulations verify that this trajectory engineering preserves wave-packet localization and maintains high dissociation yields ($P_{\text{diss}} > 0.95$) even under Markovian thermal dephasing ($δ/ω_0 = 10^{-4}$) and frozen rotational variations up to $J = 5$. Comparison with contemporary ultrafast spectral phase modulation protocols demonstrates that conventional smooth pulse envelopes, despite advanced phase-chirping, exhibit limitations in restricting non-hyperbolic cantori stickiness due to insufficient sub-picosecond acceleration. In contrast, our boundary-constrained shaper enforces a deterministic, single-valued dissociation corridor under equivalent few-picosecond control windows.
