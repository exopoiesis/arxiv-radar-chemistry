Hall effect in electrolyte solutions: Self-consistent Debye-Huckel-Onsager theory

Yury Budkov, Nikolai Kalikin

arXiv:2611.27392·cond-mat.stat-mech·Published 2026-11-20

A self-consistent statistical-mechanical theory of the Hall effect in electrolyte solutions is developed by extending the self-consistent Debye-Huckel-Onsager (SCDHO) framework to crossed electric and magnetic fields. The theory incorporates non-local ionic charge distributions through Slater-type form factors and ion-specific coefficients describing the effective Lorentz response, with experimental input for protons and hydroxide ions. Within the random-phase approximation, the Hall conductivity is expressed as mean-field, correlation, and electrophoretic contributions. Both interaction channels include the Lorentz rotation of the complete electric-field-induced ionic velocity. In the point-charge limit with hydrodynamics suppressed, the relaxation correction agrees with independent odd stochastic density functional theory and the relaxation part of Friedman's classical result. For 22 dilute aqueous electrolytes, the mean absolute relative deviations from measured Hall constants and Hall numbers are about 17 percent. At finite concentrations, the theory predicts a nonmonotonic Hall number for NaBPh4 and sign changes of the electrophoretic correction for NaI and HCl. Correlation effects dominate for HCl and LiOH, whereas the two interaction channels become comparable for KOH and aqueous imidazolium salts. The results clarify how ionic mobilities, effective Hall coefficients, charge distributions, and solvent hydrodynamics determine electrolyte magnetotransport.
