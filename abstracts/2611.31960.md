# Typical-query energy figures mislead institutional AI electricity demand

**Authors:** Nathan Garland, Ryan Heneghan

Institutions increasingly buy large language model services, yet we still often estimate electricity use as single Wh-per-query value. We present the Surrogate Large Language Energy Emulator (SULLEE), an open bottom-up model linking token geometry, serving hardware, and workflow structure to annual institutional electricity, with uncertainty propagated by Monte Carlo simulation. When an institution multiplies a single reported "typical" per-query energy figure by its count of visible user prompts our modelling implies that this compounds two separable shortfalls: a right-skewed task-energy distribution not represented by any single scalar, and workflow amplification of each visible prompt into additional agent and tool calls. Together these imply an underestimate of total inference electricity by a factor of approximately 3.4 under present-day observed use, rising to approximately 15 under a 2030 high-integration scenario. Using Australian public universities as a case study, we show how the top 10% of highest-energy requests contributes over half of modelled institution-level energy use. We quantify how institution-level electricity demand may increase across three AI integration scenarios of: 1) a continuation of estimated present-day 2026 usage; 2) a high-uptake present-day 2026 institution, and 3) a potential 2030 uptake scenario - based on present trends of generative AI tools and their uptake. For a representative Australian university, mean annual electricity demand for AI inference purposes is projected to increase from 17.3 MWh (with a 3.1-47.1 MWh uncertainty, within P05-P95 scenario quartile ranges) under the 2026 observed-use scenario to up to 287.4 MWh (41.5-830.7 MWh) by 2030.
